What should a Twitter pinned post say?

A Twitter pinned post should tell a new visitor who you are, what you post, why they should trust you and what to do next. This free Twitter pinned post generator turns those four parts into eight drafts for an intro, best-of thread, newsletter or launch, each under X's 280-character limit.

Your pinned post is prime space. X's help page says it is the first post people see on your profile. Yet many pins are a random post from months ago that did well once. That is a missed chance to turn a visit into a follow.

How to use the Twitter pinned post generator

  1. Pick a type: Intro, Best-of thread, Newsletter or offer, or Project launch.
  2. Add your first name and, if needed, the name of your project or newsletter.
  3. Describe who you are so it reads well after "I'm".
  4. List what you post about, separated by commas.
  5. Add one piece of proof you can back up. Skip it if you have none yet.
  6. Add a call to action and link if you want a specific one. Leave them empty for varied ideas.
  7. Press Generate again until a draft feels close, then copy it and edit it in your own voice.

The drafts are built from templates and phrase banks mixed with your words. They are starting points, not finished posts.

How does the character count work?

Each draft shows its count against X's 280-character limit for standard posts, as of October 2026. The count follows X's counting rules for posts, where links count as 23 characters and emoji and arrows count as 2.

Line breaks count too, and the drafts use them on purpose. Short paragraphs are easier to scan on a phone. If a draft goes over 280, it is marked in red. Paid subscribers can post longer text, but a pin that fits in 280 reads well for everyone.

Tips for a pinned post that converts

The best pinned posts are specific and short. These habits make a pin clearer.

  • Lead with the reader. "New here? Start with these" beats "I'm excited to share".
  • Use one real detail. A fictional example: "I've designed brands for 140 cafes since 2016." It makes the rest believable.
  • Ask for one thing. Follow, subscribe or click. Two asks split attention.
  • Refresh it. Swap your pin every few months, or when you launch something new.
  • Match your bio. The pin should expand on the bio, not repeat it word for word.

Common pinned post mistakes

Most weak pins fall into a few patterns. Each one has a quick fix.

  • Pinning an old viral post that has nothing to do with your current topic.
  • Making claims you cannot prove. Never invent numbers to sound bigger.
  • Stuffing in hashtags. X suggests no more than 2 per post.
  • Ending with no next step, so readers enjoy it and leave.
  • Dropping a bare link with no context. X's spam rules name repeatedly posting links without commentary, so always say why to click.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I pin a post on Twitter?

To pin a post on X, find the post on your profile, open its More menu (the three dots) and choose the pin option. X lets you pin one post at a time, and it is the first post visitors see. Pinning a new post replaces the old one, which stays on your timeline.

How long should a Twitter pinned post be?

A Twitter pinned post works best at about 150 to 280 characters, enough for who you are, what you post and one next step. Standard posts on X are capped at 280 characters. If you need more space, make the pin the first post of a thread and continue in the replies.

Should my pinned post be a thread?

A thread works well as a pinned post when you have several strong posts to show off. The first post acts as a menu, and replies hold the details. X's help page says threads of four or more posts are shortened on timelines with a "Show this thread" option, so make the first post count.

How often should I change my pinned post?

Change your pinned post every two to three months, or whenever your focus changes. A launch, a new newsletter or a new best thread are all good reasons to update it. An old pin about something you stopped doing confuses new visitors and wastes the first spot on your profile.

Is this pinned post generator an AI tool?

No, this pinned post generator does not use AI. It mixes your own words with templates and phrase banks written for each pin type, then shuffles them into eight drafts. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded. Always edit the result so it sounds like you.
