How do you find good hashtags for a post on X?

Good hashtags on X are short, specific and easy to read. A Twitter hashtag generator like this free tool turns your topic into CamelCase tags such as #RemoteWork, broader one-word tags, niche variants and related community tags. X recommends using no more than 2 hashtags per post, so pick the one or two that fit best.

It's tempting to add five hashtags to every post because it feels like more reach. Mostly it makes posts look like ads. One tag, sometimes two, is plenty, and only when the tag names a real conversation people follow.

How to use the Twitter Hashtag Generator

  1. Type your topic or a few keywords, separated by commas. For example: "indie game dev, pixel art".
  2. Press Generate. The tool builds four groups of ideas from your words.
  3. Tap the hashtags you like. Picked tags turn dark and appear under Your picks.
  4. Switch between #CamelCase and #lowercase to see which reads better.
  5. Paste your post into the optional box to see it with the hashtags added, plus the character count.
  6. Copy the hashtags alone, or the full post with hashtags, and search each tag on X before posting.

How many hashtags should you use on X?

Use one or two hashtags per post. X's hashtag help page says it recommends no more than 2 hashtags per post as best practice, as of October 2026, though you may use as many as you like.

The same page explains the basics. A hashtag can go anywhere in a post. Spaces and punctuation break it, so "#remote work" only tags the word "remote". On a public account, anyone searching that hashtag may find your post. That is why this tool joins words together and strips punctuation for you.

Why use CamelCase hashtags?

CamelCase hashtags capitalize the first letter of each word, so #RemoteWorkTips reads faster than #remoteworktips. It also helps screen readers say the words separately instead of guessing at one long word. Both forms link to the same hashtag search, so CamelCase costs you nothing.

Watch for accidental words. A tag that reads fine in lowercase can spell something odd when the word breaks are unclear. Capitals fix that too.

Hashtag tips and examples

Pick tags the way a reader would search, not the way a brand would label. Here is a made-up example. A fictional pixel artist posts a new character sprite. Weak choice: #art #gaming #pixel #indie #gamedev #design. Better choice: #PixelArt in the sentence, plus #GameDev at the end.

  • Put a tag inside the sentence when the word belongs there anyway: "Finished my first #PixelArt tileset."
  • Start a niche tag for a series you post every week, so people can find every part.
  • Check the tag on X first. If the recent posts don't match your topic, skip it.

Common hashtag mistakes

The biggest mistake is using hashtags that don't match the post. X's authenticity rules list excessive unrelated hashtags and hijacking trending hashtags as content spam.

  • Stacking five or more tags. It looks spammy and goes against X's own advice.
  • Using a tag you never checked. Some tags mean something different from what you expect.
  • Expecting tags to fix reach. The post itself matters far more. Read how to increase engagement without hashtags for what works instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this Twitter hashtag generator free?

Yes, the XVision Twitter Hashtag Generator is free with no sign-up and no limits. It runs entirely in your browser, so your topic and post are never uploaded. You can generate as many sets of hashtags as you want, pick the ones you like and copy them straight into X.

Does the hashtag generator show trending hashtags?

No, this hashtag generator has no trending, volume or reach data. It builds ideas from your own keywords, a list of common endings and a fixed list of well-known community tags. To see whether a tag is active right now, search it on X and look at the latest posts.

Do hashtags still help on X?

Hashtags can still help people find a post in X search, and X's help page says anyone searching a hashtag may find posts from public accounts. They are not a reach shortcut, though. X recommends no more than 2 per post, and a clear, useful post matters more than any tag.

Should hashtags go at the end or inside the post?

Hashtags work in either place, because X lets you put them anywhere in a post. A tag inside a sentence saves characters when the word belongs there anyway. Tags at the end keep the sentence clean. Pick one approach per post and keep it to one or two tags.
