      What is a Twitter profile audit?

      A Twitter profile audit checks every part of your X profile, from name and bio to photo, header and pinned post, and tells you what to fix. This free Twitter profile audit scores you out of 100 using 18 checks, then ranks the fixes so the biggest gains come first.

      Most people audit their profile by staring at it. That rarely works, because you already know who you are. A checklist makes you see it the way a stranger does, in the few seconds before they decide to follow or leave.

      How to use the Twitter profile audit

      1. Type your display name, username and bio exactly as they appear on X.
      2. Add your website and location if you have them.
      3. Tick the boxes that are honestly true. Be strict with the bio boxes.
      4. Pick how often you post and how often you reply to others.
      5. Read "Fix first". The fixes worth the most points sit at the top.
      6. Press Copy fix list, make the changes on X, then come back and run it again.

      The form starts with a made-up profile called Sam Lee so you can see how results look. Press Start blank to enter your own.

      How is the profile score calculated?

      The score adds up points from 18 checks in five groups. Some checks read your text directly, like username format and bio length. Others rely on your answers, because no tool can judge your photo or topic better than you can.

      Let me be clear about one thing. This is the tool's own checklist, not an X metric. X does not publish a profile score. The weights reflect my judgment of what visitors notice first, backed by X's help pages where possible.

      Some checks come straight from X. Usernames must be longer than 4 and up to 15 characters, and bios can be 160 characters. X's search help page says account search prefers profiles with a complete name, username and bio, which is why empty fields cost points. Sizes for the header and photo come from X's page on customizing your profile.

      What does your score mean?

      Treat the score as a to-do list, not a grade on your worth. Here is how the bands read.

      • 85 to 100: Strong profile. Small polish only.
      • 70 to 84: Good, with a few fixes that are easy wins.
      • 50 to 69: Needs work. Visitors may leave before reading your posts.
      • Under 50: Start with the basics: photo, bio and a pinned post.

      A high score does not promise followers. It removes the reasons people say no. Good posts still have to do the rest.

      Tips for quick profile fixes

      The fastest gains usually come from the bio and the pinned post, because those are what visitors read. Here is a fictional before and after.

      • Before: "Coffee. Code. Cats. #tech #startup #growth #saas". It lists facts and four hashtags but gives no reason to follow.
      • After: "Backend engineer building a budgeting app in public. Weekly posts on shipping solo, pricing and what broke." It says what you get and adds proof.

      Next, pin a post that introduces you or collects your best threads. X says the pinned post is the first post visitors see, so it works like a second bio with more room.

      Common profile mistakes the audit catches

      Most low scores come from the same handful of gaps. Each one takes minutes to fix.

      • No header, or the default blank one.
      • A username with long random numbers, like an auto-suggested handle.
      • A bio made of hashtags or job titles with no "why follow".
      • A pinned post from a year ago about something you stopped doing.
      • Posting in bursts, then going quiet for weeks.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Is the Twitter profile audit score an official X score?

      No, the Twitter profile audit score is this tool's own checklist, not an X metric. X does not publish a profile score. The checks use X's help pages where they apply, like the 160-character bio and 15-character username limits, and common profile advice for the rest, such as having a clear photo.

      How often should I audit my Twitter profile?

      Audit your Twitter profile every two to three months, and any time your work or focus changes. A new job, product or topic can make your bio and pinned post out of date. X's search help notes that profile changes can take a few days to show in search.

      Does a complete profile help me show up in X search?

      Yes, X's help page on search says account search prefers profiles with a complete name, username and bio. An empty bio or a missing name can make you harder to find. Keep your real name or brand in plain text so people can search for it easily.

      Is my profile information saved or sent anywhere?

      No, the Twitter profile audit runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded, saved on a server or linked to your X account. The tool never logs into X or reads your real profile. Close the tab and every answer is gone, so copy your fix list first.

      What should I fix first on my Twitter profile?

      Fix your profile photo, bio and pinned post first, because visitors see those within seconds. A clear photo, a bio that says what you post, and a recent pinned post often change how a profile feels more than anything else. The audit ranks fixes by points so the biggest gaps come first.
