How do you calculate average impressions on Twitter?

A Twitter impressions calculator divides your total impressions by the number of posts to get the average per post, and by the number of days to get the daily average. For example, 48,000 impressions from 60 posts is 800 per post. This free tool also estimates monthly impressions from your posting pace.

Averages turn a big, vague total into numbers you can plan with. "48,000 impressions this month" sounds nice. "800 per post at about 2 posts a day" tells you what to change.

How to use the impressions calculator

  1. Copy your total impressions and the number of posts for a period from your X analytics, or add up view counts by hand.
  2. Enter the number of days in that period and, if you like, your follower count.
  3. Read the average per post, per day and per follower.
  4. In the what-if section, try a different posting pace. "Use my average" copies your real per-post average into the estimate.
  5. If you are working toward Original Content Rewards, fill in the tracker at the bottom.

What counts as an impression on X?

An impression is one time a post was shown and counted as viewed. X's view counts page explains the rules for the public view number.

  • Any logged-in person who views a post counts, whether or not they follow you.
  • Your own views of your post count too.
  • Repeat views can count more than once, for example on web and then on a phone.
  • Views of embedded posts on other websites do not count.

So impressions are not people. If 100 people each see a post twice, that is 200 impressions but a reach of 100. That's why "impressions per follower" in this tool is a ratio, not a share of followers who saw you.

The what-if estimate multiplies posts per day by average impressions per post, then by 30 or 90 days. At 2 posts a day and 800 impressions each, that is about 48,000 a month. It is a rough guide. One post that travels can beat a whole week of quiet ones, and posting more does not keep your average the same.

How the Original Content Rewards tracker works

The tracker compares your numbers with the two thresholds X lists for its creator payout program. As of October 2026, X's Original Content Rewards page asks for at least 500,000 Home Timeline impressions from verified users in the last 90 days, plus at least 500 verified followers.

Read the fine print before you celebrate. Only impressions from verified users on the Home Timeline count, and replies are excluded. Your analytics total includes everyone, so it will be higher than the number X checks. X also says meeting the thresholds does not guarantee admission. The program replaced Creator Revenue Sharing, which closed on September 7, 2026.

The guide to X creator monetization requirements covers the rest of the rules, such as age, location and original content.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Use medians when you can. One viral post can double your average. If most posts get 300 and one got 20,000, plan around the 300.
  • Compare equal periods. A 28-day month against a 31-day month skews totals, so use the per-day average.
  • Don't confuse impressions with engagement. High impressions with few likes or replies means people scrolled past.
  • Keep expectations grounded. In Buffer's study of 18.8 million posts, regular accounts typically got under 100 impressions per post. Low numbers are normal for small accounts.

To see whether those impressions turned into actions, run a post through the engagement rate calculator or the post performance score. To pick a pace you can keep, try the posting frequency calculator. And to see if more impressions are bringing followers, use the follower growth calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many impressions does an average Twitter post get?

There is no official average from X. In Buffer's study of 18.8 million posts from August 2024 to August 2025, regular accounts typically got under 100 impressions per post, while Premium accounts got far more. Your own average from this impressions calculator is the number that matters most for planning.

Are impressions and views the same on X?

Mostly, yes. The public view count and the impressions figure in X analytics both count times a post was seen, not unique people. X's help page says view counts include repeat views and your own views. Reach, the number of different people who saw a post, is lower than either figure.

Do replies count toward Original Content Rewards impressions?

No, replies are excluded. As of October 2026, X counts Home Timeline impressions from verified users on your posts over the last 90 days, and needs at least 500,000 of them. Impressions on your replies and from users who are not verified do not count toward that threshold.

How accurate is the monthly impressions estimate?

The monthly impressions estimate is a rough guide, not a forecast. It assumes every post gets your average impressions, which never happens in practice. Use it to compare scenarios, such as one post a day versus three, and then check your real total after 30 days to see how close it came.

Where do I find my total impressions?

X's account analytics show total impressions for a date range, but reports say they need a paid plan as of October 2026. Without analytics, you can add up the public view counts on your posts by hand. Use the same date range for impressions and post count so the averages are correct.
