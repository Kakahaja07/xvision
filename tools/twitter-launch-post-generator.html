How to use the Twitter launch post generator

  1. Pick the launch type: new product, feature, beta or waitlist. Each one has its own wording.
  2. Fill in the product name and who it's for. Use the words your users would use, like "solo developers" or "wedding planners".
  3. Describe what it does, starting with a verb, like "turns your commits into a changelog".
  4. Add the key benefit, also starting with a verb, like "save an hour a week".
  5. Add proof if you have it, your price, and your link.

Which X rules matter for a launch post?

Three X rules shape a launch post: the length limit, media limits and how threads show in the feed. Here they are, as of October 2026.

  • 280 characters. A standard post can be up to 280 characters. This tool counts each link as 23 characters, however long the URL is.
  • 4 media items. You can add up to 4 photos, a GIF or a video to one post. A short demo often explains more than a paragraph.
  • Thread truncation. X shortens threads of 4 or more posts in timelines with a "Show this thread" option, according to its thread help page. So post 1 has to work alone.

What about links? This one is disputed. In April 2026, X's head of product Nikita Bier denied that link posts are penalized and suggested putting the link in the post with clear context, as roboin.io reported. The open-source ranking code gives a link click a small weight, so your post still needs replies and reposts to travel.

Tips for writing a launch post that gets replies

The best launch posts are specific. A stranger should know what the product does after one line.

  • Lead with the user, not the tech. "Bakers can price a cake in 2 minutes" beats "AI-native pricing engine".
  • Show it. Add a screenshot or a short demo video to the main post.
  • Use real proof only. A beta count, a quote or your own before and after. Never make numbers up.
  • Stay for the replies. Plan to answer replies for the first few hours. That is what the reply templates are for.

Here's a fictional example. Dev, who runs a tiny tool called "Shipnote", picks "New product", types "turns your git commits into a weekly changelog" and "write release notes in 5 minutes instead of an hour". He gets a short post, a 7-post thread and replies for pricing questions.

What are common launch post mistakes?

The most common mistake is a vague post, like "Excited to share what I've been working on!" with no product, no user and no benefit.

  • Writing about features instead of the result for the user.
  • Posting and leaving. Early replies are your best chance to start a conversation.
  • Using many hashtags. X recommends no more than 2 per post.
  • Launching only once. Follow-up posts reach people who missed day one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I post my launch as a single post or a thread?

Post your launch as a single post first, then add a thread if you have a story to tell. A single post is easy to read, pin and repost. A thread works when you need to show the problem, a demo and proof. X truncates threads of 4 or more posts, so post 1 must stand alone.

Where should I put the link in a launch post?

Put the link in the launch post with a clear sentence about what people will see. In April 2026, X's head of product said link posts are not penalized, though the topic is still disputed. In a thread, the generator places the link near the end, after the problem, demo and proof.

How long should a Twitter launch post be?

A Twitter launch post should fit in 280 characters, the standard X limit. Shorter posts are easier to scan, so leave room for your link, which this tool counts as 23 characters. The generator shows a live count under every draft and flags any version that runs over.

What time should I post a product launch on X?

Post a product launch on X when your audience is online and you can stay to reply. Buffer's 2026 data favors weekday mornings, while Sprout Social's favors weekday afternoons, so test both. Saturday is the weakest day in both studies. Block out the first few hours for replies.

Is this launch post generator AI?

No, this launch post generator is not AI. It uses written templates filled with your product name, audience, benefit, proof, price and link, plus some randomness for new versions.
