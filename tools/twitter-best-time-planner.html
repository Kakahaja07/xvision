What is the best time to post on Twitter in my time zone?

The best time to post on Twitter depends on when your audience is awake, not on your own clock. Sprout Social's 2026 data points to Tuesday to Thursday, 12 to 6 p.m., and Buffer's points to Tuesday at 9 a.m. The free Twitter Best Time Planner converts those windows into your time zone.

I live far from most of my readers. For months I posted at "lunchtime," which was lunchtime for me and the middle of the night for them. The fix was not a new trick. It was simple time zone math.

How to use the Twitter Best Time Planner

The planner needs two things: where you are and where your audience is.

  1. Pick your own time zone. It starts on the one your device reports.
  2. Pick your main audience time zone. Add up to two more if your followers are spread out.
  3. Choose which published windows to use: Sprout Social, Buffer or both.
  4. Read the heatmap. Darker cells are hours where more windows overlap for your audience.
  5. Copy the 2-week test plan or download it as a CSV with empty columns for your results.

One important detail: both sources describe their times as local time. Neither names one fixed time zone, such as Eastern Time. So the planner reads each window as your audience's local time and converts it to yours.

How does the conversion work?

The planner checks every hour of your week and asks, "What time is it for my audience right now?" If that audience time falls inside a published window, the cell gets darker.

It uses your browser's built-in time zone rules, so daylight saving time and half-hour zones like India's are handled for the coming week. With more than one audience, the scores add up. A dark cell can mean one strong window or two regions overlapping.

Example: your audience is in New York and you are in London. Sprout's Tuesday 12 to 6 p.m. becomes Tuesday 5 to 11 p.m. for you. Buffer's Tuesday 9 a.m. becomes 2 p.m. your time, in most weeks of the year.

Why the sources disagree, and what to do about it

Sprout leans toward afternoons and Buffer toward mornings. That is not an error. They used different samples, different networks and different measures.

This is where your own test matters. The 2-week plan tests the strongest hour on up to five days in week one. In week two it tries a different hour on those days, plus one control slot outside every window. The control shows you whether the windows matter for your audience at all.

Tips for a fair test

  • Use a similar post type in each slot, such as a short text tip. Otherwise, you are testing content, not time.
  • Check results 24 hours after each post, not after 10 minutes.
  • Look at several posts per slot before you decide. One viral post can fool you.
  • Repeat the test every few months. Audiences shift.

Fictional example: Ana is a developer in Lisbon whose followers are mostly in San Francisco. The planner shows Sprout's best window landing from about 8 p.m. to 2 a.m. her time. She schedules the late slots and replies over breakfast.

Common mistakes

  • Posting at your own peak hour when your audience is asleep.
  • Treating a published window as a promise. These are averages across many accounts.
  • Forgetting the second region. A New York morning is a London afternoon, so one slot can reach both.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do Sprout Social and Buffer disagree on the best time?

Sprout Social and Buffer measured different things. Sprout used about 2 billion engagements across roughly 307,000 profiles on several networks, while Buffer used more than 8.7 million X posts sent through its own app. Different samples and methods give different peaks, which is why your own test matters most.

What if my audience is spread across many time zones?

Add up to three audience time zones in the Twitter Best Time Planner. The heatmap adds their windows together, so the darkest cells are hours that suit more of your audience at once. If one region clearly matters more, plan most posts for it and test the other region less often.

What if the best window falls in the middle of my night?

Schedule those posts instead of staying awake. X Pro can schedule posts, and X lists it under the Premium+ tier as of October 2026. Third-party schedulers like Buffer also work. Then answer replies when you wake up, since late replies still help the conversation keep going.

How long should I test posting times?

Test for at least two weeks, which is what the Twitter Best Time Planner's test plan covers. One post can do well or badly by luck, so compare several posts per time slot. Keep the post type similar, check impressions and engagements after 24 hours, and repeat the test every few months.
