What does a Twitter post rewriter do?

A Twitter post rewriter turns one draft into several cleaner versions. This free tool cuts filler words, splits long sentences, turns text into a list, adds a closing question, strips hashtags and emoji, and fixes spacing. It is rule-based, so every change follows a fixed rule, and each version is counted against the 280-character limit.

Most drafts aren't bad, just loose. I read mine back and find the same habits: "I think" at the start, a "really" in the middle, one sentence that never ends. This tool catches those patterns in a second.

How to use the Twitter Post Rewriter

  1. Paste or type your draft into the box. The counter shows how many of the 280 characters it uses.
  2. Read the six versions next to it (below it on a phone). Each one applies a different rule.
  3. Check the line under each version for the new count and the characters saved.
  4. Press Edit to load a version back into the box, then stack another rule on top.
  5. Press Copy, paste the text into X and read it once more before you post.

Why is this rewriter rule-based?

This rewriter is rule-based because rules are predictable. You can see exactly why a word disappeared, and the meaning stays yours. It does not use AI, so it will never invent a claim, a number or a story you didn't write.

The trade-off is real: rules don't understand context. If "just" matters ("we just launched"), the Shorter version may still remove it. That is why your draft stays visible next to every version. Everything runs in your browser, and nothing is uploaded.

How long should a post on X be?

A standard post on X can be up to 280 characters, as of October 2026, according to X's How to Post help page. Paid subscribers can write much longer posts. There is no official "best" length, so don't trust anyone who quotes a magic number.

Based on what I've seen, one point in two to four short lines is easier to scan on a phone than a solid block. Links and emoji can count for more than one character, so trust the counter.

Tips and an example rewrite

Before (a made-up draft): "So I just wanted to share that I have been posting on X every single day for 90 days, and honestly it is really the best thing I have done for my small design studio. I think that consistency is basically more important than talent."

After: "I've been posting on X every day for 90 days. It's the best thing I've done for my small design studio. Consistency is more important than talent." The opener, the hedges and the filler are gone, and the long sentence became two.

  • Rewrite in layers. Run Shorter first, press Edit, then pick the question or list version.
  • Keep one idea per post. If the list version has more than five items, it may work better as a thread.
  • Keep your voice. If a line sounds like someone else, keep your own phrasing.

Common mistakes when rewriting a post

The most common mistake is cutting a word that carries meaning, so always check.

  • Ending every post with a question. If every post ends with "Thoughts?", people learn to ignore it.
  • Adding hashtags back in. X's hashtag help page recommends no more than 2 hashtags per post as best practice.
  • Posting near-copies. X's spam rules cover repeatedly posting identical or nearly identical posts, so pick one version and post it once.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this Twitter post rewriter free?

Yes, the XVision Twitter Post Rewriter is free with no sign-up and no limit on how many drafts you rewrite. It runs entirely in your browser, so your text is never uploaded or stored. You can paste a post, copy any of the six versions and close the page without leaving a trace.

Does the post rewriter use AI?

No, the post rewriter uses fixed editing rules, not AI. It removes a known list of filler words and hedges, swaps wordy phrases for short ones, splits sentences at commas and semicolons, and changes the layout. Because of that, it never adds facts, numbers or opinions that were not already in your draft.

Will rewriting change the meaning of my post?

Usually not, but it can. Words like "just", "very" and "maybe" are often filler, yet sometimes they carry meaning. Removing "maybe" turns a guess into a claim, for example. Compare each version with your original draft, and keep your own wording wherever the rule went too far.

Can I rewrite a post that is over 280 characters?

Yes, you can paste text of any length, and each version shows its new count against 280 in red when it is still too long. The Shorter version shows exactly how many characters it saved. If the post is still over, split the text into a thread or cut a whole sentence.
