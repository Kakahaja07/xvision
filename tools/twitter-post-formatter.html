What is a Twitter post formatter?

A Twitter post formatter cleans up the layout of a post before you publish it on X. This free tool adds line breaks, converts bullets to one style, removes extra spaces and blank lines, and can add Unicode bold. A live preview card shows how the post looks, with a count against the 280-character limit.

Text pasted from a notes app or a doc rarely looks right on X. You get stray double spaces, four blank lines in a row, and a mix of dashes and asterisks for bullets. You can fix that by hand in the composer, or paste it here, pick a layout and copy the clean version.

How to use the Twitter Post Formatter

  1. Paste your text into the box. Lines that start with -, *, •, → or a number are treated as list items.
  2. Choose how to break lines: keep your own lines, put each sentence on its own line, or put each paragraph on one line.
  3. Choose the spacing: a blank line after every line (Space every line), only between paragraphs (Space paragraphs), or no blank lines.
  4. Pick a bullet style. Numbers count up on their own, and a new list starts after any normal line.
  5. Turn on bold only if you need it, then check the preview card and the counter.
  6. Press Copy formatted post and paste it into X.

What does each formatting option do?

Each option changes layout only. Your words stay the same, except for bullet markers and bold letters.

Is Unicode bold safe to use on X?

Unicode bold is safe to post, but use it sparingly. The bold letters are not formatting. They are separate math symbols that only look bold, so some screen readers spell them out letter by letter or skip them, and people searching for the plain word may not find your post.

They also cost more. The counter here weighs each bold letter as 2 characters, so a bold 20-letter line uses 40 characters of your 280. My rule: bold a word or two in a long post, never a whole paragraph. The tool shows a warning whenever bold is on so you don't forget. For more styles, the Twitter Font Generator has italic, script and other Unicode sets, with the same trade-offs.

How many characters can a formatted post use?

A standard post on X can use up to 280 characters, as of October 2026, according to X's How to Post page. Line breaks count too, so a post with a blank line between every line uses a few more characters than the same words in one block.

Paid subscribers can post much longer text. Even then, spacing matters more as length grows, because nobody reads a wall of text on a phone.

Formatting tips and examples

Good formatting makes a post easy to scan. Here is a made-up example. Before: one paragraph with three tips separated by commas. After: a short first line, a blank line, then three bullets.

  • Lead with one line that stands alone. The first line decides whether people keep reading.
  • Use one bullet style per post. Mixing dashes and arrows looks messy.
  • Use numbers for steps, bullets for ideas. Numbers tell readers the order matters.
  • Leave the last line alone. A blank line before your closing thought or question gives it weight.

Common formatting mistakes

The biggest mistake is adding so much space that the post looks thin and stretched. A blank line between every short line works for four or five lines. For ten lines, switch to "Space paragraphs".

  • Bold everywhere. When everything is bold, nothing stands out, and the post gets harder to read for screen reader users.
  • Bullets for a single item. A list needs at least two items.
  • Ignoring the counter. Formatting adds characters. Check the count after you change the spacing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this Twitter post formatter free?

Yes, the XVision Twitter Post Formatter is free, with no sign-up and no limits. It runs entirely in your browser, so your text is never uploaded or saved. You can format as many posts as you like, copy the result and paste it straight into the X composer on the web or in the app.

Do line breaks show up on X?

Yes, line breaks you paste into the X composer show up in the published post. Single line breaks and blank lines both carry over. The formatter removes stacks of three or more empty lines, which usually look like a mistake, and keeps one blank line wherever you choose a spaced layout.

Do line breaks count toward the 280-character limit?

Yes, each line break counts as one character toward the 280-character limit on X. A blank line between two lines uses two characters. That is why a heavily spaced post can hit the limit sooner than you expect, and why the preview card counts the formatted text, not your original.

Can I use bold or italic text in a normal X post?

You can fake bold with Unicode characters, which is what this formatter does. They copy and paste like normal text. The downside is accessibility: some screen readers spell the letters out, search may not match them, and each letter counts as 2 characters. Use bold for a word or two only.
