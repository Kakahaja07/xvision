      How do I compare engagement between Twitter posts or accounts?

      Compare engagement rates, not raw likes. Divide each post's engagements by its impressions, or each account's engagements by its followers, then rank the results. The Twitter Engagement Comparison Tool does both for 2 to 5 posts or accounts and shows two separate rankings, because the two rates measure different things.

      Raw numbers fool people all the time. A post with 210 engagements looks like a winner next to one with 95, until you notice it was seen six times as often. Rates level the field.

      How to use the Twitter Engagement Comparison Tool

      1. Pick "Posts" or "Accounts."
      2. Give each row a name you'll recognize.
      3. Enter impressions, engagements and followers for each row. For accounts, use totals from the same date range.
      4. Add rows up to 5, or remove them down to 2.
      5. Read the two ranked lists. Each one has its own bars and its own winner.
      6. Copy the comparison if you want to keep it in your notes.

      What is the difference between the two engagement rates?

      Engagement rate per impression tells you how well content worked for the people who saw it. Engagement rate per follower tells you how active an account is relative to its audience size. They use different denominators, so their numbers can't be mixed or compared with each other.

      RateFormulaBest for
      Per impressionengagements / impressions x 100Judging content quality
      Per followerengagements / followers x 100Comparing accounts of different sizes

      X itself uses the per-impression version. Its CSV definitions page defines engagement rate as engagements divided by total impressions. Many benchmark reports use the per-follower version. The Rival IQ 2026 benchmark report divides interactions by follower count and puts X's median at 0.03% per post for 2025 data.

      Here's where people go wrong. They take a 2% per-impression rate from their analytics, compare it with that 0.03% per-follower benchmark, and decide they're crushing it. Different formula, different scale. Compare like with like.

      Tips for fair engagement comparisons

      A fair comparison changes only one thing at a time. Keep everything else as close as you can.

      • Use the same time window. Compare posts at a similar age, ideally 2 or more days old, so late engagement has landed.
      • Use the same engagement definition. X's own "engagements" count includes clicks and expands. Likes plus replies plus reposts is a smaller number.
      • Use follower counts from the same moment. For older posts, an estimate is fine. Just be consistent.
      • Compare similar formats. Buffer's March 2026 format study found text posts had the highest median engagement rate on X, ahead of images, video and links, so mixing formats can blur the lesson.

      Common mistakes when comparing engagement

      Most mistakes come from mixing numbers that don't belong together:

      • Ranking by raw likes instead of a rate.
      • Putting a per-impression rate next to a per-follower rate.
      • Comparing a 2-hour-old post with a week-old one.
      • Reading too much into tiny samples. A post with 50 impressions and 5 likes shows 10%, but it proves little.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Which engagement rate should I use to compare accounts?

      Use engagement rate per follower to compare accounts of different sizes, since it scales activity to audience size. If you can see each account's impressions, add the per-impression rate too. The Twitter Engagement Comparison Tool shows both side by side, and each one gets its own separate ranking.

      Can I compare my posts with a competitor's posts?

      Yes, but usually only on the per-follower rate. Anyone can see likes, replies, reposts and view counts on public posts, plus follower counts on profiles. Full engagement counts, which include clicks and expands, are private to the author, so use likes plus replies plus reposts for every row to keep it fair.

      Why does my best post rank differently on each list?

      A post can rank high per impression and low per follower when it reached many people outside your followers, or the other way around. That is normal. It tells you whether a post won on quality of reaction or on size of reach, which is more useful than a single score.

      What is a good engagement rate on X?

      There is no single good engagement rate on X, because it depends on the formula. Rival IQ's 2026 report put X's median at 0.03% per follower per post for brands. Per-impression rates are much higher by design. Compare yourself with your own past posts first, then with peers using the same formula.

      Does the tool save the numbers I enter?

      No, the Twitter Engagement Comparison Tool does not save or send anything. All math runs inside your browser on this page, and the numbers disappear when you close or reload the tab. Use the copy button if you want to keep a comparison for later.
