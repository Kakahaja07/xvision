How do you calculate Twitter engagement rate?

A Twitter engagement rate calculator divides a post's interactions by its views (impressions) and multiplies by 100. This free calculator adds likes, replies and reposts, so 288 interactions on 12,000 views gives 2.40%. A second method divides by followers instead, which is how benchmark reports such as Rival IQ measure it.

How to use the engagement rate calculator

You only need the numbers X shows under every post.

  1. Open the post on X and note the view count, likes, replies and reposts.
  2. Type the views into the Views box and the three interaction counts into their boxes.
  3. Add your follower count if you want the reach figure. It is optional.
  4. Read the three results: engagement rate, reach vs followers, and the share of interactions that are replies.
  5. Repeat for 5 to 10 recent posts so you can compare them with each other.

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

The two engagement rate formulas

The per-view formula measures quality and the per-follower formula measures activity relative to audience size. Here they are side by side.

  • Per view (per impression): (likes + replies + reposts) ÷ views × 100
  • Per follower: (likes + replies + reposts) ÷ followers × 100

This calculator shows the per-view rate. It also shows "reach vs followers", which is views ÷ followers. Multiply the per-view rate by that reach number and you get the per-follower rate, so you can work out both from one screen.

How does X calculate engagement rate?

X's own analytics count more actions than this calculator, so X's number is usually higher. X's CSV definitions page describes engagement rate as engagements divided by total impressions.

The catch is what X calls an engagement. Its list includes reposts, replies, follows and likes, plus clicks on links, hashtags, media, your username and your profile photo, and post expansions. Those clicks are not shown publicly, so a calculator that only uses public numbers can't include them.

That is fine if you stay consistent. Use one method for every post you compare.

Is my engagement rate good?

The fairest answer is to compare a post with your own recent posts. A rate that is high for you is a good sign, whatever any chart says.

If you want an outside number, the best known one comes from the Rival IQ 2026 benchmark report. It put X's median engagement rate at 0.03% per post in 2025 data, up from 0.015% the year before. That figure is per follower, and it comes from company accounts sampled across industries.

So compare like with like. Only hold your per-follower rate against 0.03%, never your per-view rate. Your per-view number will almost always look far bigger because the bottom of the fraction is different. And remember that a personal creator account is a different kind of account from the brands in that sample, so treat it as a rough reference.

Tips for reading your results

  • High rate, low reach: people who saw it liked it, but few saw it. Try posting a similar idea at a different time, or test new hooks with the hook generator.
  • Low rate, high reach: the post traveled but didn't land. This often happens when a topic is broad and the post doesn't give strangers a reason to act.
  • High reply share: the post started a conversation. Reply back. Conversations are usually what you want more of on X.

For a full 0 to 100 grade that also counts bookmarks, profile visits and link clicks, try the post performance score. To track views across many posts, use the impressions calculator. To put two accounts or two periods side by side, the engagement comparison tool does that.

Common mistakes

Most bad engagement rate numbers come from mixing methods, not from bad posts.

  • Comparing a per-view rate with a per-follower benchmark.
  • Checking a post after one hour and comparing it with a post that has been live for a week.
  • Counting a viral outlier as your normal. Use a median of several posts.
  • Forgetting that your own views of your post count as views, which matters on very small posts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as engagement on Twitter?

Engagement on Twitter means any action someone takes on a post. This calculator counts likes, replies and reposts because those are public. X's own analytics count more, including link clicks, profile clicks, media clicks, follows and post expansions, so X's engagement rate for the same post is usually higher than a public-numbers estimate.

Why is my engagement rate higher than the 0.03% benchmark?

Your rate is probably higher than 0.03% because you calculated it per view, while the Rival IQ benchmark is per follower. Dividing by views gives much larger numbers. The benchmark also comes from company accounts across industries, so even a fair per-follower comparison is only a rough reference for a personal account.

Do quotes and bookmarks count in this calculator?

No, this calculator counts likes, replies and reposts only, so the result stays simple and repeatable. If a post got many quotes or bookmarks, add them into the reposts box to include them. Just do the same for every post you compare, or the rates will not line up fairly.

How long should I wait before checking a post's engagement rate?

Wait at least 24 hours, and check every post at the same age so the comparison is fair. Views and likes keep adding up after posting. X's business help center says post activity metrics can take up to 36 hours to settle, so a 48-hour check is a safe habit.
