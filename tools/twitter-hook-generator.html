What is a Twitter hook, and how do you write one?

A Twitter hook is the first line of a post or thread, the part that decides whether people keep reading. This free Twitter hook generator takes your topic, audience and angle and gives you 11 opening lines across eight types, from curiosity and contrarian to number, story and mistake hooks.

I write hooks last. Once I know what the post actually delivers, I can make the first line promise exactly that, no more and no less.

How to use the Twitter Hook Generator

It takes a few seconds, and the hooks update as you type.

  1. Type your topic in a few words, like "pricing your services".
  2. Add your audience as a plural, like "new freelancers" or "first-time founders". This is optional.
  3. Pick an angle, or leave it on Mix to get every type.
  4. Read the 11 hooks and pick two or three that fit what your post really delivers.
  5. Copy one and edit it so it sounds like you. Generate again gives you a fresh set.

What makes a hook good instead of bait?

A good hook makes a promise the post keeps. Engagement bait makes a demand ("like if you agree", "wait for the end") or a promise the post can't keep. This generator never writes bait.

X treats bait as low quality. Its Authenticity policy bans coordinating to exchange engagement, and its Original Content Rewards rules tell members not to repeatedly ask people to like, reply, repost or follow. X's open-source ranking code also gives negative weights to "not interested" and "mute author" signals, as of October 2026.

Readers push back too. XVision, the free Chrome extension behind this site, can collapse posts that look like engagement bait into one quiet line in the feed.

Tips for writing better hooks

The best hooks are specific, honest and short.

  • Write the hook last. You can't promise what you haven't written yet.
  • Be specific. "3 pricing mistakes" beats "some thoughts on pricing".
  • Name the audience when it helps. "For new freelancers" pulls in the right readers.
  • Cut the warm-up. Delete "So," or "I've been thinking" at the start and see if the line gets stronger.
  • Rotate types. Keep notes on which hook types your audience responds to.

A fictional example: topic "meal planning", audience "busy parents". The Mix set might offer "Most busy parents get meal planning wrong in the same way." If your post explains that one shared mistake and its fix, the hook fits. If the post is a single quick tip, a how-to hook is the honest choice.

What hook mistakes should you avoid?

Most weak hooks either promise too much or say too little.

  • Overpromising. If the post doesn't deliver, readers notice and don't come back.
  • Using one type every day. Ten contrarian hooks in a row stop feeling bold.
  • Vague questions. A question nobody can answer in one reply gets no replies.
  • Fake urgency. Stacked emoji and ALL CAPS read as noise, not energy.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a Twitter hook be?

Keep a Twitter hook to one short line that reads in a glance on a phone. X has no separate hook limit; the whole post must fit 280 characters as of October 2026. Each hook here shows its count, so you can see how much room is left for the rest.

Is engagement bait against X's rules?

Yes, some engagement bait breaks X's rules. X's Authenticity policy bans coordinating to exchange likes, reposts, replies or follows. Members of X's Original Content Rewards program must not repeatedly ask people to like, reply, repost or follow. Even when bait breaks no rule, readers can tap Not interested or mute you.

Does the hook generator use AI?

No, the hook generator does not use AI. It mixes your topic and audience into a large bank of hand-written hook patterns across eight types, then shuffles them. Everything runs in your browser, so it's instant and private, and you can generate fresh sets as often as you like.

Which hook type gets the most engagement?

No hook type wins everywhere, and X publishes no data ranking them. The right type depends on your post: number hooks suit lists, story hooks suit real experiences, and questions suit posts that invite replies. Test two or three types with your own audience and compare results over a few weeks.
