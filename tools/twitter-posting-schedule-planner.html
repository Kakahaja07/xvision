Runs in your browser. The calendar file is made on your device.

What is a good Twitter posting schedule?

A good Twitter posting schedule spreads your posts evenly across the hours you are really online, leaves time for replies, and repeats each week so it becomes a habit. The free Twitter Posting Schedule Planner builds one from your posts per day, active hours and time zone, then exports it as a calendar file.

Here's the thing. Posting "whenever I have something" tends to bunch posts up in the evening, followed by days of silence. A fixed schedule spreads them out so your followers see you regularly.

How to use the Twitter Posting Schedule Planner

It takes about a minute. The defaults give you a sensible starting plan.

  1. Enter how many posts you want per day and how many replies you aim to write.
  2. Set the hours you are usually awake and able to check X, such as 8:00 to 21:00.
  3. Pick your time zone. It starts on the zone your device reports.
  4. Tap the days you want off. Sunday is off by default.
  5. Click Build schedule and check the weekly grid.
  6. Download the .ics file to add reminders to Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar, or the CSV for a spreadsheet.

How does the planner choose the times?

The planner splits your active hours into equal slots and puts one activity in the middle of each slot. Posts take evenly spaced slots, and reply sessions fill the gaps, so nothing clusters.

The formula is simple. Slot time = start + window × (slot number + 0.5) ÷ total slots, rounded to the nearest 5 minutes. Replies are grouped into 15-minute sessions of about 5 replies each.

Example: 8:00 to 21:00 is 780 minutes. With 3 posts and 10 replies, you get 3 posts plus 2 reply sessions, so 5 slots that are 156 minutes apart. The planner gives you:

Why should you spread posts out?

Spacing helps each post get its own moment. X's open-source For You algorithm applies an author diversity rule: in a viewer's feed, each post after an author's first is multiplied by a decaying factor. Three posts in ten minutes mostly compete with each other.

The same repo says For You drops posts older than 48 hours, so a steady daily rhythm keeps fresh posts in the pool. That's my reading of the code, not an X statement about schedules. Your own results matter more.

Reply sessions matter too. They keep you in conversations between posts, and they are often how small accounts get found.

How do you actually schedule posts on X?

XVision does not schedule or publish posts. This planner only creates calendar reminders. To schedule real posts, you have three main options as of October 2026.

  • X Pro. X's guide to X Pro says you can schedule posts for the future and view them in a Scheduled column. X lists X Pro access under the Premium+ tier.
  • The ads.x.com composer. It can schedule posts, though X's help page frames these as Promoted-only posts.
  • Third-party schedulers. Buffer, for example, offers a free plan with up to 3 channels and 10 scheduled posts per channel.

Or keep it simple: let the calendar ping you, then post by hand. The guide on how to schedule Twitter posts in advance walks through each option.

Tips and a quick example

Start with fewer posts than you think you can handle. You can always add a slot next month.

Fictional example: Tom is a nurse who posts study tips. He works day shifts, so he sets 2 posts a day, 5 replies, active hours of 18:00 to 22:00, and Sunday off. His posts land at 18:40 and 21:20, with a reply session at 20:00. It fits his real life, so he sticks with it.

  • Import the .ics into a separate calendar so you can hide it in one click.
  • Write posts in a batch, then use the reminders only to publish and reply.
  • Recheck your schedule each season, since your routine changes.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Setting active hours you are not really online. A reminder at 7:00 a.m. is useless if you wake at 9:00.
  • Posting and leaving. Stay a few minutes to answer early replies.
  • Copying someone else's posting times without testing them on your audience.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I import the .ics file into Google Calendar or Outlook?

Yes, the .ics file uses the standard iCalendar format, so Google Calendar, Outlook and Apple Calendar can import it. In Google Calendar, open Settings, choose Import and export, and pick the file. Consider importing into a new, separate calendar so you can hide or delete the whole schedule in one step later.

Does the planner post or schedule tweets for me?

No, the Twitter Posting Schedule Planner only creates reminders in your own calendar. It never connects to X. To schedule real posts, use X Pro, which X lists under the Premium+ tier as of October 2026, the scheduler in ads.x.com, or a third-party tool such as Buffer.

What happens when daylight saving time changes?

Each event in the .ics file is converted to UTC for its own date, using your chosen time zone's rules. So a 9:20 a.m. post stays at 9:20 a.m. local time on both sides of a daylight saving change. If you move to another time zone, build a new file for the new zone.

Should I post at exactly the same time every day?

Not exactly the same, but close is good for building a habit. Same slots make posting automatic. Once a month, shift one slot by an hour or two and compare impressions in X analytics, which is a paid feature as of October 2026. Keep the times that work and drop the rest.
