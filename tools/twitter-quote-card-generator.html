How do you make a quote card for Twitter?

A Twitter quote card generator turns a short quote and a name into a clean image you can post on X. Type the quote, add the speaker and an optional @handle, then pick a theme, font and size such as 1:1 (1080 x 1080 pixels). Download a PNG or JPEG. It is free and runs in your browser.

Here's the thing about good lines: in a busy feed they slide past. On a calm card, people pause for a second.

How to use the Twitter quote card generator

  1. Type the quote. Keep it to one idea.
  2. Add the attribution: the person who said it, or your own name.
  3. Add an X handle if you want one, with or without the @.
  4. Pick a theme (Light, Dark, Paper or Bold), a size, a font and the alignment.
  5. Watch the preview. The text shrinks until it fits. If the tool warns that the quote is too long, cut a few words.
  6. Click Download PNG or Download JPEG, then attach the image to your post on X.

Which size should you pick for X?

Pick 1:1 or 4:5 for most quote cards, and 16:9 when the quote is longer or you want a wide, slide-like look. X's help page on posting photos and GIFs says a single photo with a standard aspect ratio between 2:1 and 3:4 shows in full in the post (as of October 2026). What makes a quote card easy to read?

A readable quote card has one short idea, strong contrast and plenty of empty space. Most good cards hold about 10 to 30 words. Past that, the text gets small on a phone and people scroll on.

  • Cut the warm-up. Start the quote where the point starts.
  • Pick contrast over style. Light and Dark are the easiest to read. Paper feels softer. Bold works for punchy one-liners.
  • Add context in the post text. Say why the line matters to you. A card with no words around it gives people nothing to reply to.

How should you attribute a quote correctly?

Only put a person's name on a card if you can point to where they said those exact words. Misquotes travel fast on X, and a wrong name hurts your credibility more than a missing one. Check the original source before you design anything.

  • Quote the exact words. If you shortened them, write "paraphrased" in the post.
  • For your own words, use your name and handle.
  • For a customer or teammate, ask before you put their name and words on a public image.

Common quote card mistakes

  • Too many words. If the text drops to a small size, cut it in half rather than switching fonts.
  • Wrong or invented attribution. Famous names attract fake quotes. Verify first.
  • No alt text. People using screen readers can't read text inside an image. Put the full quote and the name in the image description.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this Twitter quote card generator free?

Yes, the Twitter quote card generator is free with no sign-up and no watermark. You can make as many cards as you like in 1:1, 4:5 or 16:9 sizes and download each one as a PNG or JPEG file. Everything runs in your browser, so there is no account, upload or usage limit to worry about.

Are my quotes uploaded anywhere?

No, your quote never leaves your device. The card is drawn on an HTML canvas inside your browser, and the PNG or JPEG file is created locally when you click download. There is no server step, so you can safely design cards with unreleased product lines, client quotes or drafts you are not ready to share yet.

Can I post a quote card with someone else's words?

Yes, as long as the quote is accurate and credited to the right person. Check the original source, such as a book, interview or post, and copy the exact wording. Short credited quotes are common, but rules differ by country, so check the source's terms before using long passages or anything sold as paid content.

How do I add alt text to a quote card on X?

On the X app, tap the +Alt button on the attached image, type the description and tap Done. On x.com, click Add description instead. X's help center lists a 1,000 character limit as of October 2026. For a quote card, type the full quote and the speaker's name so screen reader users get the same words.

Why does the text get smaller when I type more?

The generator auto-fits the quote to the card. It starts with large text and steps the font size down until every line fits between the quote mark and the name. If the text reaches the smallest readable size and still does not fit, the tool shows a warning so you can shorten the quote or pick 16:9.
