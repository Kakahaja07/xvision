How do I choose content pillars for Twitter?

Choose 3 to 5 content pillars that sit where your expertise, your audience's problems and your goal overlap. Most accounts need one teaching pillar, one proof or story pillar and one conversation pillar. The free Twitter Content Pillar Generator suggests pillars, example topics, formats and a posting mix in one click.

Without a plan, every post is a fresh decision. Monday is a hot take, Tuesday a meme, Wednesday a random link. Visitors can't tell what the account is about, and often neither can the person running it. Pillars fix that.

How to use the Twitter Content Pillar Generator

You need about a minute and three honest answers.

  1. Type your niche, like "personal finance" or "indie game dev".
  2. List 2 to 5 things you know well, separated by commas. These become the topics inside each pillar.
  3. Pick your goals, such as growing followers, getting clients or selling a product.
  4. Choose 3, 4 or 5 pillars and how many posts you plan per week.
  5. Click Generate pillars. Use Generate again for a new set, and copy the cards you like.

What does each pillar card mean?

Each card gives you a pillar name, its purpose, four example topics, three post formats and a share of your posting mix. The purpose line tells you why the pillar exists, which helps you decide what to cut later.

The mix works like this. Every pillar type starts with a base score. Each goal you pick adds points to the pillars that serve it. Then the scores turn into percentages rounded to 5, with at least 10% each, so the total is always 100.

Goal you pickPillars that get a bigger share
Grow followersOpinions most, plus a small boost for most others
Build authorityHow-to most, then results, opinions and curation

Land a jobResults most, then how-to, stories and behind the scenes

A teaching pillar is always included, because helping for free is how most people find a reason to follow. An offer pillar only appears if you pick getting clients or selling a product.

Why do content pillars help on X?

Pillars help because they make you predictable in a good way. A visitor who reads three posts should know what they'll get by following.

They also fit how X recommends posts. X's open-source algorithm repo says the For You feed ranks posts by predicting each viewer's actions from their recent engagement history. Out-of-network sources include SimClusters, which group accounts and posts by interest. My reading: a clear set of topics makes it easier for the right people to keep seeing you. X hasn't stated that as a rule.

Formats matter too. Buffer's 2026 content format study found text posts had the highest median engagement rate on X (3.56%), ahead of images (3.40%), video (2.96%) and link posts (2.25%). That's why text formats show up often on the cards.

Tips and a quick example

  • Name pillars so you remember them. If you can't list them from memory, rename or cut one.
  • Write topics as questions you can answer. "How long does budgeting really take?" is easier to post than "Budgeting."
  • Mix formats inside a pillar. A teaching pillar can hold threads, single tips and checklists.
  • Put your best pillar in your bio and pinned post. It tells visitors what to expect.

Fictional example: Sam knows budgeting, index investing and paying off debt, and wants to grow followers. With 7 posts a week, the generator might suggest How-to: budgeting at 35%, opinions at 25%, stories at 20% and curation at 20%. That's about 2 or 3 teaching posts, 2 opinions, and 1 or 2 each of the rest.

Common mistakes with pillars

  • Choosing pillars you find fun but your audience doesn't care about.
  • Letting the offer pillar take over. A feed of pitches loses followers.
  • Picking pillars too broad to post about, like "business" or "life."
  • Never checking which pillar's posts actually get replies and follows.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many content pillars should I have on X?

Three to five content pillars work for most X accounts. Three is easier to remember and keeps your profile focused, which helps new visitors understand what you post. Five gives more variety for accounts posting daily. If you can't name your pillars from memory, you probably have too many.

What does the mix percentage mean?

The mix percentage is the share of your posts that should come from each pillar. With a 35% teaching pillar and 7 posts a week, about 2 or 3 posts teach something. The percentages always add up to 100 and shift toward the pillars that match the goals you pick.

Should every pillar promote my product?

No, most pillars should help people for free, and only one should focus on your offer. The Twitter Content Pillar Generator adds an offer pillar only when you choose a goal like getting clients or selling a product, and keeps it small. Useful posts earn the trust that makes the occasional pitch work.

Can I change my content pillars later?

Yes, and you should review your content pillars every month or two. Look at which pillar's posts got the most replies, reposts and new followers. Give that pillar a bigger share, cut the weakest one, or swap it for a new pillar. Pillars are a plan, not a promise.
