How can you preview a Twitter thread before posting?

A Twitter thread preview shows your posts stacked the way X displays a thread, before you publish anything. Paste your thread into this free tool, split posts with blank lines or a line of three dashes, and you get an X-style mock with your name, handle, connector line and a live 280-character count on every post.

Here's why I preview. A thread can look fine in a notes app and still feel wrong on X. Line breaks land differently, one post runs long, or the hook gets buried. Seeing it in a timeline-style layout catches those problems in seconds.

How to use the Twitter Thread Preview

The preview updates as you type, so you can edit and check in one place.

  1. Paste your thread into the text box.
  2. Separate posts with a blank line. If your posts have blank lines inside them, put a line with three dashes (---) between posts instead.
  3. Type your display name and @handle so the mock looks like your account.
  4. Switch between Light and Dark to see both themes.
  5. Fix any post marked in red, then copy your final text back into X or your scheduler.

A numbering line like 3/7 that sits alone after a blank line stays attached to its post. That means threads from the Twitter Thread Splitter preview correctly.

How does the 280-character check work?

The counter uses X's weighting rules. As of October 2026, a standard post allows 280 characters, every link counts as 23 and emoji count as 2. Any post over 280 turns red, and the part past the limit gets highlighted, so you know exactly how much to cut.

Paid X plans can write longer single posts, but X's help pages give different caps (25,000 on one page, 4,000 on another). The preview flags anything over 280 because that is the limit every account shares.

Why does the first post matter so much?

The first post decides whether anyone opens the rest. X's thread help page says threads of 4 or more posts get cut short on timelines with a "Show this thread" link. On a timeline, most people see your hook and little else.

When your thread reaches 4 posts, the preview reminds you of that. Look at post 1 by itself. Does it make a clear promise? If not, test a few openings from the Twitter Hook Generator.

Tips for a thread that reads well

Small layout choices change how easy a thread feels on a phone.

  • One idea per post. Each post should still make sense if someone shares it alone.
  • Short lines and white space. Walls of text look heavier on a small screen.
  • Links where they belong. Put a link in the post it supports. Each one costs 23 characters.
  • One clear ask at the end. A single question beats three requests.

A fictional example: a 6-post thread about learning to bake bread. In the preview, post 3 shows 301 / 280, with its last sentence in red. Cutting the filler phrase "at the end of the day" and one extra adjective fixed it in a single edit.

What mistakes does the preview catch?

Most thread problems are small, and they're easy to miss in a plain text box.

  • A post that is over the limit by a few characters, often because of a link or emoji.
  • Two posts that merged because a blank line was missing.
  • A post that ends mid-thought because it was split in the wrong place.
  • A hook that only makes sense after you see post 2.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Twitter thread preview look exactly like X?

No, the Twitter thread preview is a close mock, not a pixel-perfect copy of X. It shows the stacked layout, avatar, name, handle, connector line, links and line breaks the way a thread reads. Fonts, spacing and buttons vary by device and app version, so always glance at the real post too.

How do I separate posts in the preview?

Separate posts with one blank line. If a single post needs blank lines inside it, put a line with three dashes between posts instead, and the preview will split only on those dashes. A numbering line like 2/6 that sits alone after a blank line stays with the post above it.

Is the thread preview tool private?

Yes, the thread preview tool is private. Everything runs in your browser, and the text, name and handle you type are never uploaded or saved on a server. It doesn't connect to your X account either, so you can test drafts freely before you post them for real.

Why does my post show more characters than I typed?

Your post shows more characters because X weights some characters. Every link counts as 23 characters no matter its length, emoji count as 2, and most Chinese, Japanese and Korean characters count as 2. The preview follows the open-source twitter-text rules Twitter published, so its count should closely match X's own composer.
