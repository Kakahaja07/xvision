How to Build a Community on X in 2026

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you build a community on X?

To build a community on X, give a specific group a reason to keep coming back and talk with each other, not just with you. Reply to comments, run a weekly prompt, host live Spaces, and spotlight members' work. X announced in April 2026 that it would shut down its Communities feature, so don't rely on one tool.

Picture two accounts with 3,000 followers each. One posts and gets a scatter of likes from strangers. The other posts and the same twenty names show up in the replies, joking with each other, sharing their own progress, tagging friends.

The second account has a community. It's warmer, it's more fun to run, and those twenty people will back almost anything that account does next. The great news is that this isn't luck. It's a set of habits you can start this week.

Context matters here, because the tools changed in 2026. Let me explain what happened, then the methods that work no matter which features X keeps.

What does a community on X look like?

A community on X is a group of people who return regularly and talk with each other, not only with you. Followers watch. Community members take part.

You'll know you have one when you see the same names in your replies week after week, when members reply to each other's comments under your posts, and when people tag you to share something they think you'd like. Your follower count can be modest. Engagement from a steady core matters far more.

What happened to X Communities?

X Communities, the group feature launched in 2021, were scheduled to shut down in 2026. TechCrunch reported on April 23, 2026 that X planned to close Communities on May 6, 2026, later extending the migration deadline to May 30.

X's head of product, Nikita Bier, said Communities were used by less than 0.4% of users but contributed to 80% of spam reports, financial scams and malware on X. X pointed admins toward XChat group chats with joinable links, which TechCrunch said would support up to 500 members at first.

Here's the confusing part. At the time of writing (October 2026), X's help page for Communities is still online, and its Premium page still lists creating a Community as a feature. I couldn't confirm whether Communities still work for every account. Check your own app. Either way, the lesson is clear: build your community on habits and relationships, not on a single feature that could disappear.

6 ways to build a community on X

The six methods that work regardless of features are replying to everyone, weekly rituals, live Spaces, public Lists, spotlighting members, and group chats. Start with the first two, since they cost nothing and work from day one.

1. Reply to every reply

Replying to people who reply to you is the single fastest way to turn a follower into a regular. It shows you're listening, and it gives others a reason to join in. X also says that when it ranks replies, it considers whether the original author has replied. For techniques, see how to get more replies on X.

2. Create a weekly ritual

A recurring post gives people a reason to come back on a set day. Try "Share what you shipped this week," "Friday wins thread," or "Ask me anything about pricing." The format matters less than the consistency. After a few weeks, people start showing up before you even post.

3. Host live Spaces

Spaces are live audio rooms on X. According to X's Spaces help page, they're public, so anyone can listen, and up to 13 people can speak at once, including the host and two co-hosts. You need the iOS or Android app to start one. A monthly Space where members bring questions builds connection faster than any post.

4. Build a public List of members

X Lists let you group accounts, and other people can follow your Lists. Make a public List of your most active members, something like "People building in public I learn from." It recognizes them, and it helps them find each other.

5. Spotlight members' work

Quote a member's post and add why it's great. Share their launch. Credit someone whose reply gave you an idea. Recognition is the glue of every community, online or off.

6. Use group chats for your core members

For your closest regulars, a group chat gives a private space to talk. X's push toward XChat joinable links makes this easier inside the app. Keep the public conversation on your timeline, though, since that's where new people discover you.

Why mutual follows matter more than you'd think

Mutual follows matter because X's ranking currently favors replies between people who follow each other. That makes a tight, connected group more visible to itself.

X's open-source ranking code includes a mutual-follow reply boost. Since July 2026 it has raised the weight on the predicted chance that you'll reply to an original post from someone you mutually follow, with a value of 15.0 as of October 2026. In plain words, when members follow each other, their conversations are more likely to surface. So follow your regulars back and encourage them to follow each other.

How do you know your community is growing?

Your community is growing when the number of repeat repliers rises, and when members start talking to each other without you. Count the names that show up in your replies at least twice a week. That number matters more than total followers.

I built a free Chrome extension, XVision, with a network view that shows who engages with you most and who doesn't. It's a quick way to see your core group and make sure you're replying to them in return. A notebook list of names works too.

Mistakes that stall a community

The mistakes that stall communities most often are talking at people instead of with them, moving everyone off X too early, and letting spam or rudeness slide.

  • Broadcast only. If you never reply, people stop replying too.
  • Leaving too soon. Moving 20 members to another platform cuts you off from the thousands who could find you on X.
  • No boundaries. Hide or report spam and abuse quickly. Members notice when you protect the space.

What this means for you

Building a community on X is about people, not features. Features come and go, as X Communities showed in 2026. Replies, rituals, recognition and real conversations don't.

Start with your audience. If you haven't defined it yet, finding your target audience on Twitter is the first step, and attracting followers on X brings in the people who'll become regulars. Then reply to everyone, pick a weekly ritual, and watch for the names that keep coming back. Those people are your community, and they're worth every minute.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are X Communities still available in 2026?

X announced in April 2026 that it would shut down X Communities on May 6, 2026, later extending the migration deadline to May 30, according to TechCrunch. X's help page for Communities was still online in October 2026, so availability is unclear. Check the Communities tab in your own app before relying on it.

What replaced X Communities?

X pointed Community admins to XChat group chats with joinable links that can be shared and pinned on the timeline. TechCrunch reported these groups would support up to 500 members at first, with a goal of 1,000. X also launched Custom Timelines for Premium subscribers around the same time.

Do I need X Premium to build a community on X?

No, X Premium is not required to build a community on X. Replies, weekly threads, Lists, quote posts and listening to Spaces are available on free accounts. X's Premium page lists creating a Community as a Premium feature, but the most effective community habits cost nothing.

How many followers do you need to build a community on X?

There is no minimum follower count for building a community on X. A community can start with ten people who reply regularly and talk to each other. Repeat engagement matters more than size, so focus on the people already replying to you before chasing a bigger audience.

What is the difference between followers and a community on X?

Followers on X see your posts in their feed, while a community actively takes part. Community members reply often, talk to each other in your replies, share your work, and come back on a regular rhythm. An account with 1,000 engaged members can often have more influence than one with 50,000 silent followers.
