Why Am I Losing Followers on Twitter (X)? Causes and Fixes

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Why am I losing followers on Twitter?

Losing followers on Twitter (X) usually comes from three causes: X removed locked or spam accounts from your count, follow-for-follow accounts unfollowed you, or your recent posts drifted from what people followed you for. Small daily drops are normal. A sudden large drop usually points to a cleanup or a content change.

You check your profile in the morning and the number is lower than yesterday. Maybe it's 3 followers. Maybe it's 300. Either way, it stings.

Don't worry, this is one of the most common things that happens on X, the app formerly called Twitter. Every account loses some followers all the time. The useful question isn't "why did anyone leave?" It's "is this normal churn or a signal I should act on?"

Let me explain the causes one by one, starting with the ones you can't control and ending with the ones you can.

Is it normal to lose followers on X?

Yes, losing a few followers on X every week is normal. People quit the app, change interests, clean up their feeds, and X itself removes spam and locked accounts. Healthy accounts lose followers daily and still grow, because they gain more than they lose.

What matters is your net change over a week or a month. A loss of 5 on a day you gained 20 is a good day.

The 7 most common reasons you're losing followers

The seven usual causes are X's account cleanups, deactivated accounts, follow-for-follow unfollows, topic drift, a change in posting volume, a tone shift, and plain display glitches. The first three aren't about you at all.

1. X removed locked or spam accounts

X periodically removes accounts it has locked from everyone's follower counts. Its Following FAQs say directly that if an account that follows you is locked, the number shown on your profile may go down.

This has a history. In a 2018 post called Confidence in follower counts, the company (then Twitter) explained it was removing locked accounts from counts globally. It said most people would see a change of four followers or fewer, larger accounts would see bigger drops, and counts "may continue to change more regularly" as it keeps challenging problem accounts. So a sudden drop with no clear reason is often just a cleanup.

2. Some followers deactivated or got hidden as spam

X's common following issues page says that a follower missing from your list was likely hidden for spam activity, unfollowed you, or deactivated their account. A quality filter also hides suspected spam from your followers list unless you turn it off.

3. Follow-for-follow accounts unfollowed you

Some accounts follow lots of people, wait for a follow back, then unfollow. X calls this follow churn and bans it in its authenticity rules. If you gained a burst of followers you didn't recognize, expect many of them to leave within days.

4. Your topic drifted

People followed you for something specific. If you were posting about marketing and switched to daily politics or sports, some will leave. That's not a judgment on you, it's just a mismatch.

5. Your posting volume changed a lot

A sudden jump from 3 posts a day to 30 can feel like noise in someone's feed. Going silent for weeks can make people forget why they followed. Both can lead to quiet unfollows.

6. A tone shift or a heated post

One angry thread or a pile-on reply can push away people who liked your calmer side. Based on what I've seen, drops tied to one post usually happen within a day or two of it.

7. It's a display glitch

Sometimes the count is just stale or cached. If the number jumps around between the app and the web, check why your Twitter follower count isn't updating before you assume people left.

How do you tell which cause it is?

Match the timing and size of the drop to the causes. A slow, steady loss is normal churn. A sudden drop with no change in your posting points to an X cleanup. A drop right after a specific post or a topic change points to your content.

What should you do about losing followers?

Focus on the losses you can influence and ignore the rest. You can't stop X's cleanups or follow-churn accounts, and you shouldn't try. You can keep your topic clear, your rhythm steady and your tone consistent.

  • Don't chase the people who left. Wondering exactly who it was is natural. Our guide on seeing who unfollowed you on X explains what's possible and what isn't, but naming them rarely changes much.
  • Re-center your topic. Look at your last 20 posts. Would a new visitor know what you're about?
  • Check engagement, not just followers. If replies and reposts are also falling, see how to recover declining Twitter engagement.
  • Keep earning new followers. Growth beats loss. The fastest fix for a falling count is a few weeks of strong replies and useful posts.

The bottom line on losing followers

Here's the good news: most follower losses aren't a verdict on your work. Many are spam cleanups, abandoned accounts and follow-back traders leaving, which actually leaves you with more real followers who read your work.

Watch your net trend weekly, act only when a drop lines up with something you changed, and keep putting out posts your best followers would miss. Do that, and the occasional dip becomes background noise instead of a worry.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does X notify you when someone unfollows you?

No, X does not notify you when someone unfollows you. X's Following FAQs state that X does not send a notification when you unfollow someone, and the same applies the other way. The only built-in check is visiting a profile to see whether the "Follows you" label still appears next to their name.

Why did I lose a lot of followers overnight on X?

A big overnight follower drop on X is usually caused by X removing locked or spam accounts from follower counts. X's Following FAQs confirm that locked accounts are removed from counts globally. If you did not post anything unusual that day, a platform cleanup is the most likely explanation.

Do inactive followers count on X?

Yes, inactive followers still count on X as long as their accounts are not locked or deactivated. Someone who simply stops logging in stays in your count. Accounts that X locks, or that owners deactivate, drop off your count, which is why it can fall even when nobody chose to unfollow.

Can posting too much make people unfollow you on Twitter?

Yes, posting too much on Twitter (X) can lead to unfollows when the volume buries people's feeds or quality drops. X sets daily posting limits, but those are technical caps to stop spam, not goals to aim for. Keep a steady rhythm of posts your followers would actually want.

Is losing followers bad for reach on X?

Losing a few followers is not bad for reach on X by itself. X's ranking focuses on predicted engagement, like replies, reposts and profile clicks. Losing spam or follow-back accounts can even help, because the followers who remain are the ones who actually read and react to your posts.
