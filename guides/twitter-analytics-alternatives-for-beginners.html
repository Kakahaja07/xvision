Twitter Analytics Alternatives for Beginners (Free and Simple)

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 5 min read

What are the best Twitter analytics alternatives for beginners?

The best Twitter analytics alternatives for beginners are X's free single-post stats, a simple weekly spreadsheet, Buffer's free Insights, Metricool's free plan, and free browser tools such as an engagement rate calculator. Together they cover views, engagement and follower growth without paying for X's account analytics, which needs a paid plan in 2026.

When you're new on X, the analytics page can feel like a locked door. You click it, and instead of charts you get an upgrade screen. It's easy to think you're missing out on something important. Here's the good news: at the start, you're not missing much at all.

Beginners on X (formerly Twitter) need answers to three simple questions. Are people seeing my posts? Are they reacting? Am I growing? You can answer all three for free. Paid dashboards become useful later, once you post often enough for patterns to show.

Why look for alternatives to X's analytics?

X's full account analytics costs money. It has been paid-only since mid-2024, according to reports, and in October 2026 the X Subscription pricing page lists "Advanced Analytics" from its Plus plan up. Free options cover the basics well.

The X Subscription pricing page and xAI's docs put Plus at $8 a month (as of October 2026). That's a fair price once you're serious. But when you have 50 followers and three posts a week, the full dashboard mostly shows small numbers. The free routes below teach you the same habits for nothing.

If you do pay later, the walkthrough in how to use Twitter analytics in 2026 shows where everything lives.

What can you see on X for free?

You can see a public view count, likes, replies and reposts on every post, and you can open basic stats on your own posts at no cost. That's enough to spot your best posts.

X's view counts help page explains that view counts show the total times a post was viewed, and that anyone can see them. Repeat views can count more than once, and your own views count too. So treat views as a rough guide, not an exact headcount.

On mobile, tap the small graph icon under one of your posts to see its stats. This part is actually really easy, and it's the best place for a beginner to start.

Is a spreadsheet a good alternative?

Yes. A simple spreadsheet is the most useful free alternative, because it keeps your history forever. Ten minutes a week is enough.

Make a sheet with these columns: date, post link, topic, views, likes, replies, reposts, and engagement rate. Add a second tab with your follower count each Monday. Engagement rate is just (likes + replies + reposts) ÷ views × 100. If you'd like the formulas done for you, follow how to build a Twitter analytics dashboard.

Not a spreadsheet person? Our free engagement rate calculator does the math for one post at a time, right in your browser. For the reasoning behind the formula, see how to calculate Twitter engagement rate.

Which free tools are easiest for beginners?

Buffer and Metricool are two of the easiest free tools for beginners, and both work across several networks. Each has limits on X that are worth knowing upfront.

  • Buffer: its Insights help page says the free plan covers the last 7 days, last 30 days and month to date. Exports and custom date ranges are paid. Its views chart is hidden for X, so you'll see engagement more than reach.
  • Metricool: its homepage says you can use it "for free, forever" with one brand. Its help center notes that full X metrics come with its Advanced and Custom plans, so the free level shows less.
  • XVision: I built this free Chrome extension. It shows your daily follower gains over 7 and 30 days, your best day and your viral hits, and rates each post against your usual views. It doesn't export data or show X's official impressions, and it needs no sign-in.

One more note. Social Blade used to be a common free pick for follower history, but its Twitter page now says support for the platform has been discontinued (checked October 2026). Older guides that recommend it are out of date.

For more advanced tools once you outgrow these, see the best X analytics tools for creators.

What should a beginner actually track?

Track three numbers each week: total views on your posts, your average engagement rate, and your follower count. Then note your best post and why you think it worked.

That's the whole system. Based on what I've seen, beginners who track too much stop tracking at all. Three numbers and one note is a habit you can keep. You've got this.

Those numbers only matter if they feed your posting. Pair them with a plan from Twitter growth strategy for beginners. If you want a fresh look at your profile too, try a free Twitter profile analyzer.

When does paying for analytics make sense?

Paying makes sense once you post several times a week, have a few months of data, and want official account-wide numbers like profile visits and new followers per period. Until then, free tools teach you more per minute.

A good sign you're ready: your spreadsheet shows clear patterns, and you want to dig into why. At that point, X's paid analytics or a tool like Typefully or Metricool's higher plans will add real depth.

Start free, learn fast

You don't need a paid dashboard to learn what works on X. Free post stats, a simple spreadsheet and one or two free tools cover everything a beginner needs. Track three numbers a week, study your best post, and keep going. By the time you're ready to pay for Twitter analytics, you'll know exactly what you want from it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a free version of Twitter analytics?

There is no free version of X's full account analytics in 2026, since the dashboard requires a paid plan. Free alternatives still exist: public view counts and single-post stats on X, Buffer's free Insights, Metricool's free plan, and a weekly spreadsheet log. Together they cover views, engagement and follower growth.

Can I see impressions on X for free?

Yes, every post on X shows a public view count that anyone can see, which works like impressions. X's help center says repeat views can count more than once and the author's own views count too. Account-wide impression totals over time need X's paid analytics or your own spreadsheet log.

Is Social Blade still available for X?

No, Social Blade no longer supports X. When checked in October 2026, its Twitter page said the company had decided to discontinue support for the platform. Beginners who want follower history can log their count weekly in a spreadsheet or use a tool that tracks followers over time.

What is the easiest free analytics tool for X?

The easiest free option for X is the stats screen on each of your own posts, opened by tapping the graph icon. It needs no setup. For a wider view, Buffer's free Insights covers up to 30 days, while a simple spreadsheet keeps your full history at no cost.

How much does X's analytics cost?

X's account analytics comes with a paid plan. As of October 2026, the X Subscription lists Advanced Analytics from its Plus plan, which xAI prices at $8 a month. Older X Premium tiers continue for existing subscribers, but no official page confirms which legacy tiers include analytics.
