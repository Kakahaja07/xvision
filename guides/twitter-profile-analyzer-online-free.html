Twitter Profile Analyzer Online Free: What to Check and Which Tools Help

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Is there a free Twitter profile analyzer online?

Yes, free Twitter profile analyzers exist online. SuperX's X Profile Audit gives any public account a health score out of 100 with no signup, and browser extensions can show follower counts and account age inside the X feed. A manual 10-minute check of bio, ratio, posting rhythm and replies works just as well.

Most people look for a profile analyzer after a frustrating week. Posts get a few views, follows stall, and you start to wonder if something is wrong with the account itself.

Here's the good news: a profile check almost always turns up something fixable. And you don't need to pay for it. Let me walk you through what a free analyzer can tell you, which tools help, and how to read the results without overreacting.

What can a free profile analyzer actually measure?

A free profile analyzer can only measure what is public on X (formerly Twitter): follower and following counts, account age, post count, bio, recent posts and their visible engagement. It cannot see your impressions history, profile visits or audience details. Those live in X's paid analytics.

That limit matters. When an online tool gives you a score, it is an estimate built from public signals. SuperX says plainly that its audit is "for entertainment purposes only". Treat any score as a prompt to look closer, not a verdict.

Which free Twitter profile analyzers can you use?

You can use three kinds of free analyzers: web audits, browser extensions and a manual checklist. Each one shines in a different situation, and using two together gives the clearest picture.

Web audits

SuperX's X Profile Audit analyzes any public account and returns a health score out of 100. Its page says it needs no signup or payment, and it looks at follower ratio, engagement rate and posting consistency. It is handy for checking a competitor too.

Browser extensions

Extensions analyze profiles while you browse. I built a free one called XVision. Hover the small signal ring next to any name and you see followers, following, follow-back ratio, account age, post count and whether they follow you. It also flags inactive and low-value accounts. Everything stays in your browser, and it never posts or follows for you.

The manual checklist

Your own eyes are the most accurate analyzer, because you know your goals. The checklist below takes about 10 minutes and costs nothing.

8 signals to check on any X profile

The eight signals that matter most are bio clarity, profile photo and header, pinned post, follower-to-following ratio, account age, posting rhythm, reply activity and engagement on recent posts. Go through them in order.

  1. Bio clarity. Can a stranger tell who you help and what you post in 5 seconds? The bio has a 160-character limit, per X's profile help page.
  2. Photo and header. X recommends 400x400 pixels for the profile photo and 1500x500 for the header. A blurry or missing image looks inactive.
  3. Pinned post. It is the first post visitors see. Pin your best example of what the bio promises.
  4. Follower-to-following ratio. Following thousands while few follow back can look like follow-for-follow behavior.
  5. Account age. New accounts need more proof. A clear bio and pinned post carry extra weight here.
  6. Posting rhythm. Long gaps followed by bursts make it hard for anyone to know what to expect.
  7. Replies. Do you reply to others in your niche? A profile with zero replies looks like a broadcast channel.
  8. Engagement on recent posts. Compare your last 10 posts with each other, not with famous accounts.

How do you read the results without overreacting?

Read profile analyzer results as a list of fixes ranked by effort. Change the cheap things first, such as bio, pinned post and photo, then work on habits like posting rhythm and replies. Re-check after two to four weeks, not two days.

This is where it gets interesting. Most "bad" scores come from one or two signals, not the whole profile. A low engagement score might simply mean your last few posts were links with no context. A low ratio score might mean you followed a lot of accounts early on. Neither is a disaster.

What can't a free analyzer tell you?

A free analyzer can't tell you how many people visit your profile, where your impressions come from, or whether X has limited a post. Those answers need X's own tools, which are mostly paid, or X's post labels.

Account analytics on X became paid only in June 2024, according to Social Media Today, and xAI now lists Advanced Analytics on the Plus plan and above. If profile visits are your main question, our guide on how to turn profile visits into followers covers what to do with them.

Start with one fix today

A free Twitter profile analyzer online is a great starting point, as long as you treat the score as a guide. Run one audit, go through the eight signals, and fix the easiest item today, probably your bio or pinned post. If you need help rewriting the bio, try our free Twitter bio generator AI prompt. Small, steady fixes add up faster than you think.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a profile analyzer tell if my X account is shadowbanned?

No, a free profile analyzer cannot confirm whether an X account has limited visibility. SuperX notes that its audit does not check shadowban status. X says it does not shadow ban, but it does limit the reach of some posts and can add labels telling authors when a post's visibility is limited.

What is a good follower-to-following ratio on X?

X does not publish a good follower-to-following ratio. A ratio near or above 1 usually looks healthy, while following far more accounts than follow back can look like follow-for-follow behavior. X also limits following to 400 accounts per day, so mass following was never a sound plan.

Are free Twitter profile analyzers safe to use?

Free Twitter profile analyzers are generally safe when they only read public data and never ask for your X password. Avoid tools that request permission to post, follow or send messages for you. Browser extensions should state what data they collect, and the best ones keep data inside your own browser.

How often should I analyze my X profile?

Analyzing an X profile once a month is enough for most accounts. Profile changes take time to show results, and X notes that profile changes can take a few days to appear in search. Run a quick check after any big change, such as a new bio, niche or pinned post.

Can I analyze someone else's Twitter profile for free?

Yes, any public X profile can be analyzed for free. Web audits such as SuperX's X Profile Audit accept any public username, and browser extensions show follower counts and account age while browsing. Protected accounts are private, so outside tools cannot see their posts or engagement.
