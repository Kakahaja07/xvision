How to Track Twitter Follower Growth on X

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you track Twitter follower growth?

To track Twitter follower growth, record your follower count at the same time each day or week, then work out net change and growth rate. X's paid account analytics shows new followers for a chosen date range, and a simple spreadsheet or tracking tool fills the gaps. Weekly numbers are steadier than daily ones.

Here's a pattern a lot of people know. You check your profile in the morning and see 1,204 followers. That evening it says 1,198. Did a post go wrong? Is your account in trouble? Most of the time, nothing happened at all. You just looked too closely at a number that moves up and down every day.

Tracking follower growth on X (formerly Twitter) is about seeing the trend under that noise. When you log the right numbers on a steady schedule, you can tell whether your account is really growing, how fast, and which posts are pulling people in. You can absolutely do this, and it takes about two minutes a week.

Which follower numbers should you track?

Track three numbers: your total follower count, your net change for the period, and your growth rate as a percentage. If your tool shows them, add followers gained and followers lost as two separate numbers.

  • Total followers: the count on your profile, taken at the same time each week.
  • Net change: this week's total minus last week's total.
  • Growth rate: net change divided by last week's total, times 100. This lets a 1,000-follower account and a 50,000-follower account compare progress fairly.
  • Gained vs lost: net change hides a lot. Gaining 60 and losing 50 feels very different from gaining 10 and losing 0.

Let me explain why the percentage matters. Ten new followers is huge at 200 followers and tiny at 20,000. The growth rate shows real momentum at any size.

How do you calculate follower growth rate?

Follower growth rate is (new total minus old total) divided by the old total, times 100. Run it weekly, and you'll see a trend within a month.

Where can you see follower growth on X?

X shows new followers inside its account analytics, which needs a paid plan: legacy X Premium or, since October 2026, The X Subscription. Single-post stats also show how many people followed you from that post. For free history, you'll need your own log or a third-party tool.

Sprout Social's 2026 guide to X analytics says the account overview includes new followers, with date ranges of 7 days, 28 days, 3 months or a year. On mobile, tapping the graph icon on one of your posts shows that post's new followers and profile visits.

X's Post Activity Dashboard help page defines "Follows" as the "total number of times someone began following you directly from your post." That's the most direct link between a post and growth that X offers. Full steps for the analytics page are in how to use Twitter analytics in 2026.

A quick warning if you used older tools. Social Blade was a popular free way to see follower history, but its old Twitter page now shows a notice that it has discontinued support for the platform, linking to Social Blade's announcement on X (checked October 2026).

Which tools can track follower growth for you?

Several tools log your follower count automatically, so you don't have to. They differ in how often they update, what they cost and whether they split gains from losses.

A few examples I checked on their own sites in October 2026:

  • Typefully: its analytics help page says the follower count refreshes every 45 minutes and that its analytics support X accounts only.
  • Metricool: its help center lists followers gained and lost for X, with full X metrics on its Advanced and Custom plans.

How often should you check your follower count?

Check your follower count once a week for decisions, and no more than once a day for curiosity. Daily numbers swing too much to act on.

Based on what I've seen, daily checking mostly creates worry. Weekly totals smooth out the noise from a few unfollows or a quiet weekend. Pick a fixed time, like Monday morning, and log the number then. Consistency matters more than precision here.

What should you do when growth stalls or drops?

Don't panic. Look at your last two weeks of posts, compare them with a strong week, and change one thing. A short dip is normal, and a steady decline usually has a clear cause.

If you're losing more than you gain week after week, the guide on why you're losing followers on Twitter covers the common causes. If the number simply won't move at all, even after a good post, check Twitter follower count not updating. And once you have a few months of data, analyzing your Twitter audience growth helps you understand who those new followers are.

Small weekly numbers, big clear picture

Tracking Twitter follower growth doesn't need fancy software. Log your total each week, work out net change and growth rate, and note what you posted. After a month, you'll know your real pace. After three months, you'll know what drives it. That's a great position to grow from.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I track followers daily or weekly?

Weekly tracking works best for most X accounts. Daily follower counts swing with a few unfollows or a quiet day, which makes trends hard to read. Log your total at the same time each week, such as Monday morning, and calculate net change and growth rate from those weekly snapshots.

What is the difference between gross and net follower growth?

Gross follower growth counts every new follower you gained in a period. Net follower growth is gained followers minus lost followers. An account that gains 60 and loses 50 has a net growth of 10. Tools such as Metricool show both numbers, while your profile count only shows the net result.

Does X analytics show follower growth for free?

No, X's account analytics, which includes new followers over time, has required a paid X plan since mid-2024, according to Social Media Today and later guides. Free users can still see new followers on single posts and can log their profile count by hand in a spreadsheet each week.

How do I know which post brought me new followers?

X shows follows per post in its post-level stats. The Post Activity Dashboard counts "Follows" as people who followed you directly from a post, and the mobile post stats show new followers too. Compare those numbers across posts to see which topics and formats turn readers into followers.

Can I track another account's follower growth?

Yes, another account's follower growth can be tracked by logging its public follower count at the same time each week. X does not share other people's analytics, so the count on their profile is the only reliable source. A spreadsheet with one row per week is enough to see their trend.
