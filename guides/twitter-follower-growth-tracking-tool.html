Twitter Follower Growth Tracking Tool: 5 Honest Options Compared

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

What is the best Twitter follower growth tracking tool?

The best Twitter follower growth tracking tool depends on your budget and how much detail you want. X's own analytics show new followers over 7 days to a year on paid plans. A free spreadsheet works if you log your count weekly. Browser extensions chart daily gains, and API-based apps add history but need account access.

Most people start tracking followers the same way: they glance at the number on their profile and try to remember what it was last week. That works until the day you want to know whether a thread actually moved the needle.

A follower growth tracking tool fixes that by recording your count over time, so you can see good days, bad days and the trend in between. The great news is that there's an option for every budget, including several that cost nothing.

This guide compares the tools. If you want the step-by-step method for reading the numbers once you have them, see how to track Twitter follower growth.

What should a follower tracking tool show you?

A good follower tracking tool shows your follower count by day, your net change over a week and a month, and ideally which days or posts brought the biggest jumps. Anything beyond that is a bonus.

Three questions decide which option fits you:

  • How much history do you need? A week, a month, or a year?
  • How much effort will you put in? Automatic, or a two-minute weekly habit?
  • What access will you give? Some tools need permission to read your account.

Option 1: X's built-in analytics

X's built-in analytics are the most official option, and they shine for anyone already paying for X. According to Sprout Social's guide to X analytics, the account dashboard at x.com/i/account_analytics shows new followers, profile visits, impressions and engagement over the past 7 days, 28 days, 3 months or a year.

Access is the catch. X hasn't published a current help page that says exactly which plans include account analytics. The X Subscription pricing page lists "Advanced Analytics" as an X feature of the Plus plan and above, as of October 2026. Older Premium tiers still exist for current subscribers, so check your own account.

Best for: people who already pay for X and want official numbers next to their impressions.

Option 2: X's Professional Home

X's Professional Accounts help page says switching to a Professional Account is free and describes a Professional Home with account analytics over the past 28 days. That makes it worth a look if you don't pay for X.

I'd treat this one with care. I couldn't confirm in October 2026 how much follower data Professional Home shows to accounts without a paid plan, and X's help pages don't always match the app. Switch, look, and decide for yourself.

Best for: businesses and creators who want to try a free, official view first.

Option 3: A simple spreadsheet

A spreadsheet is the most underrated follower tracking tool. It's free, private, and it keeps your history forever, as long as you remember to update it.

Set up four columns: date, follower count, posts that day, and notes. Write down your count at the same time each day or each week. In the notes column, record anything unusual, like "thread went wide" or "posted about a new topic." After a month, a simple line chart tells you a lot.

Best for: anyone who wants total control and doesn't mind a two-minute habit.

Option 4: A browser extension

A browser extension can track your follower growth automatically while you use X, without a separate account or API connection. I built one, XVision, so I'll be clear about what it does and doesn't do.

It's a free Chrome extension. Its growth dashboard charts your daily follower gains over 7 and 30 days, highlights your best day, and rates each of your posts against your usual views. It needs no sign-in and keeps the data in your browser. It doesn't export analytics, doesn't show X's official impressions, and doesn't list who unfollowed you.

Best for: people who want a free daily view on desktop without granting account access.

Option 5: Third-party apps using the X API

Third-party analytics apps pull your data through the X API and store it on their servers, which lets them keep long histories and send reports. They're powerful for teams and agencies.

Two things to check before you sign up. First, cost. The X API now uses pay-per-use pricing, and the official X API pricing page lists reading a user profile at $0.010 and reading followers or following at $0.010 per account returned, as of October 2026. Tracking just your count is cheap. Tracking your full follower list for a large account is not, which shapes what apps can offer for free. Second, permissions. A tracker only needs read access. If an app asks to post or follow for you, think twice.

I haven't listed specific apps or prices here, because both change often. Check each app's own site for current pricing. For a wider survey, see the best X analytics tools for creators.

Best for: teams, agencies and anyone who needs long history and shareable reports.

Which follower tracking tool should you pick?

Pick the tool you'll actually look at every week. A free spreadsheet you update beats an expensive dashboard you never open.

  • Just starting out? A spreadsheet or a free extension is plenty.
  • Already paying for X? Use X's analytics, then add notes in a sheet.
  • Running a brand or client accounts? An API-based app with reports may be worth the cost.

Whatever you choose, look at the trend weekly rather than daily. A single bad day often comes from X removing locked accounts, which we explain in why you're losing followers on Twitter. And if you want names rather than numbers, seeing who unfollowed you on X covers what's possible.

The takeaway

You genuinely can't go wrong with any of these options, as long as you use one consistently. Start free, watch your weekly trend for a month, and upgrade only if you find yourself wanting more history or reports. The data you collect will quickly show you which posts and habits bring people in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a free Twitter follower tracker?

Yes, there are free ways to track Twitter (X) follower growth. A spreadsheet where you log your count daily or weekly costs nothing. Free browser extensions can chart daily follower gains on desktop. X's free Professional Accounts also describe a 28-day analytics view, though its follower data is unconfirmed.

Can I see my follower growth history on X?

X's built-in account analytics show new followers over 7 days, 28 days, 3 months or a year, according to Sprout Social, and they generally require a paid X plan. X does not show a free long-term follower history on profiles, so a spreadsheet or tool is needed for that.

How often should I check my follower growth?

Checking follower growth once a week is enough for most X accounts. Daily numbers jump around because of normal unfollows and X's removal of locked accounts. A weekly net change, compared over a month or two, shows the real trend and which posts or habits actually helped.

Are follower tracking apps safe to connect to X?

Follower tracking apps on X are generally safe when they use X's official sign-in and ask only for read access. Avoid any app that asks for your password or wants to post, follow or send messages for you. Review connected apps in the Apps and sessions section of your settings.

Can a follower tracking tool show who unfollowed me?

Only tools that save copies of your full follower list can show who unfollowed you on X, and only from the day they start saving. X itself sends no unfollow notifications. Reading full follower lists through the X API costs $0.010 per account as of October 2026, so many tools limit this.
