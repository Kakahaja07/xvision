Twitter Subscriptions for Creators Explained (2026 Guide)

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

What are Twitter subscriptions for creators?

Twitter subscriptions for creators, now called Creator Subscriptions on X, let followers pay a monthly fee for exclusive posts, badges and closer access. As of October 2026, creators need 2,000 verified followers and 5 million organic impressions in 3 months to apply, and X says they can earn up to about 97% of gross revenue.

You've probably seen a small "Subscribe" button on some creators' profiles and wondered what's behind it. What I find interesting is how different it is from X's other payout program. It doesn't pay for views at all. It pays for loyalty.

Creator Subscriptions is a membership. Your biggest fans pay you every month, and in return they get things regular followers don't. It matters because it's recurring income that comes from people who chose to support you, not from an algorithm deciding who sees your posts.

How do Creator Subscriptions work on X?

Creator Subscriptions work like a monthly membership. After you're approved, anyone can subscribe to you for a monthly fee to unlock bonus content and closer access.

According to X's About Creator Subscriptions page, your earnings depend on how many subscribers you have and the price you choose, minus platform fees from Apple or Google. Subscriptions can be bought on iOS, Android and the web.

What do subscribers get?

Subscribers get access to content and conversations that non-subscribers can't fully see. As of October 2026, X lists these features:

  • Subscriber-only posts: posts only your subscribers can read in full.
  • Subscriber badge: a public badge next to their name when they reply to you.
  • Subscriber-only replies: you can limit replies on a public post to subscribers.
  • Subscriber-only Spaces: live audio conversations only subscribers can join.
  • Subscription link: a link to your subscription page you can share on and off X.
  • Subscribers tab: on iOS, a tab in your notifications for subscriber activity.

Non-subscribers see previews of your subscriber posts on your profile and in the Home Timeline. To read the full post, they have to subscribe. That preview is a quiet advert for your membership every time you post.

Who can offer subscriptions on X?

Creators who meet X's eligibility rules can apply through Creator Studio. As of October 2026, the main thresholds are 2,000 verified followers and 5 million organic impressions in the last 3 months.

X's Subscriptions policy adds more requirements. You need to be 18 or older, have a complete profile (name, bio, photo and header), a verified email, two-factor authentication, an X Premium subscription, a post in the last 30 days and completed identity verification. If you're eligible but not picked right away, X says it adds you to a waitlist. My guide to X creator monetization requirements compares these rules with Original Content Rewards.

How is the subscription price set?

You set your own monthly price by choosing one of the price points X makes available. You can change it later in Creator Studio.

X says most price change requests are reviewed and approved within 1 to 2 business days. I'd start at a price that feels easy for your most engaged followers. It's easier to add perks over time than to win back people who felt the first price was too high.

How much of the money do creators keep?

Creators can earn up to about 97% of gross subscription revenue, according to X. X says it does not take a revenue share itself.

The deductions come from third-party payment processing and app store fees, plus cancellations and refunds. So the exact share you keep can vary.

This isn't fully consistent across X's help pages, so it's worth knowing both versions. X's older Creator Dashboard page says creators get up to 97% of revenue after in-app purchase fees until they reach $50,000 in lifetime earnings, then up to 90%. Its example is a $5.00 subscription bought on iOS: Apple's 30% fee takes $1.50, X keeps a minimum of $0.10, and the creator gets up to $3.40. Check your own terms in Creator Studio before you plan around a number.

When do subscription payouts arrive?

Payouts arrive about 60 days after the end of the month in which X received the subscription revenue. You also need at least $50 in earnings before X pays out.

X gives an example: revenue from March is typically paid within the first two weeks of May. If you're under $50, the balance rolls over to the next month. Once a payout is sent, X says funds usually reach your bank in 3 to 5 business days, sometimes up to 10.

  • U.S. creators: get paid through X Money.
  • Creators outside the U.S.: connect a Stripe payout account and verify their identity.
  • U.S. taxes: X sends an IRS Form 1099 if you earned more than $600 in the previous tax year.

Subscriptions vs Original Content Rewards

Subscriptions pay you for loyal fans, while Original Content Rewards pays you for qualified views. You can think of one as a membership and the other as a share of attention.

What should you post for subscribers?

Post things that feel closer and more personal than your public feed. X itself suggests unscripted thoughts, asking subscribers for ideas and opinions, and personal replies only subscribers can see.

Here's what I'd add. Give subscribers a reason to show up each week, like a behind-the-scenes update or a monthly live Space. Make them feel like insiders, not customers. Subscriptions work best when they feel like a community, which is why my guide on building a community on X is worth a read before you launch.

Is Creator Subscriptions worth it?

Creator Subscriptions is worth it if you already have a core group of fans who want more of you. If most of your followers are casual readers, a service or a digital product sold on X may earn more.

Keep the rules in mind. X says purchases are generally final and non-refundable unless the law requires otherwise. If you deactivate your offering, X notifies your subscribers, and you'd have to qualify and set it up again to restart. And like all X programs, rules can change. If you're not at 2,000 verified followers yet, other ways to make money on X can start much sooner.

The bottom line on X subscriptions

Twitter subscriptions for creators turn your most loyal followers into steady monthly supporters. The bar to join is high, but the revenue share is generous and the perks are simple to run. If people already reply to everything you post, you may be closer to a paid community than you think.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many followers do you need for subscriptions on X?

X requires at least 2,000 verified followers to offer Creator Subscriptions, as of October 2026. Verified followers are followers who pay for X Premium, so your total follower count isn't what matters. You also need 5 million organic impressions in the last 3 months and a post in the past 30 days.

How much does X take from creator subscriptions?

X's Subscriptions page says X takes no revenue share and creators can earn up to about 97%, with deductions for app store and payment fees. An older X help page says up to 97% after app store fees until $50,000 in lifetime earnings, then up to 90%. X's help pages disagree, as of October 2026.

Can non-subscribers see subscriber-only posts?

No, non-subscribers can't see subscriber-only posts in full. X shows them a preview on your profile and in the Home Timeline. If a subscriber quotes or reposts one of those posts, non-subscribers still only see the preview. They need to subscribe to unlock the full content.

Can subscribers get a refund on X?

Usually no, because X says Creator Subscriptions purchases are final and non-refundable, unless the law requires a refund. If Apple or Google issues a refund for an app store purchase, X adjusts the creator's earnings. Successful chargebacks also reduce a creator's estimated earnings for that period.

Can I offer subscriptions without X Premium?

No, X's Subscriptions policy requires creators to be subscribed to X Premium, as of October 2026. X says anyone can subscribe to a creator. Creators also need identity verification, two-factor authentication, a verified email address and a complete profile with a bio, photo and header image.
