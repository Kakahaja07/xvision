How to Get More Reposts on Twitter (X)

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you get more reposts on Twitter?

To get more reposts on Twitter, post things people want their own followers to see: a useful checklist, a clear one-line take, original data, or a short summary of breaking news with context. Make each post stand on its own, credit sources, and share it when your audience is online. Never ask for "RT if" reposts.

Think about the last thing you reposted. Chances are it made you look smart, helpful or funny in front of your own followers. That's the whole secret in one sentence. People don't repost to help you. They repost because sharing your post says something good about them.

A repost (once called a retweet) shares someone's post with your followers. A quote post does the same but adds your own comment on top. On X (formerly Twitter), reposts matter because they carry your post into audiences that don't follow you yet, which is how small accounts get discovered.

Why do people repost on X?

People repost on X when a post is useful, true, funny or says something they wish they'd said. A repost is a small public endorsement, so people share what fits the image they want to show.

That shapes everything. A post that asks for help rarely gets reposted, because sharing it does nothing for the person who shares. A post that teaches something in ten seconds gets reposted a lot, because sharing it makes the sharer look generous.

X's help page on how to Repost also notes something interesting. If someone tries to repost an article link they haven't opened, X may show a prompt asking if they want to read it first. So the post itself, not just the headline, needs to earn the share.

What kinds of posts get the most reposts?

The posts that get the most reposts are save-worthy, quotable or newsworthy. They give value fast and make sense without any extra context.

How to get more reposts on Twitter, step by step

To get more reposts on Twitter, make your posts easy to share: clear, complete, credited and well-timed. Each step below removes one reason someone might hold back.

1. Make it stand on its own

When your post lands in a stranger's feed, they won't know your backstory. If the post needs three earlier posts to make sense, it won't travel. Write each post so a cold reader gets the point.

2. Lead with the payoff

Put the useful part first. "How to write a cold email that gets a reply" is weaker than "Cold emails get more replies when the first line is about them, not you." Our guide on writing engaging Twitter posts covers first lines in detail.

3. Make it quotable

Short, sharp lines get shared. If one sentence in your post could stand alone as a quote, put it first. People love to quote-post a line and add "This."

4. Share original work

A chart you made, a lesson from your own project, or a list you built from experience can't be found anywhere else. That makes it worth passing on. Just be honest about where the data comes from.

5. Credit your sources

If you share someone else's idea, tag them or link them. It's the right thing to do, and the original author may repost you, which puts your post in front of their audience.

6. Turn long ideas into threads

Threads that teach something useful are often reposted from the first post. Make that first post strong enough to share alone. The guide on writing viral Twitter threads goes deeper.

7. Repost your own best work

X's help center says you can repost your own older posts when they become relevant again. If a post did well a month ago, bring it back when the topic comes up. New followers haven't seen it.

Do reposts help reach as much as replies?

Reposts spread your post to new people, but in X's ranking code a plain repost counts for less than a reply. As of October 2026, X's weight file gives a repost 1.0, a reply 5.0 and a quote post 5.0.

Those numbers come from X's current ranking weights in the xai-org/x-algorithm repo, and they change often, so don't read too much into exact values. One weight stands out for sharing: "share via copy link" carries 20.0, the highest positive weight in the file. Posts people want to pass on to a friend are clearly valued. In the 2023 release, a repost was also 1.0 and a reply was 13.5, so the pattern has held. The way I see it, reposts and replies do different jobs. Reposts carry your post to new audiences. What to avoid

Avoid "RT if you agree," repost-for-repost groups and posts copied from others. They may add a few shares but they hurt trust and can break X's rules.

X's authenticity policy bans "coordinating to exchange engagement," which covers groups that agree to repost each other. And "RT if" style posts are exactly the kind of engagement bait many people filter out. XVision, the free extension I built, collapses posts like that by default. You don't need them. Useful posts get shared on their own.

Give people something worth passing on

Getting more reposts on Twitter comes down to one question: would someone feel good sharing this with their followers? If the answer is yes, you've done the hard part.

Start with one checklist or one sharp take this week, make it stand on its own, and post it when your audience is around. Track which posts get shared, then make more of that kind. Small shares turn into bigger ones over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a repost and a quote post?

A repost on X shares someone's post with your followers exactly as it is. A quote post shares it with your own comment, and you can add media like photos or a GIF. Replies to a quote post don't automatically include the original author unless you mention their username.

Is it okay to ask people to repost?

Yes, an occasional honest request is fine on X, such as "Please share this if you know someone hiring." Asking on every post feels like bait, though. X's authenticity policy bans coordinating to exchange engagement, so avoid repost-for-repost groups and keep any requests rare, specific and genuine.

Can you repost your own tweet on X?

Yes, X lets you repost your own older posts. X's help center says this is useful when an older post becomes relevant again, or when you want all your followers to see one of your replies. Reposting a strong post weeks later reaches followers who missed it the first time.

Do quote posts count as reposts?

Quote posts sit in the same Repost menu on X, but they are a separate type of post with your own comment added. A quote post starts a new conversation under your name, and replies to it don't include the original author automatically. A plain repost simply shares the original.

Why do some posts get likes but no reposts?

Posts often get likes but no reposts on X when they are personal or need context. A like says "I agree," while a repost says "my followers should see this." Personal updates, inside jokes and questions get likes. Useful lists, clear takes and original data get shared more often.
