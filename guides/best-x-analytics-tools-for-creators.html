Best X Analytics Tools for Creators in 2026

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

What are the best X analytics tools for creators?

The best X analytics tools for creators in 2026 are X's own account analytics for official impressions, Typefully or Tweet Hunter for growth-focused creators, Metricool or Buffer for free multi-network stats, and Sprout Social or Hootsuite for teams. Pick based on budget, how many networks you post on, and whether you need CSV exports.

There's a real joy in the first time a chart explains something you couldn't see before. You notice that every thread you write brings followers, or that your Tuesday posts quietly outperform the rest. The right analytics tool makes those moments happen every week instead of by accident.

The catch is choice. Dozens of tools promise X (formerly Twitter) analytics, and many look alike. So I kept this list to tools whose features I checked on their own websites in October 2026. Each one shines for a different kind of creator. Where I couldn't confirm a detail, like a price, I left it out.

Is X's own analytics the best place to start?

Yes, if you can pay for it. X's account analytics is the main source of the official impressions, profile visits and engagement rate for your account. Every other tool works from data X shares with it.

The dashboard sits at x.com/i/account_analytics and has required a paid plan since mid-2024, according to reports. Since October 12, 2026, X Premium has been part of The X Subscription. The X Subscription pricing page lists "Advanced Analytics" from the Plus plan up, and xAI's docs put Plus at $8 a month (as of October 2026). Setup steps are in how to use Twitter analytics in 2026.

If you want official numbers and post mainly on X, this is hard to beat. The trade-off is that it's X only, and its date ranges stop at one year.

Which tools are best for growth-focused creators?

Typefully and Tweet Hunter stand out for creators who live on X. Both pair writing tools with analytics built around posts and followers.

Typefully explains its numbers more clearly than most. Its analytics help page covers impressions, engagements, engagement rate and a profile conversion rate (new followers divided by profile visits). It refreshes your follower count every 45 minutes, and any chart can be downloaded as a CSV. Its analytics support X accounts only, even though you can write for several networks.

Tweet Hunter leans hard into growth. Its homepage lists daily follower growth, impressions, clicks and profile visits, plus a way to identify the tweets that brought the most followers, engagement and reach. It offers a 7-day free trial. If finding your best posts is the main job, this one is a strong fit.

Which tools are best if you post on several networks?

Metricool and Buffer are great picks for creators who post beyond X. Both have free options, which makes them easy to try.

Metricool says on its homepage that you can use it "for free, forever" with one brand. Its X metrics help page lists followers gained and lost, impressions, engagement and a post list. Full X metrics need its Advanced or Custom plan, so the free and Starter levels show less.

Buffer includes Insights on its free plan, covering the last 7 days, last 30 days and month to date. Exports and custom date ranges are on paid plans. One honest limit: Buffer's help center says its views chart is hidden for X, because X only shares follower counts at the channel level. It's great for a simple cross-network view, less so for deep X reach analysis.

Which tools are best for teams and brands?

Sprout Social and Hootsuite are built for teams that need reports. They cost more, but they shine at presentation-ready reporting and comparing accounts.

Sprout Social lists a profiles report covering follower growth, content performance and engagement across any number of X profiles, a comparison report against competitors, and PDF or CSV export on its X analytics features page. It offers a 30-day free trial.

Hootsuite lists X among its supported networks, with top-performing posts, best times to post, and report exports in .csv, .xlsx, .pdf and .ppt. If you manage a brand plus a personal account, it keeps everything in one place.

My pick for most creators

If you post mainly on X and want growth, start with X's own analytics for official numbers and add Typefully or Tweet Hunter if you want deeper post insights. If you're on several networks, Metricool's free plan is a great first step. Teams will be happiest with Sprout Social or Hootsuite. Whatever you choose, the best X analytics tool is the one you'll open every week. Pick one, try it for a month, and let the data guide your next post.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is X's built-in analytics enough for most creators?

X's built-in analytics is enough for many creators who post only on X, because it has the official impressions, engagement rate, profile visits and new followers. It needs a paid plan, and its date ranges stop at one year. Creators on several networks, or who want long history, usually add another tool.

Which X analytics tools are free?

Free X analytics options in October 2026 include Metricool's free plan, Buffer's free Insights covering up to 30 days, and single-post stats on X itself, which every author can open. Tweet Hunter and Sprout Social offer free trials of 7 and 30 days. X's own account dashboard requires a paid plan.

Do third-party tools show the same numbers as X analytics?

No, third-party tools often show slightly different numbers from X analytics. They read data through X's API, which limits private metrics such as link clicks to posts from the last 30 days. Buffer, for example, hides its views chart for X. Small gaps between tools are normal and not a sign of errors.

Which tool is best for tracking follower growth?

For follower growth, Typefully refreshes follower counts every 45 minutes, Tweet Hunter lists daily follower growth, and Metricool splits followers gained from followers lost. A weekly spreadsheet log is the free fallback. The best choice depends on whether you also need scheduling or multi-network reports.

Do I need more than one analytics tool?

Most creators need only one main analytics tool plus a simple spreadsheet for long-term history. A second tool makes sense when one covers a gap, such as CSV exports, competitor comparisons or other networks. Using several tools at once often creates conflicting numbers and slows down weekly reviews.
