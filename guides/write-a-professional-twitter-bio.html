How to Write a Professional Twitter Bio (6 Simple Steps)

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you write a professional Twitter bio?

To write a professional Twitter bio, state your role and specialty, add one proof point like years of experience, say what you post about, and end with a next step. Keep it under X's 160-character limit and in plain words. Example: "B2B email marketer for software companies. 8 years in SaaS."

Picture a very good accountant whose bio says: "Numbers person. Tea. Thoughts are mine." She wants freelance clients from X. But nothing on her profile says she does taxes for freelancers, so the right people scroll past.

Now picture the same person with one changed line: "Freelance accountant for UK creators. I explain tax deadlines in plain English." Same posts, same skills. Suddenly the profile does its job.

A professional bio on X (formerly Twitter) is your short pitch. It tells clients, recruiters, peers and journalists what you do and why they should trust you. You don't need fancy words to write one. You need the right order, and this part is actually really easy once you see it.

What makes a Twitter bio "professional"?

A Twitter bio is professional when a stranger can tell your job, your specialty and your credibility in one read. Professional doesn't mean stiff. It means clear, accurate and focused on the reader.

X gives every account 160 characters for the bio, according to X's profile help page. That's tight, so a professional bio cuts anything that doesn't help someone decide to follow, hire or contact you.

How to write a professional Twitter bio, step by step

Write a professional Twitter bio in six steps: pick your reader, name your role, add proof, say what you post, add a next step, then trim. Each step takes a few minutes, and you can do them all in one sitting.

Step 1: Pick the one reader you most want

Decide who matters most: clients, employers, peers or press. A bio that tries to speak to all of them speaks to none. If you're a freelance designer, your reader is probably a small business owner, not other designers.

Step 2: Name your role and specialty

Start with what you do, in words your reader uses. "Email marketer for software companies" beats "Growth ninja." Add the niche if you have one, since a specialty is easier to remember than a general title.

Step 3: Add one proof point

Give the reader a reason to trust you. Good proof points are years of experience, a notable employer, a certification or a result you can stand behind. Use only one or two. A list of every credential reads like a CV, and the bio isn't the place for that.

Step 4: Say what you post about

This is the step most professionals skip. People follow for what they'll see in their feed. "I share subject lines and tests that worked" gives a clear reason to follow, even for people who will never hire you.

Step 5: Add a next step

Tell the right reader what to do. "DMs open for new clients," "Newsletter below" or "Open to analyst roles" all work. Put the actual link in the separate website field so the bio stays readable.

Step 6: Trim it and read it out loud

Cut filler like "passionate about" and "lover of." Then read the bio out loud. If it sounds like something you'd say when meeting someone at a work event, it's ready.

Should you add "views my own" to a professional bio?

Adding "views my own" is a personal or employer choice, not an X requirement. Some companies ask staff to include it. If yours doesn't, you can skip it and save about 13 characters.

If you work in law, finance or health, a short note like "Not legal advice" can set expectations. Check your employer's or industry's social media rules before you post, since they may require specific wording.

How do the name and photo support a professional bio?

Your name and photo make the bio believable. Use your real name, or the name clients know you by, and a clear headshot. X allows a display name of up to 50 characters, so you can add a short role tag if it helps.

X's username help page notes that you can update your display name at any time, separate from your @username. That makes it easy to try "Priya Shah | Tax for Freelancers" for a month and see how it feels.

Your bio is your handshake, so keep it clear

Here's the good news: a professional Twitter bio is one of the fastest wins on X. Six steps, one sitting, and every visitor now knows what you do. You can edit it whenever your role changes, so it never has to be perfect on day one.

Write it for one reader, back it with one proof point, and tell people what you'll share. Then let your posts build the rest of your personal brand on X. You've got this.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I not put in a professional Twitter bio?

A professional Twitter bio should not include vague traits like "passionate" or "coffee lover," long lists of titles, or claims you cannot back up. Avoid confidential employer details and too many hashtags. With only 160 characters, every word should help a reader understand your role, your proof or what you post.

Should a professional Twitter bio be written in first or third person?

A professional Twitter bio usually reads best in first person or as short phrases, like "I share tax tips" or "Email marketer for SaaS." Third person, such as "Jane is a marketer," can feel distant on X. Pick one style and keep it consistent across the whole 160-character bio.

Do I need to use my real name on a professional X account?

No, X does not require you to display your real identity, according to its authenticity policy. For a professional account, though, a real name or a known professional name builds trust with clients and employers. Fake identities used to deceive others are against X's rules, so keep your name honest.

Should I put my job title or my employer in my Twitter bio?

Put your job title or specialty first, because it tells readers what you do. Add your employer only if it builds trust and your company is comfortable with it. Many professionals write "Email marketer at [Company]" and add "views my own" when their employer's social media policy asks for it.

How often should I update a professional Twitter bio?

Update a professional Twitter (X) bio whenever your role, employer, specialty or main offer changes, and review it every few months. Old details like a past job or a finished project confuse readers. Small edits are free and take about a minute from the Edit profile screen.
