How to find your best time to post on X

Why generic "best time" charts mislead, and a simple method to find the hours your own audience is listening.

You have probably seen charts claiming the best time to post on X is a certain weekday morning. They are averages across millions of accounts in every time zone and niche. Your audience is not average. Here is how to find your own best time.

Why timing matters

Most of a post's views arrive in its first few hours. If you post when your audience is asleep or busy, those hours are wasted, and by the time people are online the post has been pushed down by newer ones.

Step 1: Look at your niche, not the world

The best signal is how posts in your own feed perform by the hour they went out. If posts from accounts like yours reach furthest around mid-morning, that is when your audience is reading.

Measure reach fairly: compare a post's views to its author's follower count. A post with 5,000 views from an account with 1,000 followers did far better than one with 10,000 views from an account with 100,000.

XVision: the Timing tab does this automatically. It samples posts in your feed, scores each one's reach against its author's followers, and shows your best three-hour window and best day.

Step 2: Test your own posts

For two weeks, post at different times: some in the morning, some at lunch, some in the evening. Keep the type of post similar so timing is the main difference.

Then compare each post's views to your usual. Patterns show up quickly.

XVision: once you have enough posts, the Timing tab also shows the hour your own posts have done best.

Step 3: Mind your audience's time zone

If most of your followers live in a different time zone, your best time may be late at night or early in the morning for you. Many tools, including X's own scheduler, let you schedule posts so you don't have to be awake.

What matters more than timing

Timing is a multiplier, not a fix. A strong post at an average time beats a weak post at the perfect time. Get the content right first, then use timing to give it the best start.

  • Post when your audience is online.
  • Stay around for the first 15 minutes to reply to comments. Early conversation helps the post spread.
  • Don't post twice in a row. Give each post its own window.
