How to Improve Content Relevance on X So the Right People See Your Posts

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you improve content relevance on X?

To improve content relevance on X, post consistently about a few clear topics, use the plain words your audience uses, and engage with accounts in that niche. X matches posts to viewers through engagement patterns. Its 2023 SimClusters code grouped accounts into about 145,000 communities based on who follows whom.

Picture two accounts with the same follower count. One posts about marketing on Monday, football on Tuesday and crypto on Wednesday. The other posts about email marketing almost every day. The second account is far easier for X to place in front of the right people.

That's what relevance really means on X. It isn't a quality score for your writing. It's how well the system can match your posts to people who care. And here's the encouraging part: you control most of the signals that make that match easy.

What does content relevance mean to the X algorithm?

To the X algorithm, relevance is the predicted fit between one post and one viewer. The ranking model guesses how likely each viewer is to reply, like, share or skip a post, and it bases those guesses mostly on what that viewer engaged with recently.

The 2026 xai-org README calls the viewer's recent engagement history "the main input to the model." Its retrieval step turns each viewer and each post into a list of numbers, called an embedding, and returns posts that sit closest to the viewer. Close numbers mean similar interests.

X's own Help Center page on timelines describes it in plainer terms: For You shows posts from accounts and Topics you follow plus recommended posts, chosen using signals like popularity and how people in your network interact with them.

So relevance is built from two sides. Your posts need a clear shape, and the people who engage with them need to look alike.

How do SimClusters decide which community you belong to?

SimClusters groups accounts into communities based on the follow graph, then describes posts by which communities engage with them. A post liked mostly by one community gets pushed toward more people in it.

According to the 2023 SimClusters README, the system found around 145,000 communities. Its "Known For" data covered the top 20 million producers, with each producer tied to at most one community. Viewers get "InterestedIn" profiles that capture long-term interests, and a post's profile updates each time it gets a like.

This is genuinely fascinating, and it has a practical lesson. Who engages with your post shapes where it goes next. If your early likes come from the right community, the post travels inside it. The 2026 README still lists SimClusters as a source of out-of-network posts, though the 2023 numbers may have changed.

Does X read the words in your posts?

Partly, but X hasn't explained how much words matter for ranking. The 2026 README describes Grox, a system that runs when posts are published, creates numeric representations of post text and images, and classifies things like spam and adult content.

The README also says Grox prompts are withheld from the public code. In October 2025, Elon Musk posted that "Grok will literally read every post and watch every video" to match people with content, as Social Media Today reported. That's an executive's stated goal, not a documented ranking rule. X hasn't confirmed how text understanding feeds into today's scores.

My practical read: write clearly enough that a person, or a model, can tell your topic in one glance. Vague posts are hard to match no matter how the matching works.

Step by step: how to improve content relevance on X

These steps make your posts easier to match. They're simple, and once you do them for a few weeks, they become second nature.

  1. Pick two or three content pillars. Choose topics you can post about for months. Everything else becomes an occasional extra, not the main menu.
  2. Use your audience's words. Say "cold email reply rate," not "outreach magic." Plain, specific terms help people and systems recognize your topic.
  3. Engage inside your niche. Reply to and follow accounts in your topic. Your interactions and theirs shape which community you're linked to.
  4. Win the right first engagers. Post when your niche is active. Early likes from the right people point your post toward their community.
  5. Skip off-topic viral bait. A random trending post may get views from people who will never engage again, and it muddies your signal.
  6. Keep hashtags light. X's Help Center recommends no more than 2 hashtags per post. Clear wording does more than a pile of tags.

If you're unsure who your niche even is, my guide on finding your target audience on Twitter helps you define it. For daily post ideas inside your pillars, see what to post on X every day.

How can you tell if your relevance is improving?

Your relevance is improving when more of your engagement comes from people in your niche, including people who don't follow you yet. Replies from strangers who clearly share your interest are the best sign.

Look at who replies, who follows, and whether new followers post about the same topics you do. If your new followers are random, your topic signal is probably blurry. Relevance also helps beyond the feed. The same mutual-follow and interaction patterns feed account suggestions, which I cover in how X recommends accounts to follow. And a focused account is easier to grow into a recognizable name, as my guide on building a personal brand on X explains.

What this means for you

For you, improving content relevance on X means being easy to recognize. The algorithm doesn't need you to be famous. It needs a clear pattern: consistent topics, consistent words, and an audience that looks alike. Build that pattern for a month and the matching starts working in your favor, which is the foundation for reaching the X For You page. Pick your pillars today. That one decision makes every post after it easier.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does posting about many topics hurt reach on X?

Posting about many unrelated topics can make matching harder on X. The 2026 ranking model relies mostly on each viewer's engagement history, and SimClusters ties each producer to at most one community in the 2023 code. A focused account sends clearer signals about who should see it.

Do Topics on X affect who sees my posts?

Topics affect what viewers see. X's Help Center says For You includes posts from accounts and Topics a person follows, plus recommended posts. X hasn't explained exactly how a post gets linked to a Topic, so clear wording about your subject is the safest approach.

Can I change my niche on X without losing reach?

You can change niches on X, but expect a dip while the system learns the new pattern. Your current audience engaged with your old topic. Moving gradually, by blending the new topic into existing posts for a few weeks, usually feels smoother for followers.

Do replies to big accounts improve my relevance?

Thoughtful replies in your niche can improve relevance on X. They connect you with people who share your interests, and interactions between accounts feed X's matching. In the 2023 code, Real Graph predicted how likely one user is to interact with another based on likes, reposts and follows.

How long does it take for X to learn my niche?

X hasn't published a timeline for learning an account's niche. The 2026 For You code reads recent engagement history and only ranks posts from the last 48 hours, so changes can show quickly. A steady pattern over several weeks gives the clearest signal.
