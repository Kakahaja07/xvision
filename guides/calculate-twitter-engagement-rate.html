How to Calculate Twitter Engagement Rate (Formulas and Examples)

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you calculate Twitter engagement rate?

To calculate Twitter engagement rate, divide a post's total engagements by its impressions and multiply by 100. A post with 40 engagements and 2,000 impressions has a 2% engagement rate. X uses this per-impression formula in its own dashboards, while benchmark reports such as Rival IQ's divide by follower count instead.

You've probably seen it. One account gets 300 likes and looks like a winner. Another gets 30 likes and looks like it's struggling. Then you notice the first one has 200,000 followers and the second has 900. Raw likes hide the real story. Engagement rate shows it.

Engagement rate is the share of people who did something after seeing your post. On X (formerly Twitter), that something can be a like, reply, repost or click. It lets you compare posts fairly, even when one got far more views than another. And the math is simpler than it sounds.

What is the Twitter engagement rate formula?

The main Twitter engagement rate formula is engagements divided by impressions, times 100. Two other versions divide by followers or by reach. Each answers a slightly different question, so pick one and stick with it.

X's own help center defines it plainly. In its Export CSV Definitions page, engagement rate is "the number of engagements divided by total impressions." Hootsuite's engagement rate guide lists the other common versions. Here are the three you'll actually use:

  • Per impression (X's method): engagements ÷ impressions × 100. Best for judging how well a post landed with the people who saw it.
  • Per follower: engagements ÷ followers × 100. Useful for comparing accounts of different sizes, and the method behind most industry benchmarks.
  • Per reach: engagements ÷ reach × 100. Reach means unique people, not total views. Most X tools report impressions instead, so this one is less practical on X.

Let me explain the key words. An impression is one time your post was shown on someone's screen. On X, the public view count under a post is the same idea. X's view counts help page notes that repeat views can count more than once, and your own views count too. An engagement is any action on the post. The difference between the two is covered in Twitter impressions vs engagement explained.

How does the math work on a real example?

Take one post, add up its engagements, then divide by impressions or followers. The example below uses made-up numbers so you can follow each step.

Say your post has 2,000 impressions and you have 1,600 followers. It got 24 likes, 6 replies and 4 reposts. X's dashboard also counted 20 clicks you can't see publicly, such as profile clicks, link clicks and taps to open the post.

FormulaMathResult
Public interactions per impression(24 + 6 + 4) ÷ 2,000 × 1001.7%
All engagements per impression (X's way)(24 + 6 + 4 + 20) ÷ 2,000 × 1002.7%
Public interactions per follower(24 + 6 + 4) ÷ 1,600 × 1002.1%

Same post, three different answers. None of them is wrong. That's why you should always say which formula you used when you share a number, and only compare numbers built the same way.

Why is my number different from X's analytics?

Your hand-calculated number is usually lower than X's because X counts clicks you can't see. Its definition of engagements includes clicks anywhere on the post, while you can only count public likes, replies and reposts.

According to X's ads help center, engagements include "reposts, replies, follows, likes, links, hashtags, embedded media, username, profile photo, and/or post expansions." Post expansions alone can be a big share. So if X shows 2.7% and you get 1.7%, nothing is broken. You're just counting a smaller set of actions.

My advice: if you have access to X analytics, use its engagement rate for your own posts. Use the public-interactions version when you compare yourself with other accounts, since that's the only data you can see for them.

How do you calculate engagement rate for a whole account?

For a whole account, add up all engagements for the period and divide by all impressions for the same period. Don't average each post's rate, because small posts would count as much as big ones.

Here's a quick example with two posts:

  • Post A: 100 engagements on 10,000 impressions (1%).
  • Post B: 30 engagements on 500 impressions (6%).

Averaging the two rates gives 3.5%. But the true account rate is 130 ÷ 10,500 × 100, which is about 1.2%. The second number is the honest one. It reflects what really happened across every impression.

For a daily or weekly view, keep the same rule. Total engagements for the week divided by total impressions for the week. If you want to track this over time, a simple sheet does the job. The guide on how to build a Twitter analytics dashboard has the exact formulas.

What is a good engagement rate on X?

There's no official "good" number from X. Per follower, brand accounts are low: the Rival IQ 2026 benchmark report found a median of 0.03% per post on X in 2025, up from 0.015% in 2024.

Those figures come from company accounts and use the per-follower formula. Personal accounts that talk with their audience often do far better. Per impression, the rate will look higher because impressions and followers are different sizes.

Based on what I've seen, the most useful benchmark is your own median. If your typical post sits at 1.5% per impression and one hits 4%, that's the post to study. What is the fastest way to calculate it?

The fastest way is a calculator or one spreadsheet formula. Enter views, likes, replies and reposts, and you get the rate in seconds.

Our free engagement rate calculator takes a post's views, likes, replies and reposts, plus an optional follower count. It shows the engagement rate, reach compared with followers, and how much of the activity came from replies. It runs in your browser, so nothing you type leaves the page.

In Google Sheets, put impressions in A2, likes in B2, replies in C2 and reposts in D2. Then use:

=IFERROR((B2+C2+D2)/A2, 0)

Format the cell as a percent. The IFERROR part stops a divide-by-zero error on a brand-new post. For more free options, see the roundup of free Twitter engagement calculators.

One number, used the same way every time

Calculating Twitter engagement rate is simple: engagements divided by impressions, times 100. The hard part is consistency. Pick one formula, write down which one it is, and compare your posts against your own typical result. Do that for a few weeks and the rate stops being a vanity number. It becomes a clear signal of what your audience wants more of.

Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as an engagement on X?

An engagement on X is any interaction with a post. X's ads help center lists reposts, replies, follows, likes, link clicks, hashtag clicks, media clicks, username and profile photo clicks, and post expansions. Public counts cover only replies, reposts, quotes, likes and bookmarks, so outside tools usually count fewer engagements than X does.

Should engagement rate use views or followers?

Engagement rate should use views (impressions) when judging single posts, because it shows how people who actually saw the post reacted. Use followers when comparing accounts of different sizes, as benchmark reports from Rival IQ do. Both are valid, but never compare a per-view number with a per-follower number.

Do bookmarks count toward engagement rate?

Bookmarks are not in the engagement list on X's Post Activity Dashboard help page, which names reposts, replies, follows, likes and clicks. Some tools add bookmarks to their own totals. If you include bookmarks in your formula, label it clearly and use the same rule for every post you compare.

Can I calculate engagement rate for someone else's posts?

Yes, engagement rate can be calculated for any public post. X's API documentation treats impressions, likes, reposts and replies as public metrics, and the same counts appear under each post. Add likes, replies and reposts, divide by views, and multiply by 100. Hidden clicks will be missing from the total.

Why does my engagement rate drop when a post goes viral?

Engagement rate often drops on a viral post because impressions grow faster than engagements. Followers who know the account tend to engage more than strangers reached through the For You feed. It's a common pattern, not a rule. A lower rate on a much bigger audience can still mean more total engagement and new followers.
