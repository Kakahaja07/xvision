How to Grow a Twitter Account From Zero: A Stage-by-Stage Roadmap

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you grow a Twitter account from zero?

To grow a Twitter (X) account from zero, work in four stages. Set up a clear profile and niche, earn your first 100 followers through useful replies, reach 1,000 with a steady posting rhythm, then build simple systems that keep growth going. Each stage builds on the last, so skipping ahead rarely works.

Every big account on X started where you are now. Zero followers, a fresh profile, and posts that seem to vanish the moment you hit send. The people who made it past that point didn't find a secret. They just knew which job to focus on at each stage.

Here's something that might help your mood. In a 2019 study, Pew Research Center found that the bottom 90% of US adult Twitter users had a median of just 19 followers. The data is old, but the point still holds. A focused account with a few hundred real followers is already ahead of most people on the platform.

Why does growing from zero feel so slow?

Growing from zero feels slow because X shows posts to your followers first, and you don't have any yet. Posts shown to strangers are scored at a discount. So in the early weeks, your own posts have very little to stand on.

There is a small bright spot. X's open-source 2026 ranking code, in the xai-org x-algorithm repository, includes a "new-author boost" that lifts posts from authors whose impressions are below a threshold. X hasn't said what that threshold is. It's a nudge, not a plan, so this roadmap doesn't depend on it.

How to grow a Twitter account from zero: the four stages

The roadmap has four stages, and each one has a single main job. The time estimates are my suggestions for a part-time effort, not rules from X.

Stage 1: How should you set up a new account before posting?

Set up a new account by choosing one narrow niche, then making your photo, bio and pinned post say who you are and why to follow. Do this before you chase any growth. Every good reply you write later sends people to this page.

  • Niche: write down three topics you could post about for a year. Everything you share should fit one of them. If you're not sure who you're talking to, start with finding your target audience on Twitter.
  • Photo and name: a clear face or simple logo, and a name you'll keep.
  • Bio: one line on what you do, one line on what people get by following.
  • Pinned post: a short intro or your best idea. It acts as your landing page, so it's worth learning how to create a good pinned post.

Stage 2: How do you get from 0 to 100 followers?

You get from 0 to 100 followers mostly through replies, not posts. A thoughtful reply under a fresh post from a bigger account in your niche reaches people who already care about your topic. Some of them click your profile, and a good profile turns those clicks into follows.

Pick 20 to 30 accounts that are a few steps ahead of you. Reply early, while the thread is small, and add something real: a story, a number, a counterpoint. The step-by-step version of this stage lives in the guide to getting your first 100 followers on X.

This part is actually really easy to start. You don't need a single follower to write a good reply.

Stage 3: How do you grow from 100 to 1,000 followers?

From 100 to 1,000 followers, you keep replying but start posting on a steady rhythm, about one or two posts a day. Now there are real people who see your posts, and each post gives a new visitor more reasons to stay.

Good formats for this stage are lessons with a specific detail, progress updates with real numbers, clear opinions with a reason, and honest questions. If you run dry, keep a list of things to post on X every day. The milestone math for this stretch is in how to get 1,000 followers on Twitter.

Reply to everyone who replies to you. Those people become your first small community, and their engagement helps your next post reach further.

Stage 4: What keeps an account growing after 1,000?

After 1,000 followers, growth comes from systems: a weekly review of what worked, more of your best formats, and deeper relationships with people in your niche. You stop guessing and start repeating patterns you can see in your own numbers.

Once a week, list your three best and three worst posts. Look for the topic, format or time they share. I built a free extension, XVision, partly for this. Its dashboard rates each of your posts against your usual views and shows daily follower gains over 7 and 30 days, so the pattern is easy to spot.

What should you avoid at every stage?

At every stage, avoid mass following, follow-and-unfollow loops and any service that promises followers. X's page about following on X caps follows at 400 per day and bans "using or promoting third-party services or apps that claim to add followers". Aggressive following can get an account locked or limited.

The bigger problem is quieter. Followers who don't care about your topic never engage. That makes every future post look less interesting to the ranking system, which is the opposite of what you want.

What this means for you

Growing a Twitter account from zero is less about talent and more about doing the right job at the right time. Setup first, then replies, then rhythm, then systems. Most people stall because they post into silence at stage 2 and decide X doesn't work.

Based on what I've seen, the people who stick with replies for the first month are the ones who get a real foothold. If you want a simpler framework to follow each day, the Twitter growth strategy for beginners breaks it into three habits.

Start at stage 1 today

You don't need to plan all four stages at once. Pick your niche, fix your profile this afternoon, and write five good replies tomorrow. That's how to grow a Twitter account from zero: one small, steady step at a time. Once you've done it for a week, it becomes second nature.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you still grow a Twitter account from zero in 2026?

Yes, you can still grow a Twitter (X) account from zero in 2026. X's open-source ranking code predicts actions such as replies, profile clicks and follows, and it includes a boost for authors with low impressions. New accounts that reply usefully in one niche can earn their first followers without ads.

Should a new account follow a lot of people?

No, a new account should follow people it genuinely wants to hear from, not chase numbers. X limits every account to 400 follows per day and bans follow churn, which is following and unfollowing in bulk to inflate a follower count. Following 50 to 100 relevant accounts gives you a useful feed to reply to.

How many times a day should a new Twitter account post?

A new Twitter (X) account can post one or two times a day and spend more time on replies. Under about 100 followers, few people see your posts, while a good reply can reach a bigger account's audience. Raise your posting rate once people start replying to what you share.

Is it better to start a new account or fix an old one?

Fixing an old account is usually better if it has real followers in your current niche and no rule violations. Starting fresh makes sense when the old followers came from a different topic or from follow-for-follow tactics, because uninterested followers don't engage and can drag down how your new posts perform.

What should your first post on X be?

Your first post on X should be a short introduction that says who you are, what you will post about, and who it helps. Pin it to your profile. A specific line such as "I share weekly lessons from building a bakery website" gives visitors a clear reason to follow.
