Twitter Follower Count Not Updating? Why It Happens and What to Do

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Why is my Twitter follower count not updating?

A Twitter follower count usually looks stuck or wrong because X removes locked accounts from follower counts, hides suspected spam followers, and runs spam cleanups that shift totals. New followers can also be canceled out by people leaving. X's help center says a follower who vanishes was likely hidden for spam, unfollowed you, or deactivated.

You get a notification: "Sam followed you." You check your profile and the number hasn't moved. Or it went down by three overnight, even though nobody seems to have left.

Don't worry, this is one of the most common little mysteries on X (formerly Twitter). Most of the time, your count is doing exactly what X designed it to do. Here's what's happening behind the number, and how to check it calmly.

Why does X remove some followers from your count?

X removes locked accounts from follower counts everywhere on the platform. If an account that follows you gets locked, your displayed number can drop even though that person never unfollowed you.

X's Following FAQs say this directly: when accounts are locked, X removes them from follower counts across profiles globally. Its enforcement page adds that an account locked pending a challenge, like confirming a phone number, is also removed from Reposts and Likes.

Here's the interesting part. X ties the removal to the lock itself, so a follower who passes the check should normally count again. X doesn't spell out the timing, but a number that dips and recovers over a few days often fits this pattern.

Why did a new follower disappear from my list?

A new follower who later disappears was most likely hidden for spam, unfollowed you, or deactivated. X's help center lists exactly those three reasons.

According to X's page on common following issues, you can see your full follower list, including suspected spam accounts, by turning off the quality filter in your notification settings. If the missing follower shows up after that, X simply filtered them as likely spam.

This is good news, honestly. Spam followers don't read your posts or engage, so losing them from view costs you nothing real.

Can a spam cleanup change my follower count?

Yes. When X removes large numbers of spam or fake accounts, follower counts across the platform drop. Accounts that attracted many bots notice it most.

In April 2024, X's Safety team announced a large effort to remove accounts breaking its spam and platform manipulation rules. As MediaNama reported, X warned that users "may observe changes in follower counts" as a result. X doesn't announce every cleanup, so a sudden small drop across many accounts at once often points to one.

Is the number just slow to refresh?

Sometimes the count you see is simply a moment behind. X hasn't published how often follower counts refresh, so a short lag between a notification and the number isn't unusual.

Before you worry, try the basics:

  1. Pull down to refresh your profile, or reload the page on the web.
  2. Close the app fully and open it again.
  3. Check your profile on another device or in a browser.
  4. Wait an hour and look again.

If the web and the app show different numbers, update the app. If they match, the number is current, and the cause is one of the reasons above.

Are you gaining and losing followers at the same time?

Very often, yes. A count that seems frozen can hide steady movement in both directions. Five new followers and five lost ones look exactly like nothing happening.

This is where daily tracking helps. X's notifications show new followers, but X doesn't notify you when someone leaves. Could a problem with your own account freeze growth?

Rarely, but it's worth ruling out. If your account is limited, X can filter it out of search results and notifications, which means fewer people find you and fewer follow.

X's help page on locked and limited accounts explains that limited accounts may be filtered out of certain places. You'd normally see a message about it when you log in. A Twitter follower count not updating is rarely a real problem

Based on what I've seen, a follower count that won't update is almost never a sign that something is wrong with your account. It's usually X cleaning up locked and spam accounts, which leaves you with a more accurate number. Focus on the people who actually read and reply. Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my follower count go down when nobody unfollowed me?

A follower count can go down without unfollows because X removes locked accounts from follower counts across all profiles, according to its Following FAQs. Spam cleanups and accounts hidden for spam activity also lower the number. X ties that removal to the lock, so a follower who passes the check should normally count again.

How do I see all my followers, including spam accounts?

To see all followers on X, including suspected spam accounts, turn off the quality filter in your notification settings. X's help page on common following issues recommends this step. Followers that X hid as likely spam will then appear in your full list.

Does X tell you when someone unfollows you?

No, X doesn't send a notification when someone unfollows you. It does notify you about new followers. To spot losses, compare your follower count from day to day, or use a tracking tool that records daily changes in your follower numbers over time.

Why do bigger accounts lose followers in batches?

Bigger accounts tend to lose followers in batches because they attract more spam and bot followers. When X runs a cleanup, like the April 2024 effort its Safety team announced, many of those accounts are removed at once. X warned users that follower counts could change as a result.

Is my follower count wrong if it differs on the app and the web?

A follower count that differs between the X app and the web is usually just a refresh delay. Reload the page, close and reopen the app, or update it. X hasn't published how often counts refresh, so small gaps that settle within an hour are normal.
