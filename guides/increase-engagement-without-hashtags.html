How to Increase Engagement Without Hashtags on X

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 5 min read

How do you increase engagement without hashtags?

To increase engagement without hashtags, write the keywords people search for in plain words, stick to a clear topic, open with a strong first line, and spend time replying to others in your niche. X's own help center recommends no more than 2 hashtags per post, so going without them costs very little.

Open any growth guide from a few years ago and you'll see advice like "add 5 to 10 trending hashtags." Then look at the posts people actually share on X today. Many have none. The best ones read like a person talking, not a list of tags.

A hashtag is a word or phrase starting with # that groups posts by topic and makes them easier to find in X search. Engagement means the likes, replies, reposts and clicks a post earns. The good news is that on X (formerly Twitter), the second doesn't depend much on the first.

Do hashtags still matter on X?

Hashtags still work on X, but they matter less than most people think. X's help center describes them as a way to categorize posts and help them show up in search, and it recommends using no more than two.

Here's what X's help page How to use hashtags says, in short:

  • Hashtags categorize posts by keyword and help them appear more easily in X search.
  • Tapping a hashtag shows other posts that use it.
  • Spaces and punctuation break a hashtag.
  • X recommends "no more than 2 hashtags per post as best practice," though you can use more.

That's a modest role. Hashtags are a search label, not an engagement booster. And too many can backfire. X's authenticity policy lists "posting with excessive, unrelated hashtags" and using trending hashtags to hijack a conversation as content spam.

How to increase engagement without hashtags: 7 things that work

You can increase engagement without hashtags by doing what hashtags were supposed to do (help the right people find you) in better ways. These seven habits cover it.

1. Use plain keywords in your text

This one makes a bigger difference than most people realize. If you write about "cold email," say "cold email" in the post. X search matches words, not just tags. A post that says "Here's how I write cold emails that get replies" can be found by people searching those words. If your posts don't show up at all, see why tweets don't appear in search.

2. Stick to two or three topics

Hashtags try to tell X what your post is about. A consistent topic does that job better. When your posts keep returning to the same few themes, both readers and ranking systems get a clearer picture. The guide on improving content relevance on X explains why this matters.

3. Write a first line that stops the scroll

A hashtag can't save a slow opening. Lead with the result, the surprise or the point. Our guide on how to write engaging Twitter posts has before-and-after examples.

4. Reply to others in your niche

Replies under bigger posts put you in front of people who already care about your topic. That's targeting no hashtag can match. A good reply is seen by everyone reading that thread.

5. End with a real question

A question with an easy, personal answer brings replies. "What's your go-to tool for this?" beats "#productivity #tools." More ideas in how to get more replies on X.

6. Credit and mention people

If you build on someone's idea, tag them. It's fair, and it can bring the original author into the conversation. A mention reaches a real person. A hashtag reaches a search page.

7. Post when your audience is online

Timing does more for early engagement than tags. Post when your followers are active so the first likes and replies arrive quickly.

When is a hashtag still worth using?

A hashtag is still worth using when people actively follow or search that exact tag, such as an event, a conference, a live sports match or a community tag like #buildinpublic. In those cases, one or two relevant tags help.

What this means for you

Here's the honest takeaway: you can drop hashtags from most of your posts and lose almost nothing. Put that effort into clear words, a steady topic, strong openings and good replies instead.

Try it for two weeks. Post without hashtags, use plain keywords, and reply every day. Then compare your numbers with your usual weeks. Want more ways to spread a post once it's out? See how to get more reposts on Twitter. You may find that your posts read better without the clutter.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many hashtags should I use on X?

X's help center recommends using no more than 2 hashtags per post as best practice. You can use more, but stacking many tags makes posts look like spam, and X's authenticity policy lists excessive, unrelated hashtags as content spam. For most posts, zero or one relevant hashtag is plenty.

Do hashtags reduce engagement on X?

X hasn't confirmed that hashtags reduce engagement. What is clear is that excessive or unrelated hashtags count as spam under X's authenticity policy, and long tag lists make posts harder to read. One or two relevant hashtags are unlikely to hurt, while a wall of tags often looks low effort.

Can people find my posts without hashtags?

Yes, people can find your posts without hashtags. X search matches the words in your post, so writing the terms people search for, like "cold email tips," helps them find you. Your posts can also reach new people through replies, reposts and recommendations, none of which need a hashtag.

Should I use trending hashtags to get more views?

No, using trending hashtags that don't relate to your post is a bad idea on X. The authenticity policy lists using trending or popular hashtags to manipulate a conversation or drive traffic as content spam. Only use a trending tag when your post genuinely adds to that topic.

Do hashtags help small accounts grow?

Hashtags help small accounts a little, mainly when people actively follow a specific community or event tag. For most small accounts on X, daily replies in their niche, a clear topic and strong first lines do far more for growth than any hashtag, and they build real relationships too.
