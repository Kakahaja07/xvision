How to Get Followers on Twitter as a Founder

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 5 min read

How do you get followers on Twitter as a founder?

To get followers on Twitter (X) as a founder, post from your personal account about what you learn while building: real lessons, numbers you can share, and honest mistakes. Reply to the people your product serves, link your company in your bio, and keep promotion to a small share of your posts.

Many founders open X, post a launch announcement, get eleven likes from friends, and decide the platform doesn't work for them. I understand the feeling. But a launch post from an account nobody follows yet is like opening a shop on a street with no foot traffic.

The fix is to build the following before you need it. Founders actually have an advantage here: you're doing something interesting every single day, and people love watching real things get built.

This guide is about the followers part. For the wider plan, including how your account fits your company's marketing, see how to grow a founder account on X.

Who should follow a founder on X?

A founder's ideal followers fall into four groups: potential customers, other founders, future hires, and people who can spread the word, like investors, writers and partners. Customers matter most, but the others multiply your reach.

The mix you want shapes what you post. If you only write about fundraising, you'll attract other founders and investors, not customers. If you only post product updates, you'll attract nobody. Decide who comes first. Our guide on finding your target audience on Twitter helps you write that down.

The audience is there. Pew Research Center's 2026 fact sheet found 24% of U.S. adults earning $100,000 or more use X, and 22% of college graduates, both higher than the 18% average. Many buyers of business software sit in those groups.

Personal account or company account?

Use your personal account as the main growth engine. People follow people more readily than logos, and a founder's voice can share opinions and stories a brand account can't.

Keep a company account for announcements, support and product updates. Link the two: your bio names the company, and the company account mentions you. That way each new follower of one is likely to find the other.

What should founders post to get followers?

Founders get followers by posting the things only a founder can share: lessons from building, decisions and why you made them, honest numbers, and behind-the-scenes moments. That's content a stranger can't get anywhere else.

  • Lessons: "We lost our first big customer. Here are 3 things I'd do differently."
  • Decisions: "We cut our pricing page from 4 plans to 2. Here's the reasoning."
  • Numbers you're comfortable sharing: signups, revenue milestones, churn, or even just "week 12, still at zero paid users."
  • Useful how-tos: what you learned setting up something your customers also struggle with.
  • Customer stories: with permission, what a user achieved.

Sharing progress openly is often called building in public. It's one of the most reliable ways founders earn followers, and how to build in public on Twitter covers it in depth. If you're stuck for ideas, try the list of Twitter content ideas for startup founders.

A good rule of thumb, and it's my opinion rather than an X rule: keep direct promotion to roughly one post in five. Your followers will happily hear about the product when the other four posts were worth reading.

How should founders use replies?

Founders should spend more time replying than posting in the early months. A useful reply in your customers' conversations puts you in front of exactly the right people, even when you have few followers.

Search X for the problem your product solves, in the words customers use, and answer questions without pitching. Reply under posts by bigger accounts in your market. And reply to everyone who replies to you. Buffer's study of replying to comments found X posts where the creator replied saw about 8% higher engagement, though Buffer notes the result was only marginally significant. It's a small edge, but it's free.

Set up your profile so founder traffic converts

Your profile should say what you're building, for whom, and why you're worth following, in under 160 characters. Visitors from a good reply decide in seconds.

A clear founder bio might read: "Building [product], [what it does] for [who]. Sharing what I learn about [topic]." Then pin your best lesson thread, not your launch post. There are templates in the best X bio for startup founders, and the general method is in turning profile visits into followers.

Mistakes that slow founder growth

The mistakes that slow founders down most are posting only announcements, writing like a press release, and disappearing between launches. Each one is easy to fix.

  • Only announcements. Nobody follows an ad feed.
  • Corporate voice. Write like you talk to a customer on a call.
  • Going quiet. Steady posting between launches is what makes launches work.
  • Buying followers to look bigger. X's rules ban it, and investors and customers can usually tell. See growing followers without bots instead.

My take for founders

Based on what I've seen, founders who treat X as a public notebook grow faster than founders who treat it as a billboard. You're already learning something new every week. Writing it down in public costs maybe 20 minutes a day, and it builds an audience that's ready when you launch.

Start this week: one honest lesson post, five useful replies to potential customers, and a bio that says what you're building. Keep it going, and your next launch won't be talking to an empty street.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should founders post from a personal or company account on X?

Founders usually grow faster on X from a personal account, because people follow people more readily than brands. Use the personal account for lessons, opinions and progress, and keep a company account for announcements and support. Link both in each bio so followers of one can find the other.

How often should a founder post on Twitter?

A founder on Twitter (X) can grow with one or two thoughtful posts a day plus several replies. Steady activity matters more than volume. A Buffer study of more than 100,000 users found that posting consistently over many weeks was linked to much higher engagement per post, across several platforms.

Should founders share revenue numbers on X?

Founders can share revenue numbers on X if they are comfortable with it, and many find honest numbers attract strong interest. It is optional. Signups, milestones, experiments and lessons work too. Share only what your cofounders and investors agree to, and never invent or round numbers misleadingly.

How long does it take a founder to get 1,000 followers on X?

There is no standard time for a founder to reach 1,000 followers on X. It depends on your niche, how often you reply, and how useful your posts are. X publishes no benchmark, so claims like 30 days are guesses. Track your weekly net gain and work to improve it.
