How to Tell If Your Twitter Account Is Restricted on X

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How can you tell if your Twitter account is restricted?

You can tell if a Twitter account is restricted by checking for an in-app notice to verify your account or saying features are limited, a label on a post saying its visibility was limited, or a warning screen on your profile. Eligible accounts can also check X's Under the Hood report, which lists labels applied in the past month.

Your views have been low for a week, and the word "shadowban" keeps popping into your head. You search your own post and can't find it. Now you're wondering if X quietly put you in a box.

Let's slow down. Here's the good news: real restrictions on X usually leave visible signs, and most of them come with clear steps to fix. This guide shows you exactly where to look, and how to tell a restriction apart from a normal dip in reach.

What does "restricted" actually mean on X?

On X (formerly Twitter), "restricted" covers a few different actions: locking an account until you verify it, limiting features for a set time, putting an account in read-only mode, or limiting how far certain posts travel.

X's enforcement page lists the post-level options. They include excluding a post from search results, trends and recommended notifications, removing it from the For You and Following timelines, restricting it to the author's profile, and downranking it in replies. X calls this approach Freedom of Speech, Not Reach, and its 2023 blog post describes it as "visibility filtering."

Is a shadowban real on X?

It depends on what you mean. X's help center says plainly, "we don't shadow ban! Ever." The same help center also describes limiting the reach of posts that break its rules. So there's no official secret ban, but reach limits are real.

The difference matters. A "shadowban" suggests hidden punishment with no notice. X says it now labels posts whose visibility it limits, and that authors can appeal. If you've seen no notice and no label, a reach drop is more likely to be ranking than punishment. My guide on what to do when Twitter impressions suddenly dropped covers those ordinary causes.

Sign 1: You see a notice when you log in

The clearest sign of a restriction is a message from X itself. Locks and limits almost always show a notice when you open the app or log in on the web.

X's help page on locked and limited accounts describes the main ones:

  • "Please verify your account." A lock. You verify by phone, email or a reCAPTCHA check.
  • Features limited for a specified time. Often after aggressive following or engaging. You can wait out the countdown or, in some cases, verify your phone or email.
  • "We've temporarily limited some of your account features." A possible rules violation. You can browse, but only followers see your past posts, and you can't post, repost or like.

The page also says that while an account is limited, it "may be filtered out of certain places on X, including from search results and notifications."

Sign 2: A post carries a visibility label

X adds labels to some posts it has limited. According to X's enforcement page, since April 2023 these labels tell both the author and viewers that the post's visibility was limited.

Open your recent posts one by one and look under the text. If you find a label, you can appeal it from the post. One label usually affects that post only, not your whole account.

Sign 3: Your profile shows a warning to visitors

Ask a friend to open your profile. If your account is locked or limited, visitors may see a screen saying the account showed unusual activity or may have broken the X Rules, and asking if they still want to view it.

That screen is a strong signal. It also hurts growth, because many people won't click through. My guide on fixing Twitter profile visibility issues covers what to do next.

Sign 4: The Under the Hood report shows labels

X now offers a report called Under the Hood. It shows visibility-limiting labels applied to your account and posts over the prior month, and you can download the data.

In its September 2026 announcement, X Open Source said the report is available to accounts at least one year old that made 10 or more posts in the prior month. It now also shows if a post was withheld in a country after a legal demand. X's code repo still calls the tool a pilot, so access and details may change. If your account doesn't qualify yet, rely on the other signs.

Sign 5: Your posts vanish from search

A simple test: search a rare phrase from a recent post and switch to the Latest tab. If several recent posts never appear, while a friend's posts do, a limit is possible.

Be careful, though. X's search help page lists other reasons too, such as protected posts, an unconfirmed email and sensitive media settings. Rule those out with my guide on why tweets don't appear in search.

What isn't a restriction?

Some things feel like restrictions but aren't. Knowing them saves a lot of worry.

  • Hitting a daily limit. X's limits page says you'll get an error naming the limit you hit, and it resets after a waiting period. That page lists conflicting daily post figures, so trust the error message.
  • A normal drop after a viral post. Your baseline just went back to usual.
  • Third-party "shadowban checkers." They aren't connected to X's systems, and X doesn't confirm their results. I wouldn't rely on them or give them access to your account.

Also note that X has no general "account status" page in its help center that lists every restriction. Notices, post labels and Under the Hood are the official signals.

What should you do if your Twitter account is restricted?

Follow the steps in X's notice first. That usually means verifying your phone or email, deleting a flagged post, or waiting out a countdown. If you believe it's a mistake, X's help page says you can appeal through its support team.

Then look at what triggered it. X's Authenticity policy lists common causes, such as bulk replies, follow churn and repeated duplicate posts. Once you're clear, my step-by-step plan to recover Twitter account reach helps you rebuild.

Most "restrictions" turn out to be something simpler

Based on what I've seen, people suspect a restriction far more often than one actually exists. Check for notices, labels and profile warnings. If none appear, your account is very likely fine, and the fix is in your content and timing. For the ranking side, see why your posts aren't showing on X.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does X notify you when your account is restricted?

Yes, X usually shows a notice when an account is locked or its features are limited. X's help page on locked and limited accounts describes messages such as "Please verify your account" and "We've temporarily limited some of your account features." Limited posts can also carry a visibility label.

How long does a Twitter account restriction last?

A Twitter account restriction lasts for the time shown in X's notice, which varies with the reason. X's help page says limited features last for a specified time based on the violation. Locks end once you verify by phone, email or reCAPTCHA. Repeat violations may lead to suspension.

Are shadowban checker websites accurate?

Shadowban checker websites aren't official, and X doesn't confirm their results. X's own signals are in-app notices, labels on limited posts, profile warning screens and the Under the Hood report for eligible accounts. Avoid giving any checker access to your X account or password.

Can you appeal a restriction on X?

Yes, you can appeal a restriction on X. X's enforcement page says authors can appeal a label on a post with limited visibility. Its help page on locked and limited accounts says that if you think your account was limited in error, you can appeal by contacting X's support team.

Who can use X's Under the Hood report?

X's Under the Hood report is available to accounts at least one year old that made 10 or more posts in the prior month, according to X Open Source's September 2026 announcement. X's code repo still describes the tool as a pilot, so eligibility and features may change over time.
