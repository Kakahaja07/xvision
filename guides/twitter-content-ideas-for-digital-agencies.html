15 Twitter Content Ideas for Digital Agencies That Attract Clients

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

What are good Twitter content ideas for digital agencies?

Good Twitter content ideas for digital agencies include project teardowns, before-and-after results shared with client permission, process walkthroughs, quick tool tips, honest opinions on industry news and behind-the-scenes posts about the team. Aim for three to five posts a week, with most of them teaching something a client could use today.

Plenty of agency accounts on X (formerly Twitter) look the same: a logo, a few "we're hiring" posts and a link to the latest blog article. Then someone scrolls past an agency founder who breaks down a landing page in five quick points, and that's the one they remember next time they need help.

The difference isn't budget. It's content that shows how you think. Here are 15 ideas, grouped by the job they do, plus a simple weekly plan to use them.

Why should digital agencies post on X?

Digital agencies should post on X because buyers judge an agency by how it thinks, and X lets you show that thinking in public every day. Useful posts work like a portfolio that updates itself.

One tip before the list. Post from both the agency account and the founder's or lead strategist's personal account. I think people trust a person faster than a logo. Founders can borrow more ideas from the list of Twitter content ideas for startup founders, which focuses on product companies rather than service businesses.

Proof posts: show you can deliver

Proof posts show potential clients that your agency gets results. They are the most important content ideas for digital agencies, because they answer the question every buyer has: can these people do it for me?

  1. Public teardowns. Pick a public landing page, ad or email and suggest three improvements. Be kind and specific. This one makes a bigger difference than most people realize.
  2. Before and after. Show a design, page or campaign before and after your work, with the client's permission.
  3. Mini case study threads. The problem, what you changed and what happened, in four or five posts.
  4. Free mini audits. Invite followers to reply with their site, and give quick notes to the first few. It starts real conversations.

Process posts: show how you work

Process posts show how your agency works, which removes the fear of hiring an unknown team. Clients want to know what happens after they sign, and process content answers that before the first call.

  1. Your checklist. Share the checklist you use before launching a site or campaign.
  2. How you scope a project. Explain the questions you ask in a discovery call.
  3. Your tool stack. List the tools you use for one task and why.
  4. Free templates. Share a brief template, a report layout or a content calendar.

Opinion posts: show how you think

Opinion posts show your agency's point of view on news, trends and common myths. A clear, well-argued opinion is what makes people follow an agency instead of just reading one post.

  1. React to platform updates. When a platform changes, explain what it means for clients. For example, X publishes its For You ranking code in the open x-algorithm repo on GitHub, and plain-language summaries of updates make good posts.
  2. Myth-busting. Pick one belief in your field that's wrong and explain why, with evidence.
  3. Predictions. Share what you expect to change in your niche next year, and revisit it later.

Team and culture posts: show who you are

Team and culture posts show the people behind your agency. They build the human connection that makes a client choose you over a similar shop with similar skills.

  1. Behind the scenes. A whiteboard session, a messy first draft or a team retro.
  2. Lessons from mistakes. A project that went wrong and what you changed in your process afterward.

Conversation posts: get clients talking

Conversation posts invite replies, which start the relationships that later turn into projects. Polls and live audio are simple formats that work well for agencies.

  1. Polls. Ask clients a quick question about their biggest problem. According to X's help page on polls, a poll can have up to four options and run from 5 minutes to 7 days.
  2. Live audio Q&As. Host a Space where people bring questions. X's help page on Spaces says up to 13 people can speak at once, and you start a Space from the iOS or Android app.

Which content formats work best for agencies on X?

Plain text posts are a strong default for agencies on X, with images close behind. Short videos and link posts have their place, but they shouldn't be the only formats you use.

According to Buffer's 2026 content format study, text posts on X had the highest median engagement rate (3.56%), followed by images (3.40%), videos (2.96%) and link posts (2.25%). Buffer measured its own users' posts, so treat these as direction, not targets. For more on formats, see the guide on the best content formats for X growth.

One more tip: X recommends using no more than two hashtags per post. Most agency posts need none.

A simple weekly content plan for a digital agency

A simple weekly content plan for a digital agency uses one idea from each group: proof on Monday, process on Tuesday, opinion on Wednesday, team on Thursday and a conversation post on Friday. That's five posts and covers every job.

Show your thinking, and clients come to you

The best Twitter content ideas for digital agencies all do one thing: they let buyers see your skill before they ever book a call. Pick one idea from each group, post five times this week and notice which posts start conversations. Do more of those. Your next client might be reading already.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a digital agency post on X?

A digital agency should post on X about three to five times a week from the agency account, plus regular replies from team members' personal accounts. Consistency matters more than volume. A weekly plan with one proof, process, opinion, team and conversation post keeps the content varied and easy to sustain.

Should an agency post from a brand account or a personal account?

An agency should use both. The brand account shares case studies, templates and news. The founder's or lead strategist's personal account shares opinions, lessons and replies to industry conversations. A personal account lets buyers get to know a real person, while the agency account confirms the team's quality.

Can agencies share client results on X?

Agencies can share client results on X only with the client's permission, ideally in writing. If permission isn't possible, share anonymous results such as "a B2B software client" and remove identifying screenshots. Contracts may include confidentiality clauses, so check the agreement before posting any numbers or visuals.

Should digital agencies use hashtags on X?

Digital agencies can use hashtags on X, but sparingly. X's own help page recommends no more than two hashtags per post as a best practice. Most agency posts do better with clear, specific wording instead, because people search X by topic words as well as by hashtags.

What should a digital agency avoid posting on X?

A digital agency should avoid posting confidential client details, harsh criticism of named companies, engagement bait and constant sales posts. Promotional posts work best as roughly one in five. X's authenticity rules also ban buying engagement and coordinated engagement swaps, so grow with useful content instead.
