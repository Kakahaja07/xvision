How to Use Twitter Analytics in 2026 (X Analytics Guide)

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you use Twitter analytics in 2026?

To use Twitter analytics in 2026, open x.com/i/account_analytics (or Premium > Analytics) on desktop, pick a date range from 7 days to a year, and review impressions, engagement rate, profile visits and new followers. Account-level analytics needs a paid X plan, while basic stats for single posts appear under each post's graph icon.

Most people open their analytics page once, see a wall of charts, and close the tab. The numbers are all there. What's missing is knowing which ones to look at and what to do next.

That's the real problem with Twitter analytics. The data is easy to find once you know where it lives. The hard part is turning it into a decision. Here's the good news: you only need a handful of numbers and about 15 minutes a week.

X (formerly Twitter) analytics is the set of stats X keeps on your posts and your account. It tells you how many times people saw your posts, how many acted on them, and how many followed you. Used well, it shows you what to post more of and what to drop.

Who can access Twitter analytics in 2026?

Full account analytics on X is a paid feature. You need a paid plan to see the account dashboard, while anyone can still view basic stats on their own single posts. X's ads help center also describes a free Post Activity Dashboard for people with an ads account.

The change happened in mid-2024. Social Media Today reported in June 2024 that X launched a new analytics page for Premium subscribers and that "all account analytics have now become paid only." Current guides from Sprout Social and Brandwatch say the same thing.

A few details are worth knowing at the time of writing (October 2026):

  • The old address, analytics.x.com, now redirects to x.com/i/account_analytics. I checked the redirect myself.
  • Paid plans changed this month. X's Premium help page says X Premium is now part of The X Subscription, launched October 12, 2026. The X Subscription pricing page lists "Advanced Analytics" from the Plus plan ($8 a month) up.
  • Older X Premium plans (Basic, Premium and Premium+) continue for existing subscribers, but no official page I found says which of those legacy tiers include analytics. Check the sign-up screen before you pay.
  • The Post Activity Dashboard help page still calls that dashboard a free feature for ads accounts. It focuses on post-level data and CSV exports, covered in how to export Twitter analytics data.

X's main help center has no current article on account analytics. Its old article on the activity dashboard now redirects to an advertising page. So treat the details as widely reported, not official, and expect them to change.

How do you open X analytics on desktop and mobile?

On desktop, click Premium in the left menu and choose Analytics, or go straight to x.com/i/account_analytics. On mobile, open any of your posts and tap the small graph icon to see that post's stats.

According to Sprout Social's March 2026 guide to X analytics, the desktop view lets you pick the past 7 days, 28 days, 3 months or year.

  1. Log in to x.com on a computer.
  2. Open Premium, then Analytics (or paste the URL).
  3. Set the date range at the top. Start with 28 days.
  4. Look at the overview first, then open the content tab for single posts.

Which metrics should you check first?

Start with four numbers: impressions, engagement rate, profile visits and new followers. Together they show reach, reaction, curiosity and growth. Everything else, like likes or link clicks, explains why those four moved.

MetricWhat it tells youWhat to do with it
ImpressionsHow often your posts were shownTrack the weekly total. A drop of a week or two is normal noise.
Engagement rateShare of impressions that led to an actionCompare posts by topic and format, not by raw likes.
Profile visitsHow many people wanted to know who you areIf visits rise but follows don't, fix your bio and pinned post.
New followersGrowth over the periodNote which posts came right before a jump.

X defines engagement rate as engagements divided by impressions. Engagements include clicks anywhere on the post, not just likes. If you want the math with a worked example, see how to calculate Twitter engagement rate. For impressions in more depth, the reach guide on how to check impressions on Twitter walks through each view.

How do you read the numbers without fooling yourself?

Compare like with like. Use the same date range each time, wait a day or two before judging a post, and compare each post to your typical result, not your best one.

Here's the thing. Stats on X keep moving after you post. X's ads help center says its systems "take up to 36 hours to stabilize," so a post can look weaker on day one than it really is. I'd wait at least two days before deciding a post flopped.

Two more habits help:

  • Use your median, not your record. One viral post skews every average. Ask "is this better than my usual post?"
  • Watch rates and totals together. A high engagement rate on tiny reach can mean a loyal but small audience.

If a number looks broken, for example impressions stuck at zero, the fix guide on Twitter analytics not showing impressions covers the usual causes.

What does a good weekly analytics routine look like?

A good weekly routine takes about 15 minutes. Check the 7-day overview, find your top three and bottom three posts, write down what they have in common, and pick one change to test next week.

  1. Minute 1 to 3: Note impressions, engagement rate, profile visits and new followers in a notebook or sheet.
  2. Minute 4 to 8: Sort your posts and pick the top three. The steps for this are in how to find your best performing tweets.
  3. Minute 9 to 12: Look at the bottom three. Was it the topic, the hook or the timing?
  4. Minute 13 to 15: Choose one test, like "two threads instead of one" or "post at lunch."

Make Twitter analytics a habit, not a chore

Using Twitter analytics in 2026 comes down to a small loop. Open the dashboard once a week, read four numbers, learn from your best and worst posts, and test one change. You don't need every chart. You need the habit. Start this week, and in a month your posting will feel far less like guessing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Twitter analytics free in 2026?

No, full account analytics on X is not free in 2026. Reports since June 2024 say the dashboard needs a paid plan, and x.ai lists Advanced Analytics from the Plus plan up. Authors can still see basic stats on single posts for free, and X's ads help center describes a free Post Activity Dashboard.

Where is the analytics button on X?

On desktop, the analytics page sits under Premium in the left menu, then Analytics. The direct address is x.com/i/account_analytics, and the old analytics.x.com link redirects there. On the mobile app, open one of your posts and tap the graph icon at the bottom to see that post's stats.

How far back does X analytics go?

X analytics on desktop offers date ranges of the past 7 days, 28 days, 3 months or one year, according to Sprout Social's 2026 guide. To keep a longer history, export your data regularly or log weekly totals in a spreadsheet so older numbers are not lost.

Why do my X analytics numbers keep changing?

X analytics numbers keep changing because posts collect views and engagements for days. X's ads help center says its systems take up to 36 hours to stabilize, so early figures can shift. Judge a post after two or three days, and compare it with your usual results for the same period.

What is the difference between X analytics and the Post Activity Dashboard?

X account analytics at x.com/i/account_analytics gives an account overview for paid subscribers. The Post Activity Dashboard, reached through ads.x.com under Analytics, focuses on single posts, splits organic and promoted results, and exports a CSV of up to 30 days and 3,000 posts at a time.
