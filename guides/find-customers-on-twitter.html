How to Find Customers on Twitter (X): Search, Lists and Smart Filters

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you find customers on Twitter?

You find customers on Twitter (X) by searching for the exact words people use when they describe your product's problem, using operators like quotes, -filter:replies and min_faves: to cut noise. Save promising accounts in a private List, check who they are before engaging, and reply helpfully before you ever pitch.

Somewhere on X right now, someone is posting "does anyone know a tool that does this?" about the exact thing you sell. They'll get a few replies by lunchtime and pick one. Plenty of founders never see that post, because they're scrolling the For You feed instead of searching.

In my view, search is the most underused customer-finding tool on X. It's free, it's fast, and once you set up a few good searches, the routine takes about 20 minutes a day. Here's how I'd set it up from scratch.

Why is X search good for finding customers?

X search is good for finding customers because people describe their problems, ask for recommendations and complain about tools in public posts. Search lets you find those posts by keyword, phrase, account, language and date instead of waiting for them to appear in your feed.

According to X's help page on advanced search, you can filter by words (including exact phrases, any of several words and excluded words), by people (posts from, replying to or mentioning an account), by place and by date. Advanced search works when you're logged in. On x.com, run a search, then open Advanced search from the search filters.

Which X search operators help find customers?

The X search operators that help most are exact phrases in quotes, OR, the minus sign, -filter:replies, min_faves:, to:, lang: and since:. You can type them straight into the search bar and combine them in one query.

Most of this syntax comes from a well-known community-maintained reference on GitHub, not from X itself. That page notes its examples were last checked in 2022, so test each search before you rely on it. X changes things, and some operators may behave differently today.

Five search recipes to find customers on Twitter

Five search recipes to find customers on Twitter cover the main buying moments: asking for recommendations, describing a problem, comparing alternatives, complaining to a competitor and asking a direct question. Swap in your own product category and keywords.

  1. Recommendation requests: "can anyone recommend" OR "any recommendations" invoicing -filter:replies
  2. Problem statements: "so tired of" spreadsheets lang:en since:2026-10-01
  3. Alternative hunters: "alternative to" CompetitorName -from:CompetitorName
  4. Competitor support replies: to:competitorhandle (read these to learn what users struggle with)
  5. Direct questions: "how do you" "client onboarding" ?

A word on recipe 4. Replying to a competitor's unhappy users with a pitch looks bad and often backfires. Use those searches to learn what people want. Then write posts that solve that problem, and the right people find you.

How do you use X Lists to track potential customers?

You use X Lists to track potential customers by adding promising accounts to a private List, then checking that List daily instead of your main feed. A List shows only posts from its members, so conversations you can help with don't get buried.

According to X's help page on Lists, Lists are public by default, so switch the Private setting on when you create one. List names can't exceed 25 characters, and you can pin a List next to your For You and Following timelines. I'd make three: "Hot leads," "Customers" and "Industry voices."

Keep it human. X's authenticity rules prohibit aggressively adding users to Lists, so add people one at a time as you find them, not in bulk.

How do you qualify an account before reaching out?

You qualify an account before reaching out by checking the bio, recent posts, follower count and account age. You want real people or businesses with the problem you solve, not bots, giveaway accounts or people who never engage.

How do you approach a potential customer without spamming?

You approach a potential customer without spamming by replying publicly with a useful answer first, then offering your product only when it clearly fits their question. A DM comes later, after they've engaged with you.

A good reply answers the question even if they never buy. For example: "We had the same problem. What fixed it for us was a simple weekly checklist. I also built a tool for this if you want to see it." That's honest, useful and easy to ignore. For service businesses, the playbook in how to get clients through Twitter covers DMs and follow-ups. For bigger funnels, see how to generate leads from Twitter.

A 20-minute daily routine to find customers on Twitter

A 20-minute daily routine is enough: 10 minutes running your saved searches, 5 minutes checking your private List, and 5 minutes writing two or three genuinely helpful replies. Do it at the same time every day.

If you haven't defined who you're searching for yet, start with how to find your target audience on Twitter. SaaS founders can fold this routine into the bigger Twitter growth strategy for SaaS founders.

Your next customer is already posting

Finding customers on Twitter isn't luck. It's a handful of smart searches, a private List and a habit of replying with real help. Set up your first three searches today, and tomorrow you'll see conversations you never knew existed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Twitter search operators still work in 2026?

Twitter search operators such as exact phrases in quotes, OR, from:, to: and since: are still documented by community references for X search. X's official help page covers the advanced search form, not a full operator list. Test each operator with a known example, because X can change search behavior without notice.

Can you use X advanced search without an account?

No, X advanced search requires you to be logged in. According to X's Help Center, advanced search is available when logged in to x.com, where it lets you filter by words, people, places and dates. A free X account is enough to use it and to create private Lists.

Is it OK to reply to a competitor's unhappy customers on X?

Replying to a competitor's unhappy customers with a sales pitch usually looks opportunistic and can hurt your brand. A better approach is to study those complaints, then post helpful content about the problem. If someone directly asks for alternatives, a short, honest reply mentioning your product is fair.

What is the difference between finding customers and finding a target audience on X?

Finding customers on X means locating specific people who have a problem your product solves right now. Finding a target audience means defining the broader group you post for over time. The audience shapes your content, while customer searches surface individual buying conversations you can join today.

How many accounts should a private List of potential customers have?

A private List of potential customers works best with about 50 to 200 accounts. That is enough to see several relevant posts each day and small enough to read in five minutes. Remove accounts that go inactive or turn out to be a poor fit every few weeks.
