How to Get Your First 100 Followers on X

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you get your first 100 followers on X?

To get your first 100 followers on X, set up a clear profile, pick a narrow niche, and spend most of your time writing useful replies under fresh posts from accounts a few steps ahead of you. Post once or twice a day, answer everyone who replies, and keep the routine going for 30 days.

Your first post goes out, and nothing happens. No likes, no replies, maybe three views. It's easy to think something is broken. It isn't. Almost nobody sees a new account's posts yet, and that's true for everyone who has ever started on X.

The fix isn't posting more. It's going where people already are, being useful there, and giving them a reason to check your profile. And here's a nice bit of perspective: in a 2019 study, Pew Research Center found that the bottom 90% of US adult Twitter users had a median of just 19 followers. The data is old, but 100 real followers in one niche still puts you ahead of most accounts.

Why are the first 100 followers the hardest?

The first 100 followers are the hardest because X shows posts to your followers first, and you have none. Posts shown to people who don't follow you are scored lower. So early on, your own posts have very little to carry them.

Replies work around that problem. When you reply under a bigger account's post, your words appear in front of their audience, people who already care about the topic. That's why this plan starts with replies.

Step 1: Make your profile worth a follow

Your profile decides the follow, usually right after one good reply leads someone to it. They give it a few seconds. Make those seconds count.

  • Name: your real name, or a name you'll keep. Skip the emoji piles.
  • Photo: a clear, friendly face or a simple logo. Accounts with no photo look abandoned.
  • Bio: one line on what you do and one on what people get by following. "I build small SaaS tools and share the numbers" beats "Founder | Dreamer | Coffee".
  • Pinned post: your single best post, or a short intro thread. It's your landing page.

For photo sizes and bio wording, see how to optimize your Twitter profile for followers.

Step 2: Pick a narrow niche

A narrow niche helps the right people recognize you fast, and it means you always know what to post. "Tech" is too wide. "Building a SaaS as a solo developer" is a niche.

Write down three topics you could talk about for a year. Everything you post and most of what you reply to should fit one of them. This part is actually really easy, and it saves you from staring at a blank post box later.

Step 3: Reply before you post

Replies are the main way to get your first 100 followers on X. With 50 followers, a great post reaches almost no one. A great reply under a bigger account's fresh post can reach many people who already care about your topic.

  • Pick 20 to 30 accounts in your niche that are a few steps ahead of you. My rule of thumb is roughly 2,000 to 50,000 followers, since they tend to notice replies more than huge accounts do.
  • Reply early, ideally within the first 30 minutes, while the conversation is still small.
  • Add something: a short story, a number, a counterpoint or a useful link. "Great post!" adds nothing.

Getting the author to reply back helps too. X's page about conversations on X says that when it ranks replies, it considers whether the original author has replied. The reply strategy guide shows what a reply that adds value looks like.

I built a free Chrome extension, XVision, that puts a "Reply early" badge on fresh posts from bigger accounts that still have few replies, and keeps a reply queue of them. It makes this step quicker, but a list of accounts and some attention work too.

Step 4: Post on a steady rhythm

One or two thoughtful posts a day is plenty while you build your first 100. Consistency matters more than volume, because each post is another chance for someone who liked your reply to see more of you.

Good post types for small accounts:

  • Lessons: something you learned this week, with the specific detail.
  • Progress: real numbers from what you're building, good or bad.
  • Opinions: a clear take on your niche, with a reason.
  • Questions: genuine ones you want answered, not engagement bait.

If you want your posts to hold attention, how to write engaging Twitter posts covers hooks and structure.

Step 5: Turn replies into real connections

The people who reply to you are your first community. Reply back, follow the ones who share your niche, and remember who they are. Twenty real connections who share your posts are worth more than a thousand silent followers.

Keep a short note on each person, like "founder, building a habit app." When they post, you'll know what to say. Over time these people start sharing your work, and that's when growth begins to compound.

A daily routine for your first 100 followers

A simple daily routine takes about 30 minutes, split into two reply sessions and one post. Do it for 30 days before you judge the results.

  1. 15 minutes: reply to 10 fresh posts in your niche.
  2. Post one thing you learned or shipped.
  3. 15 minutes later: reply to everyone who replied to you.
  4. Evening: another round of 10 replies.

Once a week, look at which posts and replies did better than your usual and ask why. Was it the topic, the format or the time? Do more of that.

What should you avoid with a brand-new account?

Avoid follow-for-follow schemes, mass following and any app that promises followers. X's page about following on X caps every account at 400 follows per day, bans follow churn, and says aggressive following can get an account locked. Those followers wouldn't engage anyway.

What comes after 100 followers?

After 100 followers, you keep the reply habit and start leaning more on your own posts. The milestone plan in how to get 1,000 followers on Twitter picks up right there. For the full journey, the roadmap for growing a Twitter account from zero lays out every stage, and the 30-day plan for growing a new account fast turns it into a calendar.

Your first 100 start with one good reply

Getting your first 100 followers on X is slow at first, then it speeds up as your replies, posts and connections start working together. You don't need luck or money. You need a clear profile, a narrow niche and a daily habit of being useful. Write your first good reply today. You've got this.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to get 100 followers on X?

Getting 100 followers on X often takes a few weeks to a few months, depending on your niche and how many useful replies you write each day. X does not publish an average. A fair test is 30 days of daily replies and one or two posts a day before you judge your progress.

Should you follow people to get followers on X?

You should follow people whose posts you want to read and reply to, not to get follow-backs. X caps follows at 400 per day and bans follow churn, which is following and unfollowing in bulk to inflate your count. Following 50 to 100 relevant accounts builds a feed worth replying to.

Is 100 followers on X good?

Yes, 100 followers on X is a good start. A 2019 Pew Research Center study found the bottom 90% of US adult Twitter users had a median of 19 followers. If your 100 followers share an interest in your niche, they give your posts real people to reach and reply.

What should you post on X with zero followers?

With zero followers on X, post a short pinned introduction first, then one or two posts a day that fit your niche: a lesson, a progress update, an opinion with a reason, or a real question. Spend more time on replies, because your posts reach very few people until you have followers.

Do you need a blue checkmark to get your first followers on X?

No, you do not need a blue checkmark to get your first followers on X. It comes with paid plans, such as the $8-a-month Plus plan in the US (October 2026). Verified replies get a slight ranking preference, but a clear profile and useful replies can earn your first 100 followers for free.
