How to grow on X from your first 100 followers

A simple, repeatable plan for small accounts: a clear profile, a narrow niche, and replies before posts.

Growing a new account on X feels slow because almost nobody sees your posts yet. The fix is not posting more. It is going where people already are, being useful there, and giving them a reason to check your profile. This guide is the plan we would follow from zero.

1. Make your profile worth a follow

People decide to follow in a few seconds, usually after one good reply leads them to your profile. Make those seconds count.

  • Name: your real name, or a name you will keep. Avoid emoji piles.
  • Photo: a clear, friendly face or a simple logo. Accounts without a photo look abandoned, and many people ignore them.
  • Bio: one line on what you do and one on what people get by following. "I build small SaaS tools and share the numbers" beats "Founder | Dreamer | Coffee".
  • Pinned post: your single best post, or a short intro thread. It is your landing page.

2. Pick a narrow niche

"Tech" is too wide. "Building a SaaS as a solo developer" is a niche. A narrow topic does two things: the right people recognise you quickly, and you always know what to post about.

Write down three topics you can talk about for a year. Everything you post should fit one of them.

3. Reply before you post

When you have 50 followers, a great post reaches almost no one. A great reply under a bigger account's fresh post can reach thousands of people who already care about your topic. Early on, replies are your main growth channel.

  • Find 20 to 30 accounts in your niche that are a few steps ahead of you, roughly 2,000 to 50,000 followers. They notice replies far more than huge accounts do.
  • Reply within the first 30 minutes of their posts, while the conversation is small.
  • Add something: a short story, a number, a counterpoint, a useful link. "Great post!" adds nothing.

4. Post on a steady rhythm

One or two thoughtful posts a day is plenty. Consistency matters more than volume, because each post is another chance for someone who liked your reply to see more of you.

Good post types for small accounts:

  • Lessons: something you learned this week, with the specific detail.
  • Progress: real numbers from what you are building, good or bad.
  • Opinions: a clear take on your niche, with a reason.
  • Questions: genuine ones you want answered, not engagement bait.

5. Build real connections

The people who reply to you are your first community. Reply back, follow the ones who share your niche, and keep a short note on who they are. Twenty real connections who share your posts beat a thousand silent followers.

6. Measure what works

Every week, look at which posts did better than your usual and why. Was it the topic, the format, the time? Do more of that and less of the rest.

A daily routine that works

  1. 15 minutes: reply to 10 fresh posts in your niche.
  2. Post one thing you learned or shipped.
  3. 15 minutes later: reply to everyone who replied to you.
  4. Evening: another round of 10 replies.

Do this for 30 days before judging the results. Growth on X is slow at first and then speeds up as your replies, posts and connections start to compound.
