Why Nobody Likes My Tweets (and What to Do About It)

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Why does nobody like my tweets?

Nobody likes your tweets usually because too few people see them, or the people who see them get no clear reason to react. Small accounts are normal: Pew Research found in 2019 that the bottom 90% of U.S. Twitter users had a median of 19 followers. Better first lines and daily replies fix most cases.

You write something you think is good. You post it. You refresh. Nothing. Then you refresh again, and it's still nothing. It's a lonely feeling, and almost everyone on X has felt it.

Here's the good news: this is one of the most common problems on X (formerly Twitter), and it's fixable. Zero likes is rarely a verdict on you or your ideas. Most of the time it just means the post didn't reach the right people, or didn't give them a reason to tap the heart.

First, did anyone actually see it?

Start by checking the view count on the post. If it shows very few views, the problem is reach, not the tweet. If it shows hundreds of views and no likes, the problem is the tweet itself.

This one check saves a lot of guessing. Here's how to read it:

What you seeWhat it usually meansWhere to start
Very few views, no likesAlmost nobody saw itGrow reach through replies and better timing
Decent views, no likesPeople saw it and kept scrollingImprove the first line and add specifics
Views and likes drop suddenlySomething changedCheck for restrictions or a topic shift

If your problem is the first row, our guide on why your tweets get no views goes deeper on reach.

The most common reasons nobody likes your tweets

The most common reasons are a small audience, posts that start slow, posts that are too general, and posting without being part of any conversation. Usually it's two or three of these at once.

1. Your audience is still small

Most accounts on X are small. Pew Research's 2019 study Sizing Up Twitter Users found the median U.S. adult user posted just twice a month, and the bottom 90% of tweeters had a median of 19 followers. That data is old, but the pattern still fits what I see: a few accounts do most of the posting. If 19 people follow you, and only a few are online, zero likes is just maths.

2. The first line doesn't hook

People decide in a glance. A post that opens with "So I've been thinking about something..." loses most readers before the point arrives. Put the point, result or surprise first.

3. The post is too general

"Consistency is key" is true and forgettable. A real number, name, mistake or opinion gives people something to react to. Our guide to writing engaging Twitter posts shows the difference with examples.

4. You only post, never reply

This is the big one for new accounts. If you only post into your own small timeline, there's nobody there yet. Replying to others in your niche is how people discover you. The guide on writing replies that add value explains how to do it without sounding spammy.

5. Your topics jump around

If one day you post about football, the next about coding and then about cooking, followers never know what to expect. People follow for a reason. Give them more of that reason.

6. You posted at a quiet time

A good post at 3 a.m. for your audience may vanish before anyone wakes up. Posting when your followers are active gives every tweet a fair start.

7. Engagement is low across the board

Low likes are normal on X. Rival IQ's 2026 benchmark report found a median engagement rate of 0.03% per post per follower for brands on X in 2025. So even big brand accounts get few likes relative to their size. You're not alone.

Could your account be restricted?

An account restriction is possible but much less common than the simple causes. If your likes and views dropped suddenly and stayed low for days, it's worth checking.

Look for clear signs first: a warning from X, a locked feature, or replies that friends can't see. Our guide on how to tell if your Twitter account is restricted walks through the checks. Most of the time, though, the cause is one of the seven reasons above.

Here's exactly what to do this week

Fix the problem with a short daily routine: write a few thoughtful replies, post once with a strong first line, and track which posts do better than usual. You'll see patterns within a week or two.

  1. Reply first. Leave 10 to 20 useful replies a day on posts in your niche.
  2. Post once, well. One specific post beats three vague ones.
  3. Lead with the point. Rewrite your first line until it could stand alone.
  4. Stick to two or three topics. Let people know what they're following.
  5. Review weekly. Look at which posts got more likes than usual and why.

Zero likes today is not zero likes forever

Nobody likes your tweets right now, most likely, because you're early. That's not a failure. It's the starting line every account on X crosses.

The fix is simpler than you'd expect. Show up in other people's conversations, write posts with one clear idea, and give it a few weeks. The first few likes from strangers feel small, but they're proof the system is starting to work for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it normal to get zero likes on X?

Yes, getting zero likes on X is normal, especially for new and small accounts. Pew Research found in 2019 that the bottom 90% of U.S. Twitter users had a median of 19 followers. With an audience that size, many posts reach only a handful of people, and likes come slowly at first.

Why do my friends' tweets get more likes than mine?

Your friends' tweets may get more likes because they have more followers, post when their audience is online, or reply to others more often. Their topics may also be broader. Compare views as well as likes. If they get far more views, the difference is reach, not writing quality.

Should I delete tweets that get no likes?

No, there's usually no need to delete tweets that got no likes on X. Old posts with low engagement don't block new ones from reaching people. A better use of time is to rewrite the idea with a stronger first line and post it again later, at a busier time.

How long does it take to start getting likes?

Most new X accounts start getting regular likes after a few weeks of daily replies and steady posting, though there is no official timeline. Progress depends on your niche and effort. Ten to twenty thoughtful replies a day is a realistic routine that helps strangers find your profile.

Does X hide tweets from small accounts?

No, X does not publicly say it hides tweets from small accounts. Small accounts simply have fewer followers to show posts to at first. Replies under larger posts, clear topics and good timing help a small account reach people beyond its followers without any special status.
