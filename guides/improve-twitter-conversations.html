How to Improve Twitter Conversations on X

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you improve Twitter conversations?

To improve Twitter conversations, reply to people who answer you, ask a follow-up question instead of just saying thanks, disagree with reasons rather than insults, and stay on topic. X's help center says reply ranking considers whether the original author replied, so joining your own threads makes the whole conversation more visible.

I've watched plenty of good posts die in the replies. Someone asks a smart question, three people answer, and the author never comes back. The thread just stops. Compare that with a post where the author answers everyone, asks follow-ups and tags in a friend who knows more. That second thread keeps growing for hours.

A conversation on X (formerly Twitter) is a post plus all the replies under it, including replies to replies. Better conversations matter for two reasons. They are more fun and useful for everyone involved. And they keep a post active, which brings more people into it.

Why do conversations matter on X?

Conversations matter on X because replies carry far more weight than likes in X's ranking code. As of October 2026, X's open-source weight file gives a reply 5.0 and a like 0.66. Conversations also build real relationships, which is what turns strangers into followers.

Those values come from X's current ranking weights in the xai-org/x-algorithm repo, which change often. The same code adds a mutual-follow reply boost (15 as of October 2026) for replies to posts from people you follow who follow you back. Conversations between people who know each other count extra. Back in 2023, the older release gave a reply 13.5, a like 0.5 and a "reply engaged by author" 75.0. The numbers changed, but the direction held: a real back-and-forth counts for much more than quiet approval.

X's help page About conversations on X backs this up from another angle. It says replies are ranked using factors like whether the original author replied, whether the reply is from someone you follow, and whether the person pays for X Premium or a paid plan on The X Subscription, which absorbed Premium on October 12, 2026. For more on ranking in general, see how the X algorithm works in 2026.

How to improve Twitter conversations on your own posts

You improve conversations on your own posts by showing up in them. Reply early, answer with substance, and give people a reason to reply again.

Answer with more than "thanks"

"Thanks!" ends a conversation. "Thanks, did that work for you on mobile too?" keeps it going. One extra question turns a reply into a real exchange.

Use people's answers

Quote a good reply in your own response, or say "This is a better point than mine." People love being heard, and others reading the thread see that you pay attention.

Connect people

If two people in your replies would get along, introduce them. "You two are working on the same problem" can start a new thread you don't even need to be part of.

Ask questions people can answer

Conversations start with an easy door in. A question with a short personal answer works best. Here's a list of the best questions to ask on X for engagement.

How do you disagree well on X?

Disagree well by naming the part you disagree with, giving your reason, and staying kind to the person. A good disagreement is often the most interesting part of a thread.

Here's a simple shape that works:

  1. Show you understood: "I see why you'd say that about pricing."
  2. Name the difference: "I've found the opposite with small teams."
  3. Give a reason or example: "When we raised prices, churn didn't move."
  4. Leave the door open: "Maybe it depends on the market?"

That last line matters. It invites a reply instead of a fight. Bad-faith arguing may get views, but it attracts the wrong people and makes thoughtful readers leave.

How to use reply controls to protect a conversation

X lets you choose who can reply to a post: Everyone, Accounts you follow, Only accounts you mention, or Only verified accounts. You can change this even after posting.

According to X's help center, the setting is in the compose box under "Everyone can reply," and you can change it later from the More menu on the post with "Change who can reply." Restrictions only limit replies. People can still like, repost or quote the post.

When should you use it? I'd keep replies open by default, since that's how new people find you. Narrow them when a post gets flooded with spam or abuse, or when you want a focused talk with specific people. For single bad actors, mute or block works better than closing replies for everyone.

How to join other people's conversations well

Join other conversations by adding something new, staying on topic and keeping it short. A reply should make the thread better, not pull attention away from it.

  • Read the thread first. Don't repeat a point someone already made.
  • Add one specific thing. A number, an example or a question.
  • Stay on topic. X's authenticity policy lists "replying with content that is irrelevant to the topic of the original post" as spam.
  • Don't drop links uninvited. Share your project only when someone asks or it truly answers the question.

The guide on how to write replies that add value goes deeper on this.

One small habit helps a lot over time: remember who you talked with. I built a free extension, XVision, that lets you add private tags and notes to people, so you can recall that "@sam asked about pricing last week" the next time you see them.

Better conversations build better accounts

Improving your Twitter conversations is really about treating replies as the main event, not an afterthought. Show up, ask follow-ups, disagree kindly and protect the thread when you need to.

Do this for a few weeks and you'll notice the same names coming back. That's the start of a real network. If you want to grow it into something bigger, see how to build a community on X.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I see the whole conversation on X?

To see the whole conversation on X, tap or click the post to open its detail page, which shows the replies grouped into sub-conversations. X's help center says replies aren't always in time order, because X ranks them by relevance, such as whether the author replied or whether you follow the person replying.

Can I stop strangers replying to my posts?

Yes, X lets you limit replies when you write a post or afterwards. Choose Accounts you follow, Only accounts you mention, or Only verified accounts under "Everyone can reply." People who can't reply can still like, repost or quote the post, according to X's help center.

Why are some replies hidden or shown lower?

Some replies appear lower on X because replies are ranked, not listed by time. X's help center says ranking favors replies that are relevant, credible and safe, and considers factors like whether the original author replied, whether you follow the person, and whether they pay for X Premium or The X Subscription.

Should I reply to trolls on X?

No, replying to trolls on X usually rewards them with attention and drags the thread off topic. Mute or block the account instead, and narrow who can reply if a post is flooded. Save your replies for people who engage in good faith, since that's where real conversations grow.

Do quote posts help conversations?

Quote posts can help conversations on X by bringing your followers into a topic with your own comment on top. They start a separate thread, though. X's help center notes that replies to a quote post don't automatically include the original author, so mention them if you want them involved.
