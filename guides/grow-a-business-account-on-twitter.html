How to Grow a Business Account on Twitter (X): A Brand Account Playbook

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you grow a business account on Twitter?

To grow a business account on Twitter (X), convert it to a free Professional Account, make the profile show what the business does, post helpful content more than promotions, and reply to customers and people in your industry. Brand accounts usually grow slower than personal ones, so pair the brand with active founder and team accounts.

Scroll through a few company accounts on X and you'll notice a pattern. Many of them post launch announcements and discount codes into silence. A few others feel like a helpful person is behind them, answering questions and sharing what they know. Those few are usually the ones people follow.

Growing a brand account is a little different from growing a personal one. People follow people more easily than logos. The good news is that a business account has things a person doesn't: a product people already use, customers with questions, and a team that can share the work.

Why do business accounts grow differently on X?

Business accounts grow differently because people don't naturally chat with logos. A brand has to earn attention by being useful, responsive and a bit human, while a person can earn it just by being interesting.

In my view, that's why the best brand accounts on X act less like a billboard and more like a helpful expert. They teach, they answer, and they point to people inside the company. Promotions still have a place, but they work better when the account has already earned trust.

Step 1: Set up the business account properly

Start by converting the account to a Professional Account, which X says is completely free. It adds a business category to your profile and unlocks tools such as Professional Home analytics and profile spotlights.

According to X's Professional Accounts help page, the spotlights include a Location Spotlight (US only) with your address and hours, a Shop Spotlight for some qualifying accounts, and a Link Spotlight. To qualify, the account needs a complete profile with a name, bio and profile picture, and a clear, authentic identity.

Then make the profile do its job:

  • Profile photo: your logo, simple enough to read at a small size.
  • Header: what you do, or what's new right now. See these Twitter header ideas for businesses.
  • Bio: who you serve, what you offer and why to follow.
  • Pinned post: your most useful resource or a clear intro, not an old promotion.

X also offers paid verification for organizations. Its X Premium page lists Premium Business and Premium Organizations, which come with gold or grey checkmarks and affiliate badges. That's optional. Plenty of brands grow without it.

Step 2: Decide what the account is for

A business account grows faster when it has one clear job, such as teaching, support, news or community. A clear job tells people what they'll get if they follow, which is the same reason a person's niche matters.

A content mix that works for many brands looks like this. The split is my suggestion, not a rule.

Content typeExampleShare of posts
TeachingA quick tip that solves a customer problemAbout half
Behind the scenesHow a feature was built, or a team momentAbout a fifth
Customer voicesReal customer posts you repost or quoteAbout a fifth
Product newsLaunches, updates and offersThe rest

Step 3: Give the brand a voice

A brand voice is a short set of rules for how the account sounds, so it stays the same no matter who's posting. Write down three words that describe it, like "plain, warm, practical," and two things it never does.

If several people share the account, keep the voice notes where everyone can see them. Consistency is what makes a brand recognizable. A friendly reply on Monday and a stiff press-release tone on Friday confuse people.

Step 4: Reply to customers and your industry

Replies are where business accounts build trust fastest. Answer questions and mentions quickly, thank people who share your product, and join conversations in your industry with something useful to add.

Search for your brand name, product name and common misspellings every day, since not everyone tags you. When someone has a problem, reply in public with care and move details to DMs if needed. People watch how a brand handles problems.

Step 5: Let your people do the heavy lifting

Founders and team members often grow faster than the brand itself, because people follow people. Their posts and replies can send new visitors to the business account.

Encourage a founder or a few team members to post about their work from personal accounts, and have the brand repost the best of it. The guide on growing a founder account on X covers the founder side, and building a personal brand on X helps anyone on the team find their angle.

What should a business account avoid?

A business account should avoid buying followers, mass following, hashtag stuffing and posting only promotions. The first three can break X's rules, and the last one simply gets ignored.

X's page about following on X caps follows at 400 per day and bans services that claim to add followers. X's authenticity policy also lists "posting with excessive, unrelated hashtags" as spam. A brand that gets locked or limited loses far more than it gained.

How do you measure a business account's growth?

Measure a business account by engagement and outcomes, not just follower count. Watch replies per post, profile visits, link clicks and how many customer questions come through X.

A brand people actually want to follow

Growing a business account on Twitter comes down to being useful in public and letting real people show through. Set up the Professional Account, give the brand one job and one voice, reply generously, and let your team carry the conversation. Do that steadily, and the account starts to grow on its own.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is X still worth it for businesses in 2026?

X can still be worth it for businesses in 2026 if their customers use it. Pew Research Center's 2026 survey found 18% of US adults use X, with higher use among adults aged 18 to 29. Before investing heavily, check whether your customers already talk about your category on X.

Is a Professional Account free on X?

Yes, a Professional Account on X is free. X says converting is completely free, and it adds a professional category to your profile plus tools such as Professional Home analytics and profile spotlights. The account needs a complete profile with a name, bio and profile picture, and a clear, authentic identity.

How often should a business account post on X?

A business account on X can post once or twice a day and still grow, as long as it replies to customers and industry conversations every day. Replies often matter more than posts for a brand. Pick a pace your team can keep for a year rather than a burst you can't sustain.

Should a business account follow back customers on X?

A business account on X doesn't need to follow back every customer. Follow back partners, active customers and people in your industry whose posts you want to see and reply to. X caps every account at 400 follows per day, and mass following can get an account locked or limited.

Do businesses need a gold checkmark on X?

No, businesses do not need a gold checkmark to grow on X. The gold checkmark comes with X's paid Premium Business plans, which start at $200 a month as of October 2026. It can help people trust that an account is official, but useful posts, quick replies and a clear profile matter more for growth.
