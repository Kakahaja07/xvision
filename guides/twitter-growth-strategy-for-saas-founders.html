Twitter Growth Strategy for SaaS Founders: A 90-Day Plan

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

What is the best Twitter growth strategy for SaaS founders?

The best Twitter growth strategy for SaaS founders is to post from your own founder account about one problem your product solves, reply daily to the people who have that problem, and send profile visitors to one clear next step. Give it 90 days and judge it by signups and demo calls, not follower counts.

Picture a founder with a good product and 200 followers. She posts a launch update, gets four likes, and decides X "doesn't work for B2B." You hear this story from SaaS founders all the time. The product wasn't the problem. The plan was.

Here's the good news. A growth strategy on X (formerly Twitter) is not about going viral. It's about showing up for the same small group of buyers until they know your name. You can absolutely do that in a few focused hours a week.

Why should SaaS founders grow on X at all?

SaaS founders should grow on X because it is one of the few places where you can talk to buyers, peers and investors in public, for free, from day one. The audience is smaller than people think, which is exactly why a focused founder can stand out.

Let me explain with numbers. According to the Pew Research Center's 2026 social media fact sheet, 18% of U.S. adults say they ever use X. That share rises to 22% among college graduates and 24% among adults in households earning $100,000 or more. So X is not a mass-market channel. It skews toward graduates and higher earners, which is a useful skew if you sell software to businesses.

Who exactly are you trying to reach?

You are trying to reach one specific buyer, described in a single sentence: their role, their main problem and the moment they feel it. Every post and reply in your Twitter growth strategy should make sense to that one person.

For example: "Ops leads at 20 to 100 person agencies who lose hours every week chasing timesheets." That sentence tells you who to follow and which conversations to join.

Then search for the words they use when they complain, and save the best accounts into a private List. The exact search operators are in my guide on how to find customers on Twitter.

What should a SaaS founder post on X?

A SaaS founder should post about three things: the problem your buyer has, what you are learning while building the fix, and what you hear from customers. Roughly two posts out of three should help the reader even if they never sign up.

  • Problem posts. Explain a mistake your buyer makes, a cost they don't see, or a better way to do a task. This is where new followers come from.
  • Building posts. Share a decision, a metric or a lesson from the week. This builds trust. My guide to building in public on Twitter covers what to share and what to keep private.
  • Customer posts. Share a pattern you noticed in support tickets or sales calls, with no names. This shows you understand the market.

How do replies grow a SaaS founder account?

Replies grow a SaaS founder account because they put your name in front of people who don't follow you yet. A thoughtful reply under a bigger post in your niche can bring profile visits that your own posts can't reach at first.

This is where it gets interesting. The open-source For You feed code that xAI published on GitHub ranks posts by predicting what a viewer might do next, including replying, clicking a profile and following the author. It also applies a discount to posts from accounts the viewer doesn't follow. When you have a small audience, that discount works against your own posts. Replies are how you get around it.

A simple daily habit works well:

  1. Pick 20 to 30 accounts your buyers already read.
  2. Reply to two or three of their fresh posts each day with a real point, a number or a question.
  3. Reply to every comment on your own posts within a few hours.

How do you turn profile visits into trials?

You turn profile visits into trials with a bio that names your buyer and result, a pinned post that shows the product working, and one link with a single next step. Visitors decide in a few seconds, so remove every extra choice.

The best X bio examples for startup founders show the pattern. Your pinned post can be a short demo video or a before-and-after screenshot. The link should go to a page that matches what you post about, not your generic homepage.

Add a tracking tag to that link so you can see how many trials come from X. The guide on how to generate leads from Twitter goes deeper on offers and lead magnets.

A 90-day Twitter growth strategy for SaaS founders

A 90-day Twitter growth strategy for SaaS founders breaks into three blocks of about four weeks: set up and listen, post and reply on a fixed rhythm, then double down on what brought trials. Here's how I'd lay it out.

WeeksFocusDaily actions
1 to 4Set up and listenFix bio and pinned post, build a private List of 50 buyers, 3 to 5 replies a day, 1 post a day
5 to 8Find your voice1 to 2 posts a day across the three types, replies every day, one longer thread a week
9 to 12Double downRepeat the topics that brought profile visits and trials, drop the ones that didn't

What should SaaS founders measure on X?

SaaS founders should measure trials and demo calls from X first, then replies from real buyers, then profile visits. Follower count is the last number to watch, because 300 of the right followers can beat 10,000 random ones.

The founder account is your cheapest growth channel

A Twitter growth strategy for SaaS founders comes down to focus. One buyer, one problem, daily replies and a clean path from profile to trial. You don't need a big audience. You need the right few hundred people to keep seeing your name.

Start this week with the one-sentence buyer description and a private List. The daily rhythm becomes second nature fast. You've got this.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should a SaaS company use a brand account or the founder's account on X?

A SaaS company should use both, with different jobs. The founder's account drives growth because people follow people and trust personal opinions. The brand account handles release notes, status updates and support replies. Keep both active, but put most of the daily posting and reply time into the founder account.

How often should a SaaS founder post on X?

A SaaS founder should post about once or twice a day and reply several times a day. Consistency matters more than volume. Five good replies to buyers often do more than a third post. A realistic routine takes 30 to 45 minutes daily, split between morning replies and one afternoon post.

Do SaaS founders need X Premium to grow?

No, SaaS founders do not need X Premium to start growing on X. A free account can post up to 280 characters, threads, images and video. Paid plans, like X Premium or a paid X Subscription plan, add longer posts and more. Start free, prove your topics work, then decide if the extra features are worth paying for.

How long does it take for X to bring SaaS signups?

X usually takes a few months to bring steady SaaS signups, and a fair test window is about 90 days. The first month builds a list of buyers and a reply habit. Signups tend to follow once the same people have seen a founder's name and useful posts many times.

Is advertising on X worth it for SaaS companies?

Advertising on X can be worth it for SaaS companies once an organic message already brings trials. X's advertising page says there is no minimum budget, and campaign goals include leads, website traffic and app installs. Running ads before the message works usually means paying to learn what free posts could have taught first.
