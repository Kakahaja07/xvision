AI Powered Twitter Analytics Dashboard: What Exists in 2026 and How to Use It

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

What is an AI powered Twitter analytics dashboard?

An AI powered Twitter analytics dashboard collects your X metrics, such as impressions, engagement rate and follower growth, and uses AI to summarize patterns or suggest next steps. In 2026, Hootsuite's Wisdom assistant surfaces insights, while X's own analytics require a paid plan. You can also paste your numbers into Grok or Gemini and ask questions.

Most analytics pages are a wall of charts. You open them, see that impressions went up or down, and close the tab without knowing what to do next.

That gap is what AI promises to close. Instead of charts, you get a plain-English answer: "Your threads beat single posts this month" or "Your best day was Tuesday." What I find interesting is how much of that you can already do today, often for free, once you know where the data lives.

Where does your X data come from?

Every analytics dashboard for X (formerly Twitter) starts from the same sources: X's own account analytics, data a connected scheduling tool collects, or public numbers visible on each post. The AI layer only works as well as the data underneath it.

X's own account analytics are paid. Social Media Today reported in June 2024 that X's new analytics page made account analytics paid only. Today, xAI's pricing page lists Advanced Analytics on the Plus plan ($8 a month) and above on The X Subscription, launched October 12, 2026. Sprout Social's 2026 guide says the dashboard lives at x.com/i/account_analytics and offers ranges from 7 days to a year.

Post view counts stay public and free on each post. That is enough for simple tracking, even without a subscription. Our guide on how to use Twitter analytics in 2026 walks through X's native screens.

Hootsuite: the clearest AI insight layer

Hootsuite calls Wisdom its AI assistant, one that helps create content and surface insights. Hootsuite Analytics supports X, helps you "see when to post and identify top-performing Tweets," and exports reports as CSV, XLSX, PDF or PPT. One caution: its AI performance score is described for Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn, so check X coverage during the free trial.

Typefully: clean X metrics with useful ratios

Typefully's analytics are X-only, according to its help page on analytics metrics. It shows impressions, engagements, engagement rate and a profile conversion rate (new followers divided by profile visits). You see the last 30 days by default and can download any chart as a CSV.

Buffer and Tweet Hunter: strong analytics, AI for writing

Buffer's X analytics include engagement rate and suggestions for your best day, post type and posting frequency. Tweet Hunter tracks daily follower growth, top tweets and per-tweet impressions. Both use AI mainly for writing posts, not for reading your analytics. For a wider comparison, see the best X analytics tools for creators.

How can you add AI to your own analytics for free?

You can add AI to your analytics for free by copying a small table of your post results into a general assistant like Grok or Gemini and asking specific questions. This works surprisingly well for spotting patterns across 20 to 50 posts.

  1. Build a small table. For each post: date, time, format (text, image, thread), views, likes, replies, reposts.
  2. Add an engagement rate column. Our free engagement rate calculator does the math for each post.
  3. Ask focused questions. "Which format has the highest median engagement rate?" "Which posts got the most replies per view?" "What do my top 5 posts have in common?"
  4. Check the answers. Re-sort the table yourself and confirm the top results.

Step 4 matters. X's help page about Grok warns it "may confidently provide factually incorrect information", and the same is true of any assistant doing math on pasted data. Also skip personal or sensitive details in what you paste.

The smart way to use AI analytics

An AI powered Twitter analytics dashboard is most useful as a fast reader of your own numbers, not as an oracle. Start with clean data, ask specific questions, and verify every answer against the raw figures. Do that, and AI turns a wall of charts into one clear next step, which is exactly what most of us need. Frequently Asked Questions

Is X analytics free in 2026?

No, full account analytics on X are not free in 2026. Account analytics became paid only in June 2024, and xAI's pricing page lists Advanced Analytics on the Plus plan and above of The X Subscription. View counts on individual posts remain public and free for anyone to see.

Can Grok analyze my X analytics?

Grok can analyze X analytics numbers that a user pastes or types into a conversation, and it can spot patterns such as which formats or days performed best. X warns that Grok may give confident but incorrect answers, so double-check any totals, averages or rankings it produces against the original data.

Which metrics matter most in an X dashboard?

The metrics that matter most in an X dashboard are engagement rate, replies per post, follower growth and profile conversion rate. Impressions show reach, but engagement rate and replies show whether people cared. Profile conversion rate, which Typefully defines as new followers divided by profile visits, shows how well the profile turns visitors into followers.

Can I export my X analytics to use with AI?

X's help center does not currently document a CSV export for account analytics, so check the analytics page on your plan. Third-party tools offer exports: Typefully lets users download any chart as CSV, and Hootsuite exports reports in CSV, XLSX, PDF or PPT formats. Those files work well with AI assistants.

Are AI analytics insights accurate?

AI analytics insights are only as accurate as the data and the model behind them. Summaries can misread small samples or calculate averages wrongly. Treat AI insights as suggestions, check them against the raw numbers, and look for patterns that hold across at least several weeks of posts before changing a strategy.
