Best Twitter Header Ideas for Businesses: 10 Concepts and a Layout Guide

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

What are the best Twitter header ideas for businesses?

The best Twitter header ideas for businesses show what the company does or what's new, such as a product shot with a short tagline, a current launch, the team or the storefront. X recommends a 1500x500 pixel header and treats it like a billboard. Keep text centered, because the top and bottom edges can be cropped.

Picture a local bakery's X profile. The header is a blurry photo of a sunset. The bio mentions bread. You have to scroll to find out they deliver, and that they just opened a second shop.

Now picture the same profile with a header showing fresh loaves, the words "Now delivering across town," and the shop's web address. Same business. Very different first impression.

The header is the biggest image on your X (formerly Twitter) profile. For a business, it's free advertising space that every visitor sees. Here's how to use it well, starting with the exact size so your design doesn't get cut off.

What size is a Twitter header for a business?

A Twitter header should be 1500x500 pixels, which is X's recommended size for every account, business or personal. That's a wide 3:1 shape. X accepts JPG, GIF and PNG files but does not support animated GIFs for headers.

Those specs come from X's profile customization guide. There's one more detail most businesses miss. X's photo upload help page says that on different monitors and browsers, about 60 pixels at the top and bottom of the header can be cropped.

That's 60 pixels out of 500, or about 12% of the height at each edge. Together, nearly a quarter of the height may not show on some screens.

How should you lay out a business header?

Lay out a business header by keeping every important element inside the middle band, away from the top and bottom 60 pixels. Use the edges only for background color, texture or photo that can safely be trimmed.

Here's a simple layout guide based on X's numbers:

ZonePixels (on a 1500x500 canvas)What goes there
Top edgeRows 0 to 60Background only. May be cropped.
Middle bandRows 60 to 440 (380 pixels tall)Tagline, product, logo, web address
Bottom edgeRows 440 to 500Background only. May be cropped.

One more check: on X's current layout, your profile photo overlaps the lower part of the header. Before you finalize, upload the header and look at your profile on both a phone and a computer. If the photo covers something important, move that element toward the center or right.

X hasn't published official left-right safe zones, so the preview check is the most reliable test.

10 best Twitter header ideas for businesses

The best Twitter header ideas for businesses all share one trait: a visitor understands the business in about a second. X's business basics page suggests using the header "like a billboard to share what's new or timely." These ten ideas follow that advice.

1. Product shot plus one-line tagline

Show your main product on one side and a short line on the other, like "Accounting software for small cafes." This one makes the biggest difference for companies people don't know yet.

2. Current launch or campaign

Announce what's new: a product launch, a sale, a new location. It's exactly the "billboard" use X recommends. Just set a reminder to update it when the campaign ends.

3. Your team

A real team photo makes a business feel human. It works especially well for agencies, service firms and local businesses where customers deal with people directly.

4. Storefront or location

For shops, restaurants and clinics, a clear photo of the front door helps customers recognize the place. Add the neighborhood name if you have room.

5. The product in use

Show a customer using the product, or a screenshot of your app doing its main job. It answers "what does it do?" without words.

6. Your product lineup

For brands with several products, a clean row of items on a plain background shows range at a glance. Keep it to three to five items so each one stays visible.

7. Brand pattern with your web address

A simple brand-color pattern, your logo and your web address. It's low effort and always looks tidy. Great for businesses that don't have strong photos yet.

8. Upcoming event

Hosting a webinar, workshop or open day? Put the name and date in the header for the weeks before it.

9. Behind the scenes

A workshop, kitchen or studio photo shows craft and care. It gives followers a reason to look closer.

10. A real customer quote

A short, genuine review in big text, with the customer's permission. Never invent a testimonial. A fake quote damages trust far more than having no quote.

Which header mistakes should businesses avoid?

The most common business header mistakes are too much text, text near the edges, and images that look blurry. Each one makes the business look less professional.

  • Too much text. One line, maybe two. Phone screens show the header small.
  • Text in the top or bottom 60 pixels. It may get cut off.
  • Low-resolution images. Start from at least 1500x500 pixels.
  • Clashing with the profile photo. Use colors that work together.
  • Old campaigns. A header for a sale that ended months ago tells visitors the account is neglected.

How do you make a business header quickly?

Make a business header quickly by starting from a 1500x500 pixel canvas in any free design tool, adding guides at 60 and 440 pixels from the top, and placing your content between them. Export it as a PNG if it has text, or a JPG for photos.

Then upload it from your profile's Edit profile screen and preview it on a phone. This part is actually really easy once you've set up the canvas once. Save the file as a template, and the next update takes five minutes.

Where does the header fit in your business profile?

The header supports the rest of the profile. It should match your bio, pinned post and profile photo, so all four tell the same story.

Your header is free billboard space, so use it

A great business header doesn't need a big budget. It needs the right size, a clear message in the safe middle band, and an update whenever something new happens. Pick one idea from the list, build it on a 1500x500 canvas this week, and your profile will make a stronger first impression on every visitor.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best header size for a business account on X?

The best header size for a business account on X is 1500x500 pixels, the size X recommends for all profiles at the time of writing (October 2026). That is a 3:1 shape. X notes that about 60 pixels at the top and bottom may be cropped on some screens, so keep key content centered.

Why does my Twitter header look cropped?

A Twitter header can look cropped because X displays it differently on different monitor sizes and browsers. X's help center says about 60 pixels at the top and bottom of a 1500x500 header may be cut off. Moving text and logos into the middle band usually fixes the problem.

Should a business header include a logo?

A business header can include a logo, but it often works better in the profile photo, which X shows next to every post. Use the header for a tagline, product or current campaign instead. If you add the logo in the header too, keep it small and inside the middle band.

Can I use an animated GIF as my X header?

No, X does not support animated GIFs for header or profile images, according to its help center. You can upload a GIF, JPG or PNG file, but a GIF will show as a still image. Use a static design and save motion for posts, where video and GIFs are supported.

How often should a business change its Twitter header?

A business should change its Twitter (X) header whenever there is something new to promote, such as a launch, sale or event, and remove it when that campaign ends. A monthly or quarterly review works well for most businesses. A permanent brand header is fine between campaigns, as long as it stays accurate.
