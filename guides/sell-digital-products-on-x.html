How to Sell Digital Products on X (Step by Step)

By Prashant Sharma · Oct 26, 2026 · 6 min read

How do you sell digital products on X?

To sell digital products on X, build trust with posts that teach part of what the product covers, then send readers to a checkout page through your bio link, pinned post and launch posts. Templates, guides, presets and short courses work well. No follower minimum applies, but X's rules ban spam and artificial amplification.

Think about the last time you bought something after reading a post. It probably wasn't an ad. It was someone who'd already helped you for free, then mentioned they'd made something deeper. That's the whole model in one moment.

A digital product is anything people download or access online: a template, an ebook, a checklist, a preset, a short video course. You make it once and sell it many times. X is a good place to sell one because your posts can show your expertise every day, right where buyers already scroll. It's one of several options in my overview of how to make money on X in 2026.

What digital products sell well on X?

Products that solve one narrow problem sell best on X. Readers there skim fast, so a clear promise beats a big bundle.

  • Templates: spreadsheets, Notion boards, design files, contract drafts.
  • Short guides: a focused PDF that solves one problem start to finish.
  • Checklists and swipe files: quick wins people can use today.
  • Presets and assets: photo presets, icons, code snippets.
  • Mini courses: a few short videos on one skill.

The best product is one your posts already prove you know. If your most-liked posts are about cold email, a cold email template pack is a natural fit. To find those posts, look at which topics beat your usual numbers. My guide on finding your best performing tweets shows how.

Step 1: Validate before you build

Validate demand by asking your audience before you spend weeks building. A short post describing the product and asking who wants it tells you a lot.

Watch the replies and DMs, not just the likes. People who say "I'd buy this" or ask "when?" are real signals. You can also collect emails on a simple waitlist page. Here's the thing: a product with ten eager people waiting launches far better than a polished one nobody asked for.

Step 2: Set up a simple checkout

Most creators sell through a checkout page hosted on a store or payment platform, then link to it from their profile and posts. This keeps payment and file delivery in one place.

Pick something that handles payment, delivery of the file and receipts for you. Keep the page short. Say who it's for, what's inside, what problem it solves and the price. Add a preview image or a few sample pages if you can.

Step 3: Make your profile point to the product

Your profile should make the product easy to find in one tap. Most buyers will check your profile before they buy.

  • Bio: mention what you help with and, briefly, what you sell.
  • Link: point it at your product page or a simple page listing your products.
  • Pinned post: a short post or thread that shows the product's value and links to it.

Your pinned post works around the clock. My guide on creating a good pinned post has formats that work well for offers.

Step 4: Write posts that sell without selling

The posts that sell digital products best are posts that teach. Give away real value, then mention the product as the next step for people who want more.

I'd aim for a mix where most posts are purely helpful and only a few mention the product directly. A teaching thread is a great format. Share three or four useful steps, then end with one line about the full product. If you want help splitting a long draft into clean posts, try the free thread maker, and check single posts with the post length checker. My guide on writing viral Twitter threads covers hooks and structure.

Show proof as you go. Share real buyer feedback (with permission), behind-the-scenes progress and examples of what the product helped you do. Never invent reviews or sales numbers. Readers can tell, and it breaks trust fast.

Step 5: Run a calm launch week

A launch week gives your product a clear moment of attention. Plan a few posts across several days instead of one big announcement.

  1. Day 1: announce it, explain who it's for, link it, and email your waitlist.
  2. Days 2 to 4: share a teaching post or thread from inside the product.
  3. Day 5: answer common questions in one post.
  4. Day 6 or 7: share early feedback and a final reminder.

For a deeper launch plan, my founders' guide on how to launch a product on X covers timing and follow-up.

Should you use X Ads to sell digital products?

X Ads can help once you know a post converts, but they're optional. Start organic, then pay to push what already works.

According to X's advertising page, you can pick goals like sales or website traffic, target by keywords, interests, followers and location, and promote an existing post. I'd only promote a post that has already sold the product organically. That way you're paying to scale proof, not to test a guess.

What should you avoid when selling on X?

Avoid tactics that fake demand or annoy people. They might work once, but they damage your account and your reputation.

  • Engagement bait: X's rules ban using the platform to artificially amplify information. "Like and repost to get the link" style posts are risky.
  • Mass DMs: sending the same pitch to strangers reads as spam.
  • Fake scarcity: don't claim "only 3 left" for a file with no limit.
  • Income claims: don't promise buyers they'll earn money unless you can prove it.

The X Rules are short and worth reading once, and X's Authenticity policy spells out what counts as spam, like bulk unsolicited DMs or link-only posting. If you also promote other people's products for a commission, those posts need X's Paid Partnership label.

What this means for you

You don't need a huge following to sell your first digital product. You need a narrow product, a clear page and posts that prove you know the topic. Each sale also teaches you what your audience wants next. If you're still small, my guide to monetizing a small Twitter account shows where a product fits in the bigger plan, and turning X followers into customers covers repeat buyers.

Make one useful thing

Selling digital products on X comes down to trust. Teach in public, package your best answers into something people can buy, and launch with care. Your first product doesn't need to be perfect. It needs to solve one real problem for the people who already read your posts. Once you've done it once, it becomes second nature.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you sell products directly on X?

Most creators who sell digital products on X link to a checkout page on a store or payment platform rather than selling inside the app. You share that link in your bio, pinned post and posts. X's advertising tools can also promote those posts if you want to reach more buyers.

How many followers do you need to sell digital products on X?

No follower minimum applies to selling digital products on X. Unlike X's payout programs, which need 500 or 2,000 verified followers as of October 2026, your own product only needs buyers. A small audience that trusts your posts can produce first sales, especially for narrow, practical products.

What is the best digital product to sell on X?

The best digital product to sell on X is one that solves a narrow problem your posts already prove you understand. Templates, short guides, checklists and presets are common choices because they're quick to make and easy to explain in a single post or thread.

Is it okay to post links on X?

Yes, posting links to your own products is allowed on X. Add context to each link and mix in posts that simply help people. X's Authenticity policy counts repeatedly posting links without commentary, when that's most of your activity, as spam, along with bulk unsolicited replies and DMs.

How do you price a digital product on X?

Price a digital product on X based on the problem it solves and what your audience can afford. A small template usually costs less than a full course. Starting with a modest price and raising it after early feedback lets you learn without scaring away first buyers.
